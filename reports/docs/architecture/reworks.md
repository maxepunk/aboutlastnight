# Reworks

The automatic budget per round, a rework that fails, what each reworker reads, the rework rules, and the trace of the automatic passes.

Moved verbatim from `reports/CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-11, when CLAUDE.md became a map. A part named in bold elsewhere in this text, such as **The map**, may now sit in another file in `docs/architecture/`; CLAUDE.md lists which file holds which part.

## The trace

**The trace** (phase 2, brief 2.7): what the automatic passes of the current round did before the director reached the map or article stop. Two REPLACE channels, `_outlineTrace` and `_articleTrace`, one per stop. `incrementOutlineRevision` / `incrementArticleRevision` append one entry on an AUTOMATIC pass only (a send-back rework writes none): `{pass, round, trigger: 'check'|'evaluation', findings: {structuralIssues, advisoryWarnings, criteriaScores, revisionGuidance}, before, at}`. `before` is the whole map or bundle the rework started from, because the reworker clears `_previousOutline`/`_previousContentBundle` on every exit but a call it runs again; `findings` is copied from `validationResults` (only when it is stamped for that phase), because the next evaluation overwrites it; `trigger` comes from `automatedRevisionSource` (at the map it is `'check'`, the map checks' one rework; brief 4.6). The increment also drops entries from another round, so a channel holds at most the automated budget's passes: one at the map, two at the article. An automatic pass whose rework did not run drops its own entry, since it never returned (task 4.14e). Each channel is cleared exactly where its side's hand-edit report is: in the reject arm of `buildResumePayload` (a send back opens a round), in the approve return of `checkpointOutline` / `checkpointArticle` (approval ends the round; the llm-log keeps every pass's prompts and responses for a later readout), and at the rollback points that clear that side's `_outlineFeedback` or `_articleFeedback` (the outline's at ten points, all but `article`; the article's at all eleven). Parity is pinned for each mechanism in `server-build-resume-payload.test.js`, `checkpoint-hand-edit-clears.test.js` and `rollback-clears-completeness.test.js`. An entry's `round` is the stop's round, `stopRoundOf` (`lib/workflow/state.js`), which the increments and `getCheckpointData` both call (task 4.12c). `getCheckpointData` sends the current round's passes as `trace`, each `{pass, round, trigger, findings, at, diff, changedScopes}`: `diff` is `diffOutline`/`diffBundle` of the pass's `before` against the next pass's `before` or, for the last pass, the stop's own object (`null` when either is missing); `changedScopes` is `scopeKeys(diff)`. No whole version is sent. Storage: `SqliteSaver` writes the whole state on every superstep, so one pass adds its `before` version plus its findings to every saved checkpoint after it, until that stop's approve, a send back or a rollback clears the channel (session 092026's approved bundle is 20 KB as compact JSON, so an article pass costs about that per row). The relation is pinned in `__tests__/integration/trace-automatic-pass.test.js`, which also runs a pass through the real graph.

## Budgets, failures and what reworkers read

**Revision Loops:** the automated budget, PER ROUND of the director's, is one check rework and one fact-check fix at the story meeting, counted apart (R6; `REVISION_CAPS.ARCS` is 1: the check rework on `arcRevisionCount`, the fix on the fact check's mark on the weave; see **Arc Validation Routing**), one check rework at the map (`REVISION_CAPS.OUTLINE` is 1; brief 4.6), and 2 reworks for the article. A send back (at the story meeting, a reweave or a send-back, by its round mark) opens a new round (`humanArcRevisionCount` / `humanOutlineRevisionCount` / `humanArticleRevisionCount`) and starts the budget over. The director's rounds are never capped, and the story meeting goes forward only on the director's approval. See `PIPELINE_DEEP_DIVE.md#evaluation--revision-architecture` for the judges' one truth-only contract and the revision loops.

**A rework that fails** (task 4.14e; the final review's ruling 5) keeps the version it started from as its stop's output, at the map and the desk, as the story meeting's does (**A round that times out**). The increments leave `outline` / `contentBundle` in place while the rework runs, the rework reading its copy in `_previousOutline` / `_previousContentBundle`, and open the rework's record, `_outlineRework` / `_articleRework` (`lib/workflow/state.js` `reworkOpened`: the director's send-back with its note, or none for an automatic pass, and the counts the rework started from). A call that fails on a transient error (`isTransientError`) with calls left changes only the record (`REWORK_STATUS.RETRYING`), and the route after the rework runs the call again (`routeAfterMapRework`, `routeAfterArticleRework`), up to `REWORK_CALLS` (3) calls, spending no automated budget. Any other failure, or the last call's, gives the rework up (`REWORK_STATUS.DID_NOT_RUN`; `ai-nodes.js` `mapReworkFailed`, `articleReworkFailed`):
- **The director's send-back does not run.** The stop reopens with the director's version, its counts as they were before the round and the note's slot empty, and its payload's `roundDidNotRun` (`roundDidNotRunAt`) says so. The rework also withdraws the round's note from `directorGateNotes` (`roundGivenBack`, `withoutRoundNote`). Every later writer reads a rejection note as applied, and no rework applied this one. The note it withdraws is the rejection note at the stop with the round's text, filed in the round the given-back counts reopen. The stop gives the note back instead, in the note box or quoted by its line. The director's next action files it or leaves it out, as at the story meeting: a send-back with it files it as a rejection note again, Approve with it as an approval note, and any other action leaves it out. The meeting withdraws its own at that next action (`withdrawUnrunRoundNote`), with the same outcomes. The map checks' route opens the stop on the record (`routeMapChecks`); the desk's rework states again the verdict the desk opened on (`verdictAfterFailedRework`), so a replay skips the judge.
- **An automatic pass** ends the run in an error, as the meeting's does, with the version it started from kept and its trace entry dropped. At the desk the verdict it was fixing is escalated to the director, which `routeArticleEvaluation` takes to the stop below the cap. Retry opens the stop with no call, and so does going back to the map (R9).

A rework that completes clears its record, and each record clears at the rollback points that clear its stop's note. Getting the director's version back calls no writer (`__tests__/integration/rework-failure-graph.test.js`).

**Reworkers see what their writers saw** (phase 2, brief 2.3). Each reworker (`reviseArcs`, `reviseOutline`, `reviseContentBundle`) is built from its writer's own builders and inputs, never from copies: the writer's sections, then the revision block (`buildRevisionContext`'s context with HUMAN FEEDBACK and `<HAND_EDITS>`, the previous version, `## YOUR TASK`), then `<DIRECTOR_GUIDANCE>` last, so a later change to a writer reaches its reworker. On 092026 the article reworker had no document text and deleted four correct cards; it now carries the settled weave and the map as the director left it (brief 4.7b), one `<RECORD>`, the money figures, the director's notes and corrections, and the writer's rules and `<SCHEMA>` (the arc packages it carried too went in phase 4, brief 4.6; R5).
- **The writers' sections.** `buildWeaveSections(state)` (the arc writer, which writes the weave since phase 4; `buildWeavePrompt` = it + the standing notes), `PromptBuilder.buildOutlineUserSections` / `buildArticleUserSections` (the user prompt without `<DIRECTOR_GUIDANCE>`). The nodes' inputs come from `outlineWriterInputs(state)` and `articleWriterInputs(state)` in `ai-nodes.js`, which recompute the inputs the writers never store (the settled weave, the map as the article reads it, `articleMapOf`, `buildAvailablePhotos`, `buildSessionFacts`); the map's rework offers code's pick for the top photo first, as its writer does (brief 4.6). No writer or reworker reads a `<SHOULD_CONSIDER>` (phase 4, briefs 4.6 and 4.7b): the revision context carries the current evaluation's own advisories. The split did not change the writers' prompts by a byte: `lib/__tests__/writer-prompts-pinned.test.js` pins each writer node's render by hash (taken at `df51bc0`; each deliberate change since is re-pinned there with its reason), and `reworker-writer-parity.test.js` checks each reworker opens with its writer's sections, marker by marker.
- **System prompts.** Each is its writer's system prompt, then the rework rules, which open with the theme's rework identity line completed by the rework's task (brief 4.13): `getArcRevisionSystemPrompt(round, sessionConfig, theme)` = `weaveSystemPrompt` + `arcRevisionRules(round, theme)` (the `arc-rework` line, then `ARC_REWORK_CLAUSES[round]`, `'send-back'`, `'reweave'` or `'automatic'` for `null`, and on the director's round the line on a note that corrects a game mechanic); `getOutlineRevisionSystemPrompt(writerSystemPrompt, theme)` + `outlineRevisionRules(theme)` (the `outline-rework` line, then `OUTLINE_REWORK_CLAUSE`); `getArticleRevisionSystemPrompt(writerSystemPrompt, theme)` + `articleRevisionRules(theme)` (the `article-rework` line, then `ARTICLE_REWORK_CLAUSE`). The outline and article stages' detective branches went (R1; briefs 4.6 and 4.7b). The two composers take the writer's system prompt first, and throw on a theme name in its place; `buildOutlineRevisionSystemPrompt(promptBuilder)` and `buildArticleRevisionSystemPrompt(promptBuilder)` build it for the builder's theme. The mode block therefore appears once, where the writer's does.
- **The rework rules (phase 3, briefs 3.3 and 3.10; TH7, R23).** No journalist rework carries fixed "preserve" or "do not regenerate" text. A rework's first line is the theme's rework identity line, completed by code with the task its revision context gives it (`ARC_REWORK_CLAUSES`, `OUTLINE_REWORK_CLAUSE`, `ARTICLE_REWORK_CLAUSE`; brief 4.13), and leaves the scope to that context. `buildRevisionContext({…})` states the scope once, under `WHAT THIS REWORK DOES`. On a send back the director's note is the task and sets how much of the previous version the rework keeps ("rethink" gets a rethink). The map's headline is code's to hold (phase 17, fix wave 3, the director's call after the final review; `lib/map.js` `holdHeadline`, see **The map**): every automatic pass on the map keeps the headline of the map it started from, and the map's send-back with the director's note is the one rework that may change it, so its scope, and no other rework's, adds that the map's headline stays word for word unless the note asks for a new one, which it then writes in the voice C12 sets out. On an automatic pass (R23, the director's ruling) the rework fixes the must-fix items, the ISSUES TO ADDRESS and the fixes in CRITERIA SCORES; it takes up a SHOULD CONSIDER item or a suggestion in CRITERIA SCORES only where it touches a line it is already changing for a must-fix item; everything else stays word for word. The sentence carries its reason (the 4b fix batch): the lines it keeps passed the check or evaluation that ran before the pass, and in past reworks the new errors that reached the director were in lines rewritten with no finding behind them. On the weave (the check rework and the fact-check fix) the scope adds one sentence: every angle, thread, connection and question keeps its id, because the director's edits and pick find them by it (3 final). On the map's check rework it adds that every beat keeps its id, because the director's edits find each beat by it: a renumbered beat reads as removed, so code does not put the director's order back on it, and the page reports the move as removed (fix C5; fix C2). (Why R23 exists: at the first phase 3 gate the automatic reworks kept 45% to 72% of the outline and article sentences word for word, and one article rework changed 20 of 27 paragraphs to fix one pronoun.) A criterion's notes and fix print only when it scored below `STRUCTURAL_PASS_SCORE` (the 4b fix batch; 0.8, defined in `node-helpers.js`, which `evaluator-nodes.js` imports): a passing criterion prints its score alone on either kind of rework, whatever a stored verdict wrote, and an advisory criterion's fix prints as `suggestion:`. An automatic pass leaves out the judge's `revisionGuidance` (EVALUATOR FEEDBACK), whose must-fix steps repeat ISSUES TO ADDRESS and whose optional steps read as instructions; a code check's own lines, such as the weave checks', still reach it, under the check's own label in place of ISSUES TO ADDRESS, `WEAVE CHECK FAILURES:` and the map checks' `MAP CHECK FAILURES:` (`CODE_CHECKS` in `node-helpers.js`; the 4b fix batch, phase 4 briefs 4.4 and 4.6). SHOULD CONSIDER says only where its items came from (`REWORK_SHOULD_CONSIDER_LINE`). The reworkers' own rules do not restate the scope (the arc send back's add only that a note correcting a game mechanic corrects every thread it touches). The context no longer lists criteria to "PRESERVE" or as needing improvement; the line above CRITERIA SCORES says only who computed the scores: a model evaluation's are the judge's own and uncalibrated, and a record with no scores (the weave checks, the map checks, the fact check's short-circuit) gets no line. The previous version's header reads `PREVIOUS <PHASE> OUTPUT (the version this rework starts from):`; the send-back `NOTE:` line and `END PREVIOUS OUTPUT` are unchanged. Each reworker's `## YOUR TASK` (`ARC_REWORK_TASK`, `reworkTask`) defers to the revision context. The parked detective's context went with the old stages its reworks wrote (R1; brief 4.7c): its PRESERVE lists, its four numbered instructions, its SHOULD CONSIDER preamble (`SHOULD_CONSIDER_PREAMBLE`) and its previous-output header, with the `theme` option that chose them, so one context serves every theme.
- **What went.** The reworkers' own partial copies (the arc reworker's SESSION CONTEXT accusation, roster, notes, categories and `describeValidEvidence` list; the outline reworker's selected-arc ids and evidence counts; the article reworker's OUTPUT SCHEMA), and the `revision` craft phase. The arc rework's Sources list is the writer's (the record view's id rule). Phase 4 (brief 4.4): the arc rework returns the weave in its writer's schema (`WEAVE_SCHEMA`), so its interweaving plan, its `### PREVIOUS INTERWEAVING PLAN`, its `## WHAT THIS REWORK RETURNS` addendum and `INTERWEAVING_PRINCIPLES` went with the interweaving call; `hasInterweavingPlan` went with the outline judge (brief 4.6).
- **The banner.** A send back's rework reads `REVISION CONTEXT: MAP (round 2: the director's send back)`: the reworker passes `round` = `stopRoundOf(<stop>, state)` (`lib/workflow/state.js`; one more than `human<Stop>RevisionCount`), the round the stop will show. It is the one rule the stops log, the director's notes and the trace read too (task 4.12c). An automated pass still reads `(automated pass N)`.
- **Fail loud.** `buildOutlineRevisionPrompt` / `buildArticleRevisionPrompt` first call `promptBuilder.requirePhasePrompts('outlineGeneration'|'articleGeneration')`. That is the rule-set loader's own check (`loadRuleSet('outline'|'article', {theme})` throws naming every missing or empty rule file; phase 3, 3.2), for every builder: the parked detective's branch, which checked its craft files through the theme loader, went (R1; brief 4.7c), and since brief 4.13 the check reads the builder's theme's rules folder, so it refuses the detective, which names none, naming it. The nodes build inside their `try`, so that becomes the node's error contract.
- `scripts/render-prompts.js` renders every call's prompt from a stored thread with no model calls (phase 3, brief 3.0): the writer and reworker files and the two judges (`judge-arc.txt`, `judge-article.txt`), for either theme with `--theme`. `judge-article.txt` is rendered after the weave and the map are planted, so it reads the settled weave and the map as the director left it (brief 4.7a). Since phase 4 (brief 4.6) `outline-generation.txt` is the map writer, journalist only, `outline-revision.txt` its rework on a send-back and `outline-check-rework.txt` its rework after the map checks; a thread that holds no map gets the fixed map of `scripts/lib/fixed-map.js` planted (invented text, with a struck beat and a moved photo), and the outline judge's `judge-outline.txt` went. Since phase 4 `arc-generation.txt` is the weave writer, `arc-revision.txt` its automatic pass after the weave checks and `judge-arc.txt` the story meeting's fact check; a thread that holds no weave gets the fixed weave of `scripts/lib/fixed-weave.js` planted, as it gets the fixed notes. Since phase 4b (brief 1G) a thread whose weave or map is in an earlier shape gets the fixed one planted over it, by the guard's own tests (`lib/old-thread.js` `isOldShapeWeave`, a weave with no angles, phase 4's or piece 1's since brief 3E, and `isOldShapeMap`), and the run logs which it planted and why (`plantReason`: none held, or the old shape). The judges render through `scripts/lib/render-calls.js`, which repeats their nodes' argument lists; `__tests__/unit/scripts/render-calls.test.js` fails when a node sends anything else, so a change to what `createEvaluator` passes its builders goes into that module in the same commit. A render that is empty, holds `[object Promise]` or lacks its marker fails the run; each judge's markers name the blocks its call reads (task 4.11): `WEAVE:` for the meeting's fact check, and `<SETTLED_WEAVE>`, `MAP:` and `CONTENT BUNDLE:` for the article judge. `--sections <dirA> <dirB>` reports which top-level sections of two renders differ. `--compare` is retired (task 4.11): it held a render to main's byte for byte, and every phase 4 call differs from main's by design, so it exits 2, naming `--sections`. Its header comment lists the files and the options, and `REQUIRED_MARKERS` under it the markers, which `__tests__/unit/scripts/render-prompts.test.js` holds to the renders.
