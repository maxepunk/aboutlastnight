# The director's edits are final

How the director's edits at a stop are recorded, carried, read by every rework, judge and check, and put back after an automatic pass, and what the round's report says.

Moved verbatim from `reports/CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-11, when CLAUDE.md became a map. A part named in bold elsewhere in this text, such as **The map**, may now sit in another file in `docs/architecture/`; CLAUDE.md lists which file holds which part.

**The director's edits are final** (before phase 4, F1 and FA; spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-story-meeting-and-map.md` section 7). An automatic pass never changes a line the director wrote: a judge that disagrees with one lists it for the director, and code puts back what a pass changed anyway. A send-back's changes are the director's edits, one per field the director changed (`lib/hand-edit-diff.js`), `{id, scope, path, at, before, after}`: `at` is the steps to the field, each element of a collection found by what names it (a section's id, or on the map its slot, a card's tokenId, a beat's, a thread's or a connection's id, a block's type with its text, filename or tokenId), and `path` the same place for reading (`sections[#closing].content[0].text`, `sections[#closing].beats[#b6].move` on the map, `financialTracker.entries[1].amount`). A card's headline, a beat's move, one tracker entry's amount and a photo's caption apart from its filename are each one edit, so the writer's untouched text beside them stays the writer's (FA, requirement 2); an element the director added whole is one edit whose `after` is the element. On the map, the director's order of a section is one edit of the section, its beat ids in their order, which owns no text and names at least two beats; it stands while the section holds every beat it names, each once, in that order, and none of them prints anywhere else on the map (fix A: the spec's section 17; fix C3), and after an automatic pass that changes it or takes one of those beats out of the section, code puts each beat it names that the map still holds back in the section at its place in that order, while a beat the pass removed from the map stays out, as a beat the director only moved does, and a writer's beat the pass struck into left out stays struck, and the order stands on the beats that remain (fixes C2 and H2), and a beat's summary (its `synopsis`) is a line like any other, named in every place as the summary of its move (`section "closing", the summary of beat "b6"`), never by the field's name (phase 4b, piece 4, R3 and R11; see **The map**). A section's blocks pair only with blocks of their own type, so a block retyped (only the JSON editor retypes one) is a cut and an addition (task 4.3c), and a photo, a card or a reference only with one of its own filename or tokenId (`pairSectionBlocks`), so one renamed is a cut and an addition too, and two the director swapped are two moves, each keeping its own caption or text (task 4.5d). A null `after` is a cut, whose `before` is the text removed and whose `pieces` are each of its sentences of three words or more that the director's version no longer held. A rewritten text field also records `removed`: each sentence of its old text the director's version no longer holds, as written (requirement 3). A block that left one section and arrived unchanged in another is one edit, a move, whose `from` names the section it left (requirement 4); a block the director moved within its section is a move too, whose `between` names the blocks it follows and precedes (task 4.3, the desk). A Key Evidence entry (the sidebar's `evidenceCards`) the director deleted at the desk is a cut, and one they moved within the sidebar is a move whose `from` is `evidenceCards` and whose `between` names the entries it follows and precedes by their tokenIds, the desk's naming rule deciding which entries moved, beside an edit of any field they changed on it (`diffSidebar`, task 4.14c). A move is the block's place only: the director changed none of its fields, so its text is still the writer's, which the judges, the guard and the fact check read as the writer's (fix round 1, finding 2). Given the session roster's names (both send-back arms of `buildResumePayload`), a cut or a rewrite records `names`: the names its text held that the director's version no longer names (requirement 9). An edit's id (`E1`, `E2`, ...) is stable within its stop. The edits stand at their stop across every send-back (`standingAfterSendBack`): `_outlineHandEdits` / `_articleHandEdits` hold `{kind, issued, edits}`; an earlier edit stands while the version the stop showed and the version sent back carry it (a field edit, while the element it belongs to holds the director's value, a section's block found in any section; a move, while a block of its identity (its type with its filename, tokenId or text) sits in the director's section, whatever its other fields, and a move within the section while it also keeps the director's order there, or, where the director's own move of a neighbour broke that order at a send-back, while it sits among the blocks that kept their order in it, re-anchored to its neighbours there (task 4.3b), and a Key Evidence entry's move the same way within the sidebar (task 4.14c); a cut, while none of its pieces is back, and a Key Evidence entry the director deleted, while the sidebar holds no entry of its tokenId, in any words (`deletedEntryOf`, task 4.14f)), a removed sentence that came back drops from its edit, and so does a deleted Key Evidence entry's piece that either version prints outside every entry of its tokenId, which is the director's to keep or cut (`stillRemoved`, task 4.14f, fix round 1), and the send-back's own edits join with the next ids after `issued`. Text is read from the fields the page prints (`PRINTED_FIELDS`, which the article judge's `printedBundle` reads too; known item 6), and a piece under six words is back only as a whole sentence (known item 4). A diff stored before F1, or an edit stored before FA, reads as the same field-level edits. At the article stop, approve, a rollback and a fresh start clear them (the map's and the meeting's stand past approve and through the rollback to their own stop, R9), and approve-with-edits runs no rework. Every reader takes the edits the version in hand carries (`carriedEdits`):
- The reworks' `<HAND_EDITS>` block (`buildRevisionContext`) lists them by id and place with the director's text (`formatEditLines`: text quoted, an element by its fields, never JSON; a move by its place, and a block its text names by the words it begins with), each removed sentence on a `removed:` line under its edit, after `EDIT_LINES_GUIDE` (an edit covers only the place its line names, and a moved block's text is still the writer's), in one of two wordings. On an automatic pass each edit stays exactly as written, each removed sentence stays out, and each block the director cut stays out, with what it said not said again anywhere in the article, since the cut check finds only a cut's own sentences coming back (brief 4.7f; the send-back's exception is stated first, as the map's and the meeting's are). On a send-back the same holds unless the structural change the note asks for means an edit no longer fits, and the rework returns each edit it changed, removed or brought back in `changedDirectorEdits`, with one sentence on why. That list exists only in the rework call: its schema is built in code from the stored one on a send-back that carries edits (`reworkSchemaWithChangedEdits`, `ai-nodes.js`; `lib/schemas/*.json` are unchanged), and the node takes the list out before it stores the output (`takeChangedEdits`). A rework never reads a finding located in the director's text (`withoutDirectorsFindings`, requirement 7): the issues, suggestions and criterion notes and fixes that quote a standing edit are left out of its context, and so are the judge's guidance and the notes and fix of a criterion under the rule ids of an issue left out (the verdict's `criteriaRules`, which `createEvaluator` writes; requirement 5). At a send-back that verdict is the one the stop showed, which no judge wrote with the director's newest edits in view.
- The article judge reads them right after the output it judges (the outline judge left in phase 4, brief 4.6, and the detective's article judge in brief 4.7a, R1) (`renderJudgeDirectorEdits`, from `judgedEdits`, which `createEvaluator` and `scripts/lib/render-calls.js` both pass as `directorEdits`), with the same `EDIT_LINES_GUIDE`: the director's text is record, the judge scores the writer's text, and a disagreement with an edit, or a cut or removed sentence it would bring back, goes in `advisoryWarnings`, opening with `DIRECTOR_EDIT_PREFIX` (`"Director's edit "`) and the edit's id, then the rule.
- The verdict guard (`guardDirectorEdits`, in `createEvaluator` before readiness is decided). A structural issue that quotes the director's text (an edit's, a cut's or a removed sentence's) moves to `advisoryWarnings` as `Director's edit <id>: <the issue>`, whatever else it quotes, since a quote of the writer's text in such an issue is usually where the problem sits or where a fix would go (requirement 1); so does an issue the judge filed under the prefix and a standing edit that owns text, and a judge's advisory that quotes the director's text is a concern in its own place (requirement 6). A finding the judge filed under the prefix and edits that own no text alone (`hand-edit-diff.js` `ownsNoText`: a block moved at the desk, a thread flipped into or out of an angle at the meeting, the director's order of a section on the map) is read by its quotes, since a place holds only the writer's text (task 4.5e): a quote of the director's text makes it a concern under the edits it quotes, and a quote of the writer's words the writer's must-fix, whichever list the judge filed it in; one whose quotes locate nothing stays a concern beside the edits, as the judge filed it, since that is how a judge disagrees with the director's choice of a place, which no rework may change (task 4.5f). The quote match is `locateQuotedText`: `grounding.js` `quotedPassages`, split at an elision, passages of three words or more, `isVerbatimIn` in any case; a passage equal to a section heading is a location and locates nothing (known item 3); a passage the writer's printed text holds is the writer's, a moved block's text included, unless it sits only in the content of a writer's evidence card and the record holds it too (`recordTexts`), which is the record cited (known item 5). A criterion whose notes or fix quote the director's text keeps them from the rework, and one that failed joins the concerns as `Director's edit <id>: <rule ids or name>: <notes> <fix>` unless a concern already covers it. A criterion a concern covers (one under its rule ids or its name, or, for a criterion with no rule ids, one about the same edit) keeps its notes and fix from the rework and holds the output only while a structural issue under its rule ids remains (requirement 5); a failed truth criterion that no finding names and no concern covers is a must-fix line (`truthIssueLines`). The judge's guidance is left out when an issue moved or it quotes the director's text. The output is ready when no structural issue remains and every structural criterion that failed is one a concern covers; the judge's own `structuralPassed` does not hold an output with nothing left to fix. With no edits nothing changes. The concerns ride in the history entry, for the stop, and never in `validationResults`, so no rework reads them.
- The fact check (`directorEdits`, from `buildFactCheckArgs`), field by field (`editLocator`): a card's content that is not verbatim is an advisory under the edit's id when the director wrote the content, and stays structural on a card whose headline alone the director changed. An unknown source on a card the director edited, inline or in the sidebar (requirement 10), a reporter-mode phrase in a paragraph's text the director wrote, a photo whose filename the director set, and a roster player whose last mention the director's version dropped (the names the edit recorded; an edit stored without names, by whether its cut text names the player) are advisories too, each with its existing message after the prefix. The photo check and roster coverage's hero caption read the photos the page prints (`lib/publish-photos.js` `printedPhotos`), so a detective hero, which never prints, is not checked (requirement 12). The same hit in the writer's text keeps its status, a block the director only moved included; `cardFidelity` marks a director's card with `directorEdit`, and `missing`, `invalid` and `violations` list the structural hits only. A section's key is `hand-edit-diff.js` `sectionKey`, one rule for both modules (known item 7).
- Code puts back what a pass changed (`settleEdits`, in `reviseOutline` and `reviseContentBundle`; requirement 8): after an automatic pass, each standing edit the pass changed is written back from its steps and the director's value (an element the pass removed goes back where it sat, and a block put back that way, alone or with its section, loses each copy the pass left in another section, so it prints once (task 4.3c); an element a name finds (a photo by its filename, a card by its tokenId, a thread or a connection by its id) is followed by its name alone, so a field goes back on its own element, never on another the pass put in its place, and a caption the director wrote prints under its own photo after a pass that swapped two (`partnerIndex`, task 4.5d; a card's or a weave element's name is what `identityOf` finds it by, the rule the pairing reads, task 4.5e); what goes back holds no photo block the article cannot print that the pass took out of print, by removing or renaming it, so that the version the pass returned prints it nowhere (`leftOutOfRestore`, task 4.5g; `settleEdits` given `photos` and `whiteboard`: the article's rework, `reviseContentBundle`, gives the photos the session kept, `keptPhotoFilenames`, or no list when the session holds no photos, `sessionPhotoNames`, where the fact check checks none but the whiteboard, task 4.5f; each is matched by its basename, `photoBasename`, the rule the fact check imports, an empty list refuses every photo, and the whiteboard, `whiteboardFilenameOf`, is refused with or without a list, as the fact check reads it, task 4.5g): an edit on such a block, a caption the director wrote under its photo, stays out with it, a section put back whole comes back without it, and so does a section the director added whole, merged onto the one a pass kept (task 4.5f), and the report marks the edit `unprintable` when the version stored prints that photo nowhere, so an automatic pass that fixed an invalid photo the director captioned keeps its fix (tasks 4.5e and 4.5f); a section the director added whole that went back without such a photo, by either path, stands as code put it back (`settleEdits`' `narrowed`, which the article's rework stores in `_articleHandEdits` through `standingAfterPass`), so every later reader, the next send-back included, holds the rest of it as the director's (task 4.5g, fix round 1); a photo the pass kept goes back as the director left it, printable or not: a section or a photo block the director put in whole comes back with it, and a caption the director wrote on a photo a pass moved and recaptioned goes back on it in the director's section, while the pass's copy goes (task 4.5g); a block the director moved that a pass took to another section goes back into the director's section once, as the pass left it, and a block moved within its section only into the director's order there: where the pass swapped the blocks it sat between, code leaves the block where the pass, or a restore of one of its fields, put it, printed once, and the report says where that is: not put back, or, from a field's restore, back in the director's section out of their order; tasks 4.3b and 4.3c; a Key Evidence entry the director moved goes back into their order in the sidebar the same way, and one a pass removed stays out, task 4.14c; a block named by its words (a paragraph, a quote, a list) that the pass reworded while it removed or inserted the blocks around it goes on the pass's version of it, so the page prints it once (`passVersionOf`, which the report's `became` reads too): the pass's copy, the one block anywhere in the article holding four in five of the director's words and held as much by them (`passCopyOf`, `COPY_SHARE`), where nothing pairs the block or only its place pairs it with a block holding fewer of its words, such as a paragraph the pass inserted right before its copy, which stays the writer's (fix round 1); with no copy, the block in its place, unless another block of its section beats it (`closerRivals`, fix rounds 2 and 3): one that may be the pass's version (`mayBeItsVersion`: four in five of the director's words, or half while the director's holds half of its words) and is closer to the director's text both ways (more of their words, and more of its own words theirs), or holds that text whole beside a block in its place that may not be the pass's version; a block that is another block's version is weighed by the words it holds beyond that block's (`nearAsItsVersion`), so a paragraph the pass merged the director's into stays a possible version, a word changed or not, while a neighbour's rewording that holds their words through its own paragraph's holds none (`originalOf`: the pairing holds it as that block's partner, it holds half that block's words, and no other block of its section that the pass wrote is closer to that block both ways; never a block holding the director's text whole, or four in five of whose words are theirs); where code cannot tell, or nothing pairs the block and no copy exists, it goes back where it sat, and the report names the blocks the pass wrote that may be its version, the closest first (`maybeCopies`): the closest block that beat the one in its place, and that one too when it may be the pass's version, else one holding half the director's words or more, else one of its type in the place it sat, such as a full rewrite; task 4.14c), so the stored output carries every edit except two, each listed in the report: a moved block a pass removed, which stays out because only its place was the director's, and its removal can be the fix of a fault in the writer's text (fix round 1, findings 1 and 2); and the director's text on a photo the article cannot print that a pass took out of print, which stays out with its photo (tasks 4.5e to 4.5g). One rule, `passCopies`, says which copies outside the director's section are the pass's: in each other section, those beyond as many as the version the pass started from held there. It decides the copies a restore takes out, the copy a move's restore takes back and the section the report names, so a copy the director kept, such as a card cited in two sections, stays (task 4.3c, fix round 1). A cut or removed sentence that came back stays, flagged in the report: code takes text out only as the copies a pass left in another section of a block code put back (4.3c), whose text is not flagged, and as a Key Evidence entry the director deleted that an automatic pass put back. Such an entry has an id, its tokenId, so its delete follows the plan's rule for an element with one, as a struck beat on the map does (task 4.14f): an automatic pass that put an entry of its tokenId back, in any words, has every such entry taken out again, before the other restores, and the report records the restore (`takeOutDeletedEntry`); a send-back's rework that put it back keeps it, listed with the rework's reason; the entry's words printed outside every entry of its tokenId are not the entry, so the delete stands and code takes nothing out: they are flagged as cut text that came back, since code cannot match them to the entry (`entryWordsBackIn`, task 4.14f, fix round 1); and an inline card keeps the flag-only rule (a known item). At the article stop each "came back" entry carries the `pieces` it reports and is read again against the version stored after every later pass of the round, the last of which is the version stored at the stop: it stays while that version holds the text, with `became` where it is now, and goes once a later pass took the text out again (`cameBackStillIn`, also on a pass that carries no edit; task 4.14c); a Key Evidence entry a send-back's rework put back is read by its `tokenId` instead, and stays while the sidebar holds an entry of it, and the flag for a deleted entry's words is read outside every entry of the tokenId its `outsideEntry` names (task 4.14f). A removed sentence, or a deleted entry's piece printed outside it, that stays back through a later pass keeps the one entry of the pass that brought it back, so each is one line at the stop (`listedSentences`, task 4.14f). A send-back's rework is not restored: the note may change an edit, and the rework says why.
- The report (`reportAfterPass`, through `settleEdits`): see `handEditReport` under "Checkpoint payload keys the gates read".
- The article approve (`buildResumePayload`) checks every photo the page will print (`printedPhotos`) against `data/<id>/photos` and refuses the approval, naming each missing file, while the stop is still open; publish's throw stays a backstop (requirement 12).

The console shows what each pass did to the director's edits through `steeringView` into `RevisionDiff`, one line each, read from each entry's own `automatic` and `restored` flags (known item 7; the console's `editReportOf` reads a report as `handEditReportOf` does, and a test holds the two equal), folded below the article at the desk; beside an edit every stop shows only `changedEditsToShow`'s entries (task 4.10: a send-back's, with its reason, and any other pass's that code did not put back, or put back out of the director's order, brief 4.10b, or put back where a block may be the pass's version of it, task 4.14c), and the map, the story meeting and the desk's folded record say the director's edits stand when they show none (`editsStandLine`; the folded record since brief 4.10c). Each concern sits beside the edit it is about (at the desk through `deskMarks`, see **The desk's marks**) and in the fact check's `directorEdits` group; none counts as unresolved on the approve button. The story meeting's edits are the weave's (see **The story meeting**), and the map's are the map's (see **The map**).
