# The story meeting

The arc stop: the weave its writer writes (angles over one set of threads, each with its evidence), the weave checks and the fact check, the director's payloads and rounds, the questions, the settled weave later writers read, and the page.

Moved verbatim from `reports/CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-11, when CLAUDE.md became a map. A part named in bold elsewhere in this text, such as **The map**, may now sit in another file in `docs/architecture/`; CLAUDE.md lists which file holds which part.

## The stop on the server

**The story meeting** (phase 4, brief 4.5; spec 4.3, 4.4 and section 7; R9, R11, R12). The arc stop is the meeting where the director settles the weave before anything is planned. `lib/meeting.js` holds its payloads and what its stop shows; `lib/weave.js`, `lib/hand-edit-diff.js` and `lib/writer-questions.js` hold the rules its readers share.
- **The director-side schema** (`DIRECTOR_WEAVE_SCHEMA`): the writer's `WEAVE_SCHEMA`, derived in code, with the director's pick (`picked`, the id of the angle they sent on; phase 4b, piece 3, R1), and an `answer` on a question, and no thread or connection held to have its evidence (phase 4b, brief 1B): the evidence is never the director's edit (R6; `lib/hand-edit-diff.js` leaves it out of every diff, `withoutEvidence`), so a thread the director adds is its id, its name and its line, and the map writer finds its evidence. The payload gate validates against it (Ajv, strict) and refuses a malformed weave with the reason. The schema leaves extra keys open, so past it the gate refuses by name each key the meeting no longer has (`RETIRED_WEAVE_KEYS`: the weave's own story, question, headline, convergence and strongerMainThread, a role on an angle or a thread, a thread's reason and a connection's struck), when the director's version brings it where the weave the meeting showed does not hold it as it is, with a line saying the meeting no longer has it (`retiredKeyProblems`; the console's copy `MEETING_RETIRED_KEYS`, held equal by a test; 3 final). Past the schema every angle, thread, connection and question has an id of its own (ruling 3; `directorWeaveProblems`, given the weave the meeting showed), and the refusal names who made a repeat, read from each id's count in the weave the meeting showed and in the director's version (fix round 1; brief 4.5b): an id the director's version holds more often is the director's repeat, refused, naming the director (a third `t2` added over the writer's two included); a repeat the writer made (one the weave the meeting showed holds as often, such as a thread the fact check's fix added under a taken id) is a failure the checks report and a rework fixes, so it passes while the director leaves the elements under it as the meeting showed them, and every action works, a send-back that carries only a note among them; a change under it is refused, naming the writer, since no edit could find the element changed by its id. Each refusal offers only a remedy that works in its case: keep the ids the meeting showed and give each element the director added an id of its own; under the writer's repeat, send the weave back or reweave it with a note. An element with no id is refused the same way (`idlessRefusal` in `meetingResume`, from `lib/weave.js` `idlessChanges`; the final review): one the director adds, naming the director, and a change to one the writer left with no id, naming the writer; the writer's, left as the meeting showed them, pass, and the checks fail them. A repeat is read by one rule, `lib/weave.js` `repeatedIds` over ids read by `weaveIdOf` (trimmed), which the gate, the checks and the diff all call (fix round 1); the diff (`weaveEditsBetween`) pairs the elements under a repeated id in order and flags each change under one (`repeatedId`, kept on the meeting's marks), and `standingAtMeeting` refuses such a change, which no edit could find by its id. Past the repeats the gate refuses a pick that names no angle the weave holds; a pick of an angle id the writer repeated, since the gate stores the angle sent by its id and cannot tell which of them the director picked (3B fix 7: the angle the meeting opened, left open, passes, as a writer's repeat left as shown does, and the refusal offers the rework, a send-back or a reweave with a note, which gives each angle an id of its own); and a version that does not carry the thread that carries the room's verdict, naming that thread (R8; `pickProblems`): the thread taken out of the weave, its verdict flag taken off or moved to another thread, or the thread left out of the picked angle (the one the pick names, else the first). That thread is the one the weave the meeting showed flags, by id, so a version that clears the flag cannot pass (fix round 1); with no weave shown, or one that flags none, the version's own flags decide. R8 refuses only what the director did (3 final): an approve is refused whoever left the thread out of the angle, and a reweave or a send-back only when the director's version took the thread out, took its verdict off, or left it out of an angle the meeting showed telling it, since the rework can fix the writer's fault and a send-back carrying only a note must go through; each refusal names the angle the action sends (`ANGLE_THE_ACTION_SENDS`), and an approve of a weave with no angle is refused, offering Send back, since the settled weave would be empty (`NO_ANGLE_TO_APPROVE`, the console's `MEETING_NO_ANGLE_LINE`; 3 final), and the gate and the console take the action (`directorWeaveProblems(weave, {shown, action})`, `meetingWeaveProblems(weave, shown, action)`). The page locks it in the open angle, so such a version came past the console, as from the harness's approve file. The console's validator (`meetingWeaveProblems`, `console/checkpoint-view-logic.js`, 4.8; the pick's rules in `meetingPickProblem`) applies the same rules, held to the gate's decisions by a corpus test. The schema never reaches the SDK, and code strips a pick or an `answer` a writer or a rework wrote (`weaveFromOutput`, brief 4.5b), so no model writes a pick or an answer. The strike on a connection went from the weave (phase 4b, piece 3, R7): a connection shows while both its threads are in the open angle, so the director takes one out by taking a thread out, or asks for it with a note.
- **The payloads** (`meetingResume`, through `buildResumePayload`, taken only at the meeting's own stop, where only the meeting's keys are taken, `meeting`, `weave` and `note`, which are all the console's `meetingPayload` builds, and the `photosPath` rider (`MEETING_KEYS`): any other key, another stop's approval such as `{outline: true}` among them, is refused, naming the stop the thread is paused at and each key it does not take; `meetingOnlyRefusal`, briefs 4.5b and 4.5c): approve carries the weave as the director left it and an optional note; a reweave the same, and a reweave that carries no edit and no note is refused with its reason, since a pick alone, or answers alone, is nothing to fit in and travels as it is (brief 4.5b; `EMPTY_REWEAVE`, phase 4b, brief 3C); a send-back a note, and the weave when the director edited it. Each writes the director's version as the weave, with its pick (with the fact-check mark of the weave the meeting showed): the angle they picked as they left it, and every other angle as the meeting showed it, by id, since only the angle the director sends goes on and a change to another is not kept (R9; `withUnsentAnglesAsShown`, which builds the stored list from the meeting's, in its order, puts the angle sent in the first place under its id, as the pick opens the first, and writes the pick when the director's order alone made it; the gate refuses a version that drops an angle the meeting showed or adds one it never showed, naming it by its headline, `angleSetProblems`, and the console's validator by `meetingAngleSetProblem`; fix round 1); a change to a thread's name or line is the thread's, which every angle that tells it shares, so the threads are stored as the director left them. The pick is code's, as an answer is: code strips it from a writer's or a rework's output and puts the director's back after every rework while its angle survives by id, and otherwise the first angle is open (R1; `lib/weave.js` `withPickFrom`, in `weaveFromRework`). Each writes too the standing edits against the writer's last weave (`_weaveBaseline`) through `standingAtMeeting`: an earlier edit stands while the director's version carries it, and a difference no standing edit is at joins with the next id. The edits are the weave's (`weaveEditsBetween`, in the order the meeting prints its lines; phase 4b, brief 3C): "from your notes"; each line of an angle's pitch, found by the angle's id (`angles[#a1].story`; under an id the writer repeated, the first angle is the one the pick opens, so only a change to a later one is refused); each thread flipped into or out of an angle, one edit of its own kind (`angles[#a1].threads[#t6]`, `flip: 'in' | 'out'`, its `after` the thread's name; R2), carried while the angle's list holds (or lacks) that thread, so each flip keeps its author at every later look and the writer's order and own threads stay the writer's; each field of a thread or a connection; and a thread or a connection added or cut whole. The pick is no edit (R1). A whole element the director put in (a thread or a connection they added; a thread with its place in the angle they added it to, `angle: {id, flip}`, R2) stays one edit under its id when they later change part of it or take it out of that angle, its `after` the element as they left it, so the rest of it stays theirs through every pass and in the checks; one a send-back's rework changed goes, as any edit a send-back changed does (`wholeElementAsLeft`, given the weave the meeting showed; fix round 1). A thread they added stays theirs while the weave holds it under its id with their name and line, and its place in the angle is a membership of its own, as a flip is, which lapses alone when a send-back's rework moves the thread to another angle or out of every angle: every reader takes the edit as the weave carries it (`editAsCarried`, through `carriedEdits`), so the checks still hold the thread as theirs and an automatic pass that rewords it has its words put back where the send-back left it; the next look keeps the thread's edit without the place, a flip of the thread is an edit of its own, and the send-back's report reads the move as a flip (piece 3, the final review). At each place a standing edit is carried in the weave the meeting showed, the director's version is read against what the meeting showed there (`withShownEdits`; brief 4.5c, fix round 1), so a change there is theirs whether or not a pass ran since the last look (an approve, then back; a round that did not run): a field, or a thread's place in an angle, set back to the writer's is an edit, so a reweave whose only change it is has something to fit in. Approve resumes as `{approved: true}`, and the stop sets `meetingApproved`, the one approval the check node, the fact check's skip and both arc routes read (`isMeetingApproved`), and writes the approved weave to `data/<id>/analysis/weave.approved.json`. A reweave and a send-back are the director's round, marked explicitly (`_meetingRound`, `meetingRoundOf`): the mark, never the note's presence, selects the rework's scope (`buildRevisionContext`'s `meetingRound`), its system prompt (`getArcRevisionSystemPrompt(round, …)`: `arcRevisionRules(round, theme)`, the theme's rework identity line completed by the round's clause, `ARC_REWORK_CLAUSES['send-back' | 'reweave' | 'automatic']`; brief 4.13), the round's counters (`incrementArcRevision`) and the standing notes it leaves out; the note is the round's `_arcFeedback`. The route after the stop (`routeAfterArcCheckpoint`) has three outcomes: approve to the photos, a round to the rework; anything else throws. A note joins `directorGateNotes`: of kind `approval` on an approve, `rejection` on a round.
- **A round** spends no automated budget and opens a new one, and its rework reads no finding from before it. A reweave (`REWEAVE_PASS`) works on the angle the director has open: its WHAT THIS REWORK DOES names that angle by its id and headline, since the weave the rework reads carries no pick, fits into it the director's changes on that angle and on its threads, which it names by id, and says their changes on other angles stand as they are, since `<HAND_EDITS>` lists every standing edit (`openAngleEditIds`; 3 final), gives a thread they added to that angle the pieces of the record that tell it, or no evidence when the record holds none, which the meeting then says (3 final), keeps every line they did not touch, and says every other angle, every thread outside the open angle and every connection that does not join two of its threads stay as written, so the director can still switch, while a thread the open angle shares with another angle may be reworded (`reweaveScope`, `ARC_REWORK_CLAUSES.reweave`; phase 4b, brief 3C; R3). After its rework, before the edits are settled, code puts back by id, from the version the rework started from, each element paired with its own version by its occurrence under its id (`occurrenceKeys`, as the diff pairs them, so an element under an id the writer repeated comes back as itself; fix round 1), every other angle in the director's order (and the open one, the first under the pick's id as the pick opens it, should the rework drop it), every thread outside the open angle (read as the director's standing flips leave it, `threadPlacesOf`, since code puts each back after the hold, so a thread they left out that the rework brought back keeps its words; fix round 1) and every thread another angle names that the rework dropped, where it sat, and every connection whose joins, as that version had them, are not both the open angle's threads, and, when the open angle is not the first, "from your notes", which travels with the first angle (R11; 3 fix A), and takes out an angle, a thread or a connection of the rework's own outside it (`lib/weave.js` `holdOutsideOpenAngle`). Each put-back joins the round's report as `held`, `{scope, id, change: 'rewritten' | 'dropped' | 'added', became}` (`fromYourNotes`'s with `id: null`) (`reportWithHeld`), which every later pass of the round keeps (`reportAfterPass`) and the stop does not show; a report that checked no edit holds it for the readout and reads as none at the stop. The hold is the Reweave's alone: the round's check rework and fact-check fix are automatic passes, which change only what their findings name, in any angle (R3). A send-back rethinks the weave as the note asks (TH7), its scope naming the angle the director had open by its id and headline as context for the note, which may still reach any angle (`sentBackAngleLine`; 3 final), and may change one of the director's edits, saying why in `changedDirectorEdits` (its schema carries the list only when edits stand: `reworkSchemaWithChangedEdits`, `takeChangedEdits`, shared with the outline's and the article's send-backs). The round's note ends on HUMAN FEEDBACK's `NOTE:` line, as at every stop, which the removed-phrase scan reads as the end of the director's words; only the sentence about the evaluator's issues stays out (brief 4.5b). Every pass but a send-back is held to the edits (`settleEdits`): code puts back a line the pass changed and an element the director added that the pass dropped, with its place in its angle, and a thread the director flipped, by adding it to that angle's list, after its own threads, or taking it off, and nothing else, so the pass's own order and threads stay (`restoreMembership`; R2); an angle the pass took out goes back first, where it sat, as a line of its pitch puts it back, so whether the angle survives does not depend on the kind of edit on it, and `reviseArcs` sets the director's pick again on an angle code put back (`withPickFrom`, after the settle; fix round 1). The round's report (`_weaveHandEditReport`) records each change and each restore, a thread or a connection by its fields without its evidence (`weaveReportText`, the weave's counterpart of `mapReportText`, so the meeting's line reads a thread by its name and its line, and an angle by its pitch without the ids of its threads; fix round 4, brief 3C). The rework reads every connection (`weaveForRework` drops only the pick), and `<HAND_EDITS>` lists the director's changes by id and place (`WEAVE_EDIT_LINES_GUIDE`): a line of an angle's pitch (`angle "a1", story`), a thread added with the angle it went into (`thread "t8", added in angle "a1"`), and a thread flipped (`angle "a1", thread "t6", brought in`, with the thread's name), which owns no text, as a block moved at the desk owns none (`ownsNoText`). The rule under the lines names each kind of change (`WEAVE_EDITS_FINAL`, `buildRevisionContext`): a thread brought into an angle stays in it, one left out of an angle stays out, and a thread added stays where the director put it. The weave a pass leaves is the writer's last weave (`_weaveBaseline`); a round also keeps the version the director left (`_weaveMarks`), from which the stop reads what the round's passes changed (`weaveMarks`): each line of an angle's pitch, and each thread flipped, with its `flip`; each question against the question it is, by the carry's rule (`pairWeaveQuestions`), so one a rework only renumbered carries no mark (brief 4.5c). The round's weave has no fact-check mark, so the checks and the fact check run again within the round, and the meeting reopens. The rework's call is built once (`arcReworkCall`), so `scripts/render-prompts.js` renders what `reviseArcs` sends.
- **The answers.** Code keeps every answered question whole but for its id, with the director's answer, through every rework, and adds back an answered question a rework left out; no answer is read from a rework's output. A question may name the thread it sits beside (`thread`, `WEAVE_QUESTION_THREAD_KEY`; phase 4b, piece 3), read beside the answer by `weaveQuestionsOf` and never required; after every rework code drops a `thread` the weave no longer holds, answered or not, keeping the answer, so the question sits by the pitch (R10; `lib/weave.js` `withHeldQuestionThreads`, in `weaveFromRework`). A question whose `thread` came from the version the rework started from (one the director answered, which code keeps whole, or one the rework did not return, which code carried) keeps it only while the weave holds the same thread under that id, by its name or its line, or reworded in both while no other thread took its earlier name or line (the final review), so a rework that renumbers the threads and gives the id to another thread never seats the director's answer beside the wrong thread; a question the rework returned names its thread in the rework's own numbering, and only the still-held test reads it (fix round 1). Every rework keeps each question the director has not answered (`carriedWeaveQuestions`; C15), paired by one function, `pairWeaveQuestions`, which the meeting's marks read too (brief 4.5c). A previous question pairs only with what reads as the rework's version of it: the same words, then the same subject (`subjectKey`: the kind and `about`), each in its place under its id first (`lib/weave.js` `occurrenceKeys`, as the diff pairs elements), then a question of its kind in its place whose `about` names the same subject in other words (`namesOneSubject`: the same numbers, a figure's time and amount, or every word of one in the other, a name given in full). In an answered question's place that last step needs the question's own words too: the rework reads the answer, so a question in other words there is a new one. A question on another player or another ledger entry is a new question wherever the rework puts it (fix round 3), unless the two `about`s hold the same numbers: two sales at one minute for one amount into different accounts read as one subject (13 of 48 sessions with timed sales hold two such sales). An answered question takes its partner's id. The rework's ids stand: a question that comes back takes an id no question in either version holds when another question holds its own (`idOfItsOwn`), so every repeat the checks report is one the rework returned, and a rework that returns each question once, under an id of its own, clears it (brief 4.5b, fix rounds 1 and 2). What the pairing cannot tell apart it reads one way. A question the rework asks on the subject of a question it left out is that question reworded: it takes an unanswered question's place, and an answered question absorbs it. Under a rephrased `about`, an answered question absorbs only a question in its own words, and one there in other words stays beside it. A question whose `about` the rework rephrases and moves to another place, or rephrases past one subject ("7:50" for "07:50", a nickname), is a new question: an unanswered question then shows twice, and an answered one is asked again. Two questions on one subject that a rework rewords can pair the wrong way round. The fact check after a round reads the answers as the director's words (T1): `<DIRECTOR_ANSWERS>` after the notes (`renderDirectorAnswers`), which `evidenceTruth`, `moneyTruth` and `playersTruth` read, and, as the article judge does, every note the director has sent at a stop in `<DIRECTOR_STOP_NOTES>` after them (see **The notes at the stops**); the weave it judges has no answer and no pick (`weaveForJudge`).
- **The guard** (R11). The fact check reads the director's standing edits right after the weave (`judgedEdits`, `renderJudgeDirectorEdits`), and a finding located in the director's text is a concern on the fact check's mark (`_factCheck.concerns`), never the fix's work. The code checks read only the writer's text (`weaveFindings`, given the director's share from `weaveDirectorsShare`): a thread the director added may have no evidence, a line they rewrote or added (the weave's own, a line of an angle's pitch, a thread's or a connection's) is not held to story terms and is not counted toward the writer's page (`writersShareOf`), nor is the line of a thread they brought into an angle, which `writersShareOf` takes off that angle's list (the thread's text is still the writer's, held as the writer's; phase 4b, brief 3C), and the words they added are never a check's failure; a fault their change causes, such as the verdict flag they took off or a line of the pitch they emptied, is a concern under the edit's id (`_arcValidation.concerns`). The evidence is never theirs (R6), so every piece is the writer's to answer for, under the director's thread too. A repeated thread id is the writer's failure, under the id of a thread the director added or changed too, and its message names the director's thread, the one their edits find under the id (`weaveDirectorsShare`, given the weave, places it in `threadIndexes`), and tells the rework to keep the id on it (brief 4.5b; fix round, fix 3). The edits read one list of the fields the meeting prints (`lib/weave.js` `WEAVE_PRINTED_FIELDS`, walked by `printedWeaveFields`: an angle's pitch, a thread's name and line, a connection's line, never the pick or the evidence the page folds), whose one flagged field, "from your notes", the director's words, the story-terms check leaves alone. The length is the page's (see **Arc Validation Routing**), counted on the writer's share alone (`writersShareOf`), so the director's version is never held to the bound. The stop shows each concern beside its line (`meetingConcerns`).
- **A round that times out** keeps the director's version and gives back only the round's count (ruling 5): the meeting reopens, and the stop says the round did not run (`_arcReworkTimeout.round`, with its note). Its note is the director's to send again: the stop lists it in the note box, not among the standing notes, and their next action files it or leaves it out (a retry with it keeps it as filed, once; see **Approval payloads**; brief 4.5c and its fix round 1). The screen reads the retry by what the note box holds (see **The story meeting on screen**). An automatic pass that times out gives back only the automatic count, and is retried free.
- **The settled weave** (`lib/prompt-renderers/settled-weave.js` `renderSettledWeave`, `settledWeaveOf`): the angle the director picked, as they left it, for every later writer (4.6 and 4.7 call it), read through `lib/weave.js` `settledAngleOf`, the one reading of the settled story (phase 4b, piece 3; spec 2026-10-06 section 8): `{angle, threads, leftOut, connections}`, the angle the pick names (`pickedAngleOf`: `picked`, else the first angle), its threads in its order (a thread the director added after the angle's own, since the meeting appends it), every other thread in the weave's order, and the live connections between two of its threads. `storyConnections`, `lib/map.js` `settledStoryOf`, the map checks' inputs (`settledWeaveThreadsOf`, `mapCheckInputsOf`) and `lib/meeting.js` `meetingDirectorsThreads` read it too. It prints the angle's headline, story, question, why it lands and where it ends up, and "from your notes" only when the picked angle is the first, the one that rests on the director's read (R11); the threads in the story in the angle's order, each by its id, its name and its line, with its evidence under it, each piece's stance, sources and what it shows, or "No evidence yet." for a thread with none, such as one the director added (phase 4b, brief 1B), so the map writer finds the evidence there; the threads the angle leaves out by name on one line and nothing more, since the map writer adds no thread (fix round, fix 5); the connections between its threads, each by its line and the names of the threads it joins (one that joins a thread the angle leaves out is gone); every question with what its answer changes, the thread it sits beside by name, and the director's answer word for word or "Unanswered."; each change the director made at the meeting that the settled story shows marked on its line by its id in the meeting's own form, M and the edit's number (`meetingChangeId`: the stored E3 is M3; brief 4.14a): a line of the sent angle's pitch, a thread brought into its story or left out of it (on its name among the threads left out), a thread added that the story tells, a thread's name or line, a connection's line (`settledMarksOf`; phase 4b, brief 3C). A thread the director added that the sent angle leaves out carries no mark, and the map's sources and places leave it out (`meetingChangesOf`): the map writer places no thread the story leaves out, so it is no change to fit in (the final review). A change it does not show, such as a line of an angle the director did not send, stays with the meeting, and the map names only the changes it shows (`settledChangesOf`, which `lib/map.js` `meetingChangesOf` reads), so no later prompt holds a meeting change and a map's or a desk's edit under one id, nor an id it cannot find on a line. The other angles stay with the meeting, for going back to it (R9): no later writer reads them. The meeting's own rework and fact check hold its edits alone, and name them as stored, and the weave's checks read every connection, so one that joins a thread the weave does not hold is still the writer's failure.
- **Going back to the meeting** (R9). A rollback to `arc-selection` keeps the weave as the director last left it (its fact-check mark, its answers), the standing edits and their baseline, the meeting's notes, its round counters and the photo stops' choices; it clears the approval, the round's mark, marks and report, the map, the article and `evaluationHistory`, and prunes the map's and the article's rejection notes. The meeting reopens with no model call, and the article written after it is judged again. A thread from before the meeting holds no weave: rolled back to the meeting, it gets a fresh weave and its fact check. The character-IDs stop throws on a thread with no weave, and its message names the rollback to the story meeting. `/api/session/:id/arcs` serves the weave.
- **The map reads the settled weave** (brief 4.6): the map writer and its rework read it first, as their task (see **The map**). `selectedArcs`, `narrativeArcs` and `_arcAnalysisCache` went with their last readers (R4), and graph tests that run past the meeting seed `meetingApproved`.

## The questions for the director

**The writers' questions** (phase 3, brief 3.7; spec C15, R5): when the record holds nothing about a player, a roster pronoun is missing, or a ledger line looks wrong, a writer asks the director instead of guessing. Since phase 4 the questions are asked at the story meeting alone (spec section 10): the outline's `writerQuestions` field went with the map (brief 4.6), and the article's with brief 4.7b, with the field's shape (`WRITER_QUESTIONS_PROPERTY`, `WRITER_QUESTION_KINDS`), its normalizer (`writerQuestionsOf`) and its carry through a rework (`carriedWriterQuestions`, `withCarriedWriterQuestions`). **The weave's questions** (phase 4, brief 4.4) are its own property, `questions` (`WEAVE_QUESTIONS_PROPERTY`), each `{id, kind, about, question, changes, thread?}`: C15's three cases as the story meeting asks them (`WEAVE_QUESTION_KINDS`: `player`, `pronoun`, and `figure`, a figure that looks wrong in the ledger or as said in the room), what each answer changes in print, and, since piece 3, the id of the thread it sits beside (`thread`, `WEAVE_QUESTION_THREAD_KEY`), left out when its answer changes who appears in the story, so it sits by the pitch. `weaveQuestionsOf` keeps a question with all five and a valid kind, and reads its `thread` beside the answer, never among the required fields. The checks hold each question the director has not answered to 40 words across its `about`, `question` and `changes` (`QUESTION_WORD_BOUND`; an answered question's words are code's to keep, so no rework could shorten it; fix round 1) and its `thread` to one the weave holds (R4); one question per subject is the writer's rule (C15), not a check's, since no rule tells two questions on one subject from two honest questions on two ledger entries. The weave's OUTPUT FORMAT shows the list empty unless the record leaves something only the director can settle, then one entry's shape (`weaveQuestionsFormatLine`, from the schema's wording). The console's kind labels follow the weave's kinds (`WRITER_QUESTION_KIND_LABELS`). No schema but the weave's carries questions: the map's schema, its director-side gate and the console's map validator refuse the field (brief 4.6), and `content-bundle.schema.json` refuses it (brief 4.7b), so the map's and the article's reworks carry none. **A rework keeps every question it did not answer (R5):** only the weave carries a rework's questions, and the weave's rework sees its previous ones in the version it starts from (inside PREVIOUS WEAVE OUTPUT); only the director answers one, in its box at the story meeting.
