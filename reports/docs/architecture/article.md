# The article

The article writer, the stamp from the map, the programmatic fact check that runs before the judge, and the card check.

Moved verbatim from `reports/CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-11, when CLAUDE.md became a map. A part named in bold elsewhere in this text, such as **The map**, may now sit in another file in `docs/architecture/`; CLAUDE.md lists which file holds which part.

## The writer and the fact check

**The article writer** (phase 4, brief 4.7b; spec 6.1, section 8 and section 10; R1, R4, R7). The article writer writes all the prose from the map's beats: every beat in the map's sections, and no other, in the map's order, each told as its synopsis says, where it has one (phase 4b, piece 4), from the evidence it carries and cited (phase 4b, brief 1E), with the director's desk edits first, as `<HAND_EDITS>` gives them (briefs 4.7e and 4.7f).
- **What it reads** (`generateContentBundle`, `articleWriterInputs`): the settled weave first (`settledWeaveOf`), then the map as the director left it in `<STORY_MAP>` (`articleMapOf`: `state.outline`, less each photo the director has left out since), under a label that reads each beat as a move with its people, its `synopsis` where the beat has one (the one sentence saying what the article tells there; piece 4, R1), the threads it carries and its evidence, each piece's sources (each named source with what it holds, `lib/evidence.js` `SOURCES_GLOSS`, the gloss the weave writer's Sources line prints; fix round 3), what it shows and its stance, and a card as the marker on a beat with its document on the piece the beat flags (`STORY_MAP_LABEL`; phase 4b, brief 1E), then its task (`ARTICLE_TASK` in `lib/prompt-builder.js`, which points at C16, C2, C4, T1 and C9 and restates none), then what it read before: PHOTOS (the hero first, marked `[hero image]`: the map's top photo as the article reads it, `topPhotoOf(articleMapOf(state))`, the one reading the instruction's hero line, the stamp and the judge's PHOTOS share, whatever the stored `heroImage`; brief 4.7c), the record, the money, the director's notes, SESSION_FACTS, the instruction with its `<SCHEMA>`, the eight craft files, and the standing notes last. The instruction takes each section's id (the map's slot) and the top photo from the map, and each heading, the headline and the deck as the task gives them, so it names none of the theme's slots; the HERO IMAGE line went. Each section's heading, the headline and the deck are the director's own where they edited one, and each photo's place is the director's where they moved the photo, the map's otherwise (briefs 4.7c and 4.7d). The beats line and the words line give the director's edits precedence (briefs 4.7e and 4.7f): "the director's edits first, as HAND_EDITS gives them"; otherwise every beat in the map's sections, and no other, in the map's order, and the writer's words, transitions and detail. Since piece 4 (R2) the order of a section's beats is the map's: the words line gives the beats in the map's order and no longer gives the writer the order within a section, and C16 has the writer tell each beat as its synopsis says, where it has one, as `STORY_MAP_LABEL` reads it. Since phase 4b (brief 1E; spec 2026-10-05 sections 5.2, 5.3 and 7) C16 has the article writer tell each beat from the evidence it carries and cite it, and the beats line adds only what C16 does not say (fix round 3): a beat that carries no evidence, such as one the director added on the map, told from the record, and each source cited as T1 sets out and a card as C9 does, T1 named by its id alone because a user prompt carries no `<truth-rules>` tag; the words line still gives each scene its detail from the record. The instruction's evidence-card line names the cards: the director's edits first, as on the beats line; otherwise the map's, and no other, each flagged piece's document beside its beat, read through `beatCardOf` (`mapCardsLine`), so the writer never reads a card from the JSON by hand. The map keeps the beat of a card the director deleted (only a deleted photo leaves it) and lists in `leftOut` material an inserted paragraph may use, and code does not restore a send-back's rework, so the edits come first. The rule for those edits, with its send-back exception, is stated in `<HAND_EDITS>` alone (`buildRevisionContext`), and neither line restates it: 4.7e's lines did, without the exception, so a send-back whose note asked for a cut card back read two statements that disagreed. The lines name the block without its brackets, as the judges' questions name FINANCIAL_SUMMARY: a rework carries the task above the block, and the removed-phrase scan strips a `<HAND_EDITS>` block from the first `<HAND_EDITS>` it meets. With no edit, as in the first draft, the prompt carries no `<HAND_EDITS>`, and the lines ask for every beat in the map's sections, and no other. The instruction's heading and headline lines defer to the task's lines, the headline line giving the kicker no owner. So the task and the instruction, which a rework carries word for word ahead of its `<HAND_EDITS>`, ask for a heading, headline or deck the director edited at the desk, and the place of a photo they moved there, as the director's, and leave a kicker they edited, and a block they cut, added or moved, to `<HAND_EDITS>`. The top photo stays the map's: the desk edits only its caption. The builder fails loud with no weave or no map. The rework reads the same sections first (see **Reworkers see what their writers saw**).
- **The stamp** (R7; `stampFromMap`, in `generateContentBundle`): code stamps the map's headline, deck and top photo into the first draft, keeping the writer's kicker, and its hero caption when it captioned the top photo; a map with no top photo, or one the director has left out since, prints no hero. A headline or deck the director wrote on the map, or a top photo they chose there (a standing map edit the map carries), is recorded as the director's edit at the article stop (`_articleHandEdits`, through `standingAfterSendBack`, a field with no writer's text before it), so an automatic pass that changes it gets it put back and a judge's finding located in it is a concern. At the desk the director edits the headline, the deck and the hero's caption. The stamp records no cut (brief 4.7d): the director takes the top photo off the map by moving it into a section (`movePhoto`), which leaves the map with no top photo, so the writer is told to print no hero and the draft prints none; a cut of `heroImage` would be carried by its text wherever the page prints it, and T13 prints that photo in its section. A hero a later automatic pass adds to such a draft is not flagged; the desk shows it.
- **The stop** sends `settledStory` in place of the old outline's thesis, and no `writerQuestions`; a photo the director deletes at the desk joins the leave-out list (see **Approval payloads**).
- **The parked detective** (R1): the article stage's detective branches went with the old stages: its article writer and rework rules, and its printed schema, the frozen `content-bundle.detective-prompt.json`.
- `scripts/render-prompts.js` renders the writer and its rework from the thread's weave and map, or the fixed ones it plants, and the content-bundle probe builds its call from the fixed weave and map, both through `articleWriterInputs`.

**Programmatic article fact-check** (`lib/content-bundle-fact-check.js`): `evaluateArticle` runs `factCheckContentBundle` BEFORE the Opus evaluation, mirroring how `validateArcStructure` gates `evaluateArcs`. Pure string checks, no LLM: evidence-card fidelity (every substantial sentence of a card's `content` must appear in its source's `fullContent`/`content`/`description`/`text` — never its `summary`, which is the paraphrase a fabrication imitates), unknown card sources, leaked prompt-example strings, roster coverage, photo references, reporter mode, repeated absence statements (remote), and NPC pronouns. A structural failure under `REVISION_CAPS.ARTICLE` short-circuits the Opus call and routes straight to `reviseContentBundle` with one actionable line per defect; at the cap Opus runs and escalates to the human with the fact-check attached. The result lives in `_articleFactCheck` and reaches the article checkpoint as `factCheck`. Since phase 4 (brief 4.7a) it also returns each finding with where it sits, for the desk's marks (4.10): `findings`, each `{kind, status, place, excerpt, message, line, editId?}`, its `kind` the check, its `status` the list its message is in, its `place` a paragraph `{section, paragraph}` (the section's id and the paragraph's ordinal), a headline field `{field}`, a card `{tokenId, section}` or `{tokenId, sidebar: true}`, a photo `{filename, section}` or `{filename, hero: true}`, a quote block or a section the length counted `{section}` (4.10 marks the length on the section's heading), or null (a player never named, a phrase across two pieces), its `excerpt` the text the page prints there (`printedExcerpt`: a check that read a folded copy, lowercased or with the quoted spans taken out, has its hit found in the printed text), its `line` (brief 4.10b), what is wrong in the article at that place in the director's words, with that place's part of a finding that sits at several (the em-dashes outside quoted speech in that paragraph, that section's words of prose) and a card's document left for the desk to name where the line holds `DOCUMENT_SLOT`, each line saying exactly what prints (brief 4.10c: a photo the director left out says the article still prints it, and a phrase that runs across two pieces, the deck and a paragraph or two paragraphs, is quoted as the article prints it, its excerpt too, taken since brief 4.10d from where the two pieces meet in it, `acrossExcerpt`, or the check's own phrase when no two adjacent pieces hold it), and `editId` on a concern about one of the director's edits; a message that names several places has a finding at each. The desk's marks print the line, and the rework reads the message, whose prefix the console still groups by. A reporter-mode phrase in several of the director's pieces under different edits is one concern per edit, each finding carrying its own piece's `editId` and that edit's concern (brief 4.7c). Its roster coverage covers the players the map, as the director left it, places in a beat (`placedPlayers`, from `mapTally` over `mapRosterOf` in `buildFactCheckArgs`): a roster player the map does not place is the director's decision and no finding, and a placed player the article never names is fixed by writing the map's beat; with no map, every roster player is checked. Its length counts words with `lib/word-count.js`, as the weave's bound does. A hit in the director's own text, or caused by the director's cut, is an advisory under the edit's id instead (see **The director's edits are final**). **Every judgement call in the module errs toward NOT flagging** — a false structural failure costs a paid Opus revision. Two consequences of that rule (I2): the **reporter-mode scan reads NARRATOR text only** (`narratorText` — paragraph blocks plus headline/kicker/deck; NOT `visibleText`, whose generosity is for roster coverage), each piece with its quoted spans masked (`maskQuotedSpans`, one rule with `stripQuotedSpans`; brief 4.10e), because a correctly attributed player quote saying “I voted” is the article doing its job, in a quote block or quoted inside a paragraph, and it matches its vote and presence phrases ("my votes" among them since brief 4.10e) as whole words with whitespace alone between them wherever it reads them, a finding's excerpt included, which is the narrator's own words and never a quoted span (brief 4.10d: `wholePhrase`; brief 4.10e: `phraseExcerpt` in a piece, `acrossExcerpt` across two), because ALN's names end in "i" often and "Remi voted" holds "i voted" in letters; and the uncalibrated checks named in **`FACT_CHECK_ADVISORY_ONLY = ['npcPronouns', 'leakedExample', 'repeatedAbsence', 'emDash', 'productionWords', 'novaPronoun', 'length', 'headCount']`** push to `advisoryWarnings` only, never to `structuralIssues`. One rule, `QUOTED_SPANS` (brief 4.10f), says what a quoted span is, and every check that reads the narrator's prose apart from `length` reads it: the other phase 3 checks below strip the spans (`stripQuotedSpans`), reporter mode and the absence statements mask them (`maskQuotedSpans`), and an excerpt found in print may cross them (`PRINTED_GAP`). The `length` check counts the narrator's prose with its quoted lines included, under no span rule. A span in double quotation marks, straight or curly, runs from one mark to the next on its line. A single quotation mark, straight or curly, is told from an apostrophe by where it stands, since the writers quote speech in straight single marks too: in a word (Kai's) it is an apostrophe; after a plural (the players' votes) it closes a span only when no other mark on its line can; before a shortened word ('90s) it opens one only when a closing mark follows on its line; where the rule cannot tell, it reads the span. `repeatedAbsence` (phase 2) counts the narrator's first-person statements that they were not in the room, remote only, read outside the quoted spans as reporter mode reads its phrases (brief 4.10f: 'I wasn't there,' Kai said, is none, and so is a statement a quoted span splits), and flags more than one, each finding's excerpt the printed text where the count read it (beside "Kai wasn't there" or a quoted alibi it quotes the narrator's statement); its message has its own prefix, `Absence stated N times (remote):`, which no console group claims, so the article stop lists it under Advisory. Card fidelity, unknown sources, roster coverage, photo references and narrator-scoped reporter mode stay structural. **Phase 3 (3.4) added five code checks, journalist only, each an advisory with its own prefix and its own console group** (`ADVISORY_GROUPS` in `console/checkpoint-view-logic.js`), none of them on the approve button's unresolved count. Each reads the narrator's prose (`narratorSegments`: the headline, kicker, deck and paragraph blocks, never a card, a quote block or a caption), all but `length` with the quoted spans stripped (`stripQuotedSpans`), and says what it found and where: `emDash` (`Em-dash in the narrator's prose:`, C4), `productionWords` (`Production word in print:`: director, GM, game master, tier, timer, a bare "token"; T14), `novaPronoun` (`Gendered pronoun for Nova:`, T9), `length` (`Over length:`: the narrator's prose, `narratorText` whole, above 1,800 words, with the words per section; C4, R4) and `headCount` (`Head count:`: a statement of how many people were in the room that disagrees with the roster, which counts a guest reporter who plays a character once; vote and account counts never match, nor does a statement about part of the room, such as "the two people in the room who never sold" or "three players sat in the room and never said a word"; T10). The NPC check (`npcPronouns`, `Pronoun error:`) reads the same quote-stripped narrator prose for the journalist and takes the theme's NPC entries (`npcs`): Marcus written they/them or "she", and Blake (or the Valet) given a gendered pronoun that neither the director's words (`directorText`: notes, corrections, accusation, since brief 4.7a the answers at the story meeting, and since phases 14 and 15 (brief B) every note sent at a stop, T1; `lib/director-words.js`) nor the roster give; it reads the subject and reflexive forms and the possessive "his" there, and leaves the object forms, "her" and "hers" out ("Blake paid him", "Marcus buried her exposé", "Marcus took what was hers": on the 53 published reports every "her" read after Marcus was someone else's, fix 3.4c, and "hers" goes with it, fix 3.4cb), reading "his" only when no preposition governs the name ("the buyer who sold to Blake spent his cut" is the buyer's), and it never scans Nova, whose rule is `novaPronoun`'s. It reads they/them the same way, the subject and the reflexive alone (`NPC_THEY_PRONOUNS`: they, themself, themselves), never the object "them" or the possessives "their" and "theirs", which pointed at other people on every stored article that held one ("Marcus built them to", "Marcus had a room full of them"), and a pronoun in a speech tag, right before a verb of saying and set off by a comma, a dash, a bracket or a quoted line ("Marcus, she said, was"), is the speaker's in each of the journalist's pronoun scans, Marcus's, Blake's and Nova's (`inSpeechTag`, its verbs in the past, the third person and the base form; task 4.14c: five false marks in 5 of the 11 stored articles); the parked detective's scan is unchanged. The parked detective's scan reads every they/them form, as before. `evaluator-nodes.js#buildFactCheckArgs(state)` builds every argument, for the node and for `scripts/lib/render-calls.js`. The journalist fix lines say what T8 and the remote mode block say (since 3.9; a rework reads the vote and remote-presence lines as must-fix, and the repeatedAbsence line, an advisory, as a SHOULD CONSIDER suggestion under R23): the vote line puts T8 in its words since phases 14 and 15 (brief F: "Nova takes no side in the verdict: Nova never votes, joins the room's accusation or exposes a memory (T8).", its prefix unchanged, and the director's line beside it says the phrase has the reporter take a side in the verdict) and never sends the rework to name who acted, since an exposure stays anonymous unless the evidence log or the director's words name who turned it in (task 4.5f, as the article judge's T6 clause reads them); the remote line says Nova monitored from outside the warehouse and has the moment told as a scene, with attribution where it matters; the `repeatedAbsence` line says Nova says so once, early, and tells the room's events as scenes after it. No fix line carries an em-dash. The photo check no longer reads the top-level `photos` list, which never prints, and it reads a photo the director excluded as no usable reference, so its fix line offers only kept photos (the 4b fix batch), and the whiteboard photo likewise: a printed whiteboard is an invalid reference, "this is the whiteboard, the room's working notes, and its photo stays out of the article", checked even when the session's photo list is empty (`whiteboardPhoto`, from `whiteboardFilenameOf`; the 4c fix batch). Which photos the session holds is one rule, `sessionPhotoNames` (`lib/hand-edit-diff.js`, the non-empty basenames), which the article's rework reads too, to give the restore its list (task 4.5g). The detective keeps today's checks and messages. To promote an advisory check after a live session supports it, drop its name from that list and push its message to `structuralIssues` at the marked call site — but do NOT reword the message: `console/checkpoint-view-logic.js` groups by message prefix.

## The card check

**The card check reads only printed text** (phase 2 slice 2.5). Card fidelity reads inline `evidence-card` blocks only. A sidebar entry (`evidenceCards[]`) prints its significance, headline and summary and never its `content` or `owner`, so it is checked for a known document id and nothing else (a made-up id prints a headline about nothing); its `content` is not scanned for leaked examples either. `isVerbatim` strips each fragment's leading and trailing quotation marks before matching, and `normalize` folds every single and double quotation mark to `'` (092026's `ale003`, `mor003` and `95e749b7` were correct excerpts failed by those two faults). Roster coverage (`visibleText(bundle, theme)`) counts only what the theme's page prints: never sidebar `content`/`owner`, top-level `photos`, pull quotes or photo/hero `characters`, and on the detective page never the headline, the hero, the sidebar or a card owner; the evaluator passes `theme`. One report per defect: `cardFidelity` holds one item per document and outcome, `{tokenId, ok, reason, locations: [{placement: 'inline'|'sidebar', section}]}`, and one message per failing item, which names the places (`Evidence card "x" (in section "the-story", and in the sidebar) …`), so the console's card list (`cardFidelity`, with `cardLocationText`) and its counts (the strings) agree. The not-verbatim fix line says to keep the card and copy its sentences from the document with that id in `<RECORD>`; it never offers removal. The unknown-source and leaked-example lines still offer it. Each message keeps its prefix. The writer is no longer asked for `content` on a sidebar entry (`evidenceCards[].content` stays optional in the schema, described as not printed), and the article stop's sidebar editor edits the summary, not the content.
