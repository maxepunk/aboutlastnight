# The story meeting as pitched angles

Piece 3 of phase 4b in the roadmap (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-22-roadmap.md`). The design was settled with the director on 2026-10-06, after session 100326, the first session run on piece 1. It changes what the story meeting's writer pitches, what the director reads and does at the meeting, and what the map writer receives from it. By the director's call it is built before piece 2, the review of the director's changes, and before piece 4, the map's page.

It supersedes:
- the roadmap's piece 3, a pitch over a board of threads in lanes by role;
- the phase 4 spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-story-meeting-and-map.md`) on the weave's single story, its roles and main thread, the stronger main thread and the strike on a connection: sections 4.2 to 4.4, and two of its decisions, "The meeting shows one weave in full, with every thread and its role" and "The director changes the weave through roles, added threads and struck connections";
- piece 1's spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-story-level-and-evidence.md`) on the meeting's page and its writer: sections 4.1 and 7, and the meeting's bound of 300 words in sections 1 and 6.1.

What piece 1 says about a thread, a connection, the evidence underneath and the story terms stands. The vocabulary is `CONTEXT.md`'s. This piece adds angle, and retires main thread and role.

## 1. What piece 3 is for

The director settles the story by choosing among whole stories, not by assembling one from parts. The writer pitches two or three angles, each a story the article could tell. The director picks one, adjusts it, and sends it on. The page reads top to bottom as a memo.

- Picking an angle, settling its pitch, choosing which threads are in and answering the writer are one act on one page.
- Every line is in the director's words for the story: a headline, a story, a question, threads named for what happened.
- The page is at most 450 words as it prints, with any angle open.

## 2. Why: session 100326

Piece 1 worked. The meeting showed 413 words against 676 on 100226. The director approved it in one round, about 12 minutes after it opened, and answered all three questions. The director called it better and the length manageable. Three things still stood in the way.

- **The roles were the writer's words, not the director's.** "Grounds it", "complicates it", "mirrors it" and "carries it forward" are C16's lens work written onto the page. The roadmap planned this piece as a board with the threads in lanes by role. Mocked from 100326, it was rejected: "I don't get the whole 'grounds it, complicates it, mirrors it, carries it forward' thing ... It feels like a leftover from the fix we started to make already in phase 4b". Settling the pitch, choosing what's in, picking an angle and answering the writer are, in the director's words, "all connected, not discrete concepts".
- **The thread names did not say what the threads were.** 100326's money thread was "Gone dark", beside "Dropped theories" and "Alex untouched". The director: "the thread names are not very legible (not visually, but conceptually) so it's hard for me to know, for example, what 'gone dark' means".
- **The questions repeated themselves.** Two of the three asked whether the first-burial bonus was paid with the first sale, one of them in 79 words. The director answered "yes" to both.
With the roles gone, the threads needed something else to say how they make a story. The design session of 2026-10-02 weighed pitched angles against one proposal to edit, and chose one. Shown a memo of angles built from 100326, the director chose angles: "angles are right".

## 3. Angles and threads

- **An angle** is one story the article could tell: a headline, the story in two or three sentences, the question it carries, why it lands with the players, and where it ends up. It is told through threads, in its own order, and leaves the other threads out.
- **A thread** is a line of the story, as piece 1 defines it, with its evidence underneath. The angles draw on one shared set of threads. A thread's name says what happened, with its people, in plain words: "Memories sold while the room argued", not "Gone dark".
- **A connection** is where two threads touch, as piece 1 defines it. An angle uses the connections between its own threads.
- **The settled story** is the angle the director sent on, as they left it. It is what every later writer works from.

## 4. What the writer pitches

One call, as now. It reads what it reads today.

- **Two or three angles.** When the director's notes end with their own read, angle 1 is that read worked up as a pitch, and "from your notes" quotes the words it rests on, as now. The other angles are the strongest alternatives the session offers. With no read in the notes, every angle is the writer's, the strongest first.
- **Each angle:** a headline; one sentence that sums it up, for its card; the story, in two or three sentences; the question it carries; why it lands with the players, in a line; where it ends up, in a line (its convergence); and its threads, in the order it tells them.
- **The room's verdict is a thread in every angle,** because the article always reports it (T2).
- **The threads:** each a plain name and one line, with its evidence underneath (piece 1). No role and no reason. A thread no angle uses may stay, so the director can bring it in.
- **The connections:** each one line where two threads touch, with its evidence underneath (piece 1).
- **The questions** (C15): at most one per subject, each one or two short sentences with a line on what its answer changes. Each sits beside the thread its answer changes, or by the pitch when it is about who appears in the story.

Every line is in story terms as piece 1 sets them out, except the headlines, which are the article's own printed lines, and the questions, which may name the figure they ask about. "From your notes" is the director's own words, and the verdict is printed by code.

## 5. What the director reads

A memo, in this order:
1. the verdict, printed by code from the parse, and "from your notes", or one line saying the notes end without the director's read, so every angle is the writer's;
2. the angles side by side, each its headline and its one sentence; the open angle's card says it is open below;
3. the open angle as its pitch: the headline, the story, the question, why it lands and where it ends up, with the questions that sit by the pitch;
4. its threads: those in the story, in the angle's order, each its name and its line, with the questions that sit beside them; those left out, by name, each opening in place to its line; and the line to add a thread;
5. where the threads meet: the connections between the threads in the story, each its line;
6. the note box and the three buttons.

Angle 1 is open when the page opens. Any line opens in place to its evidence, "What's behind it", as piece 1 has it. No roles, no lanes and no ids. At most 450 words as the page prints, with any angle open.

## 6. What the director does

- **Pick an angle.** Instant, with no model call.
- **Rewrite any line** of the open pitch, or any thread's name or line, in place.
- **Flip a thread in or out** of the open angle. A thread brought in goes after the angle's own. The verdict's thread is always in, and says so.
- **Add a thread** the writer missed: a name and a line. It goes in the story, and the next writer finds its evidence (piece 1, section 5.3).
- **Answer each question** in its box.
- **Write a note,** and press Approve, Reweave or Send back.

A change to a pitch belongs to the angle it was made on, and switching angles keeps it on the page. A change to a thread's name or line shows in every angle that uses the thread. Whatever angle is open when the director presses a button is the one that goes. A change to an angle that was not sent is not kept past the round.

From today's page, these go: the role picker, the main thread, the stronger main thread, the left-out reasons, the convergence as a line of its own (it is each angle's "where it ends up"), and the strike on a connection. A connection shows only while both its threads are in, so taking a thread out takes its connections with it. A connection the director wants gone while both its threads stay in goes with a note, on a Reweave or a send-back.

## 7. The three buttons

A worked case, from 100326. The director opens angle 1, flips "Memories sold while the room argued" in and "Taylor buried Ashe's story, then exposed Morgan" out, adds "Jess turned in Vic's meeting with Morgan", and answers the Mel question. "Where it ends" still talks only of the titans and Alex, and the new thread has no evidence yet.

- **Approve: no wait, and the map writer takes it from here.** The open angle goes to the map writer exactly as the director left it, with an optional note that stands for every later writer. The map writer finds evidence for the thread the director added, places the money in the moves, and works out how it fits. If the record can't carry the added thread, the map's gap note says so (piece 1, section 5.3). For changes that stand on their own: a headline reworded, a line sharpened, answers given, a thread flipped that doesn't change what the story says.
- **Reweave: the writer makes the rest of the angle agree with the director's changes, and the director checks it before the map.** The rework reads the director's changes, each to be kept exactly as written. It finds evidence for "Jess turned in Vic's meeting", or says the record can't carry it. It rewrites only the lines the changes left out of step, such as "where it ends", which must now account for the money, and writes the connections between the threads now in. It works on the open angle, its threads and the connections between them: code keeps the other angles exactly as they were, and every thread and connection outside the open angle, so the director can still switch. A thread the open angle shares with another angle may be reworded, and the new words show in both. Code puts back any line of the director's that the rework changed, as now. The checks and the fact check run again, and the meeting reopens on the open angle, with the round's changes marked beside their lines. About 8 to 15 minutes of model time on 100326's timings. Offered only when the director changed the open angle or a thread, or wrote a note: a pick alone, or answers alone, have nothing to fit in.
- **Send back: the note sets the rethink.** The rework may rewrite any angle, pitch new ones, or change one of the director's edits where the note needs it, saying why. Same wait as a Reweave. The meeting reopens on the angle the director had open, if the rework kept it, otherwise on angle 1. For a direction rather than edits: "lead with the money", "angle 2, but less about Taylor", "none of these; pitch me three about the Valet".

## 8. What travels to the map

The settled story, the open angle as the director left it:
- its headline, story, question, why it lands and where it ends up, and "from your notes" when the angle is angle 1, the one that rests on them;
- the threads in the story, in its order, each with its name, its line and its evidence;
- the threads left out, by name;
- the connections between the threads in the story, each with its line and its evidence;
- every question, with the director's answer or "Unanswered";
- each change the director made at the meeting, marked by its id, as now (M3).

The other angles stay with the meeting, for going back to it (R9). No later writer reads them.

## 9. The checks

### 9.1 Code checks

Free, on the writer's output and on each rework's, before the director sees the page:
- **The angles.** Two or three. Each has every field and names its threads, all of them threads the weave holds, the verdict's among them. "From your notes", when present, is word for word in the director's notes or corrections, as now.
- **Story terms,** as piece 1 sets them out: each angle's sentence, story, question, why it lands and where it ends up, each thread's name and line, and each connection's line.
- **Structure.** Every thread, connection, angle and question has an id of its own. Each connection joins two threads the weave holds.
- **The evidence,** as piece 1 sets it out. Every thread the writer wrote has at least one piece that supports it.
- **The questions.** Each comes to at most 40 words: what it is about, the question and what its answer changes. Each one that sits beside a thread names a thread the weave holds. One question per subject is the writer's rule (C15), not a code check (section 17).
- **Length.** The page with each angle open is within 450 words as it prints, counted as piece 1 counts it, with the writer's own words held to their allowance (section 17).

A failure sends the pitch back for one automatic rework, as now. A fault that one of the director's own changes causes is a concern beside the change, never a rework, as now.

### 9.2 The fact check

One pass and one fix, as now. It reads every angle against the evidence of its threads, each line against its evidence and each piece against the record. Every angle is checked, not only the one open first, because the director may pick any of them.

### 9.3 What the director sees

Nothing, when everything passes. A check that still fails after the rework shows beside its line, in story terms, as now.

## 10. The writers

- **The meeting's writer** pitches the angles as section 4 sets out. The lens work (C16: the money, behaviour, victimization) happens in its reasoning. It reaches the page as the angles, each a different way the threads make a story, and as the evidence, each piece marked as supporting its line or cutting against it.
- **Its rework.** An automatic pass after the checks or the fact check works on the whole pitch, as now. A Reweave works on the open angle (section 7). A send-back works on whatever the note asks.
- **The map writer** reads the settled story (section 8) in place of the weave. The story leads where the angle leads, with its first thread, and lands where it ends up. Every thread in the story lands in a move and every connection between them lands too, as the map's checks hold now.
- **The article writer and the article judge** read the settled story through the settled weave, as now.

## 11. Rule text to change

The new text comes to the director to read before the build, as the rule text of piece 1 did.
- **C1** has the arc writer propose the thesis as one story and the question that carries it, with one line beside it when it sees a stronger main thread. Instead, it pitches two or three angles, each a thesis with its story, question and headline; when the director gave a thesis, it is angle 1; the director settles the thesis by picking an angle and adjusting it. The stronger main thread goes.
- **C16** has one main thread and each other thread in a role toward it (grounds, complicates, mirrors, carries forward), a left-out thread with its reason, and the lens work reaching the weave "as each thread's role". Instead:
  - the threads are shared material, and each angle uses the threads its story needs, in the order it tells them;
  - the room's verdict is a thread in every angle;
  - a thread's name says what happened, with its people, in plain words, never a label or a figure of speech;
  - the lens work reaches the angles and the evidence's stance;
  - the convergence is each angle's "where it ends up";
  - the map lays the settled angle across the sections, leading where it leads.
- **C15:** at most one question per subject, in one or two short sentences, beside the thread its answer changes or by the pitch.
- **`CONTEXT.md`:** a new entry for Angle; Weave, Arc, Thesis, Story meeting, Convergence and Reweave rewritten; Main thread retired. "Angle" leaves the avoid lists of Arc, Weave and Thesis, and "pitch" leaves Story meeting's.

## Decisions (2026-10-06)

- The story meeting is a memo of two or three pitched angles over one shared set of threads. The roles, the main thread and the lanes go.
- When the notes end with the director's read, angle 1 is it.
- The director picks an angle, rewrites its pitch and any thread in place, flips threads in or out, adds a thread, answers the questions, and approves, reweaves or sends back.
- A Reweave works on the open angle and keeps the other angles as written, so the director can still switch.
- A thread's name says what happened, in plain words.
- One short question per subject, beside what its answer changes.
- The verdict is a thread in every angle and cannot be taken out.
- The page runs to at most 450 words as it prints, with the left-out threads by name.
- The meeting's page is built before the map's; piece 2 follows them.
- Considered and dropped: lanes by role, rejected on the mock-up; the strike on a connection, unused on 100226 and 100326; reordering threads by hand.
