# Your words at every stop, and the article in the world

This is phases 14 and 15 in the roadmap (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-22-roadmap.md`, "Phases 14 to 16, after 100426"). The design was settled with the director on 2026-10-09, after session 100426, from their desk edits on 100226, 100326 and 100426 and the reasons they confirmed. The two phases ship together, and the director's next session tests them.
- **Phase 14** makes everything the director says at a stop count as their words for every later stage, lines up ledger rows the session report logged off the game's clock, and tells every stage who wrote the article.
- **Phase 15** changes the rules the writers and judges read so the article sits in its world: its authors are in the story, Nova holds only what exists in the world, the game's rules stay off the page, Nova commits to a theory, and the investigation happens at the time of day the session's clock gives.

It supersedes:
- the rule-set spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-rule-set.md`) on the account-name rule (T4), the time of day (the world and T7), Nova's place in the room (T8), the one plain line where the record stops (C4), Nova's motive line (C12) and the closing (C14);
- phase 4's four sources of the director's words (briefs 4.7c and 4.7d), which become one list with the director's notes at the stops in it.

The vocabulary is `CONTEXT.md`'s. A note is what the director sends with an action at a stop. Their session notes, entered at the start, are "the director's notes".

## 1. What the two phases are for

The director stops making the same corrections. On 100426 they ruled on the transfer times at the map, and the article judge, which never read the ruling, took it out of the article. At the desk of 100226, 100326 and 100426 they spent most of their edits putting the article back in its world: crediting the guest reporter's reporting, cutting quotes from the players' character sheets, replacing lines that read the players the rulebook, and having Nova commit to a theory. After these phases:
- a ruling the director makes at any stop holds for every later stage;
- a ledger row the session report logged on the wrong clock reaches the writers on the right one, and the director sees that it moved;
- the writers know who wrote the article, what Nova could hold, and when the investigation happened;
- the writers place the article in its world without the director's help.

## 2. Why: sessions 100226, 100326 and 100426

**100426**, the first session on pieces 3 and 4, showed the stops working: the director built their angle from the writer's threads, reordered the map, and the article followed. Two things went wrong.
- **A ruling did not hold.** The session report logged the first-burial bonus and four transfers nine hours off the rest of the timeline (stamped 12:55 to 2:14 AM, against sales from 3:54 to 5:15 PM). The director answered the writer's question about it at the meeting, then ruled at the map: "time-shifting the transfer timings 9 hours to fit them with the timeline works. They are relevant." The article writer read the ruling and used it. The article judge never sees a note, called the shift a fact error twice, and its automatic rework took the times out. The published article says "during the investigation".
- **The writers did not know their own narrator.** The director named "Cassandra" in a photo; the map writer raised her as a guest missing from the roster. Cassandra Nova is the article's writer. Only the article's byline line knew Nova's first name.

**The desk edits** of the three sessions, read with the director for what they do in the world, not as phrasing:
- **The authors are part of the story.** Taylor Chase, the contributing reporter on 100226's and 100326's byline, turned in 14 memories under his name on 100326. The writer wrote them as "Taylor put his name on 14 memories" and his byline as a conflict to disclose. The director made them "Taylor sourced 14 memories" and "thanks to Taylor Chase's investigative reporting", and his byline "because of his contributions to our investigation". On 100426 Ashe earned the byline by a deal with Nova; the writer worked out from the log that an anonymous burst of memories was Ashe's, and the director cut it.
- **Nova holds only what exists in the world.** The writers quoted the players' character sheets: "His file reads", "his brief is to 'Control the narrative'", "Her suspected motive". The director cut every one. Where a sheet's backstory mattered they kept it as evidence: "There's evidence he met Vic 'at 8 for an emergency strategy meeting'".
- **The game's rules stay off the page.** "Sellers name their own accounts, so that one says nothing about Kai Andersen" became "By all accounts, Kai was nowhere near Blake at 9:07, so this account is likely a ruse to try and frame her." "Sellers name accounts as they please; Vic1 says nothing about Vic Kingsley" became "Vic1 may or may not be an account belonging to Vic Kingsley, but it does make one wonder."
- **Nova commits to a theory.** "The ledger doesn't name the buyer. I suspect NeurAI's board." became "it is the opinion of this reporter that the NeurAI board of directors had to have been involved." The buyer line and the closing follow each session's epilogue: "an interested party associated with NeurAI" on 100226, the board on 100326, a handover to Alex and a new company, Resurgence AI, on 100426.
- **The players see their choices as their characters would.** "The idea that she buried them rests on Vic's belief alone" became "Vic's belief she buried many memories may just be supported by an anonymous tip".
- **The time of day.** 100426 ran in the afternoon. The writer wrote "By 4:22 PM this morning"; the director cut "this morning".

Every one of these traces to an input or a rule: the notes never reach the judges; Nova's first name and the guest reporter reach only the byline line; the six character sheets sit in every session's record as documents like any other; the account-name rule and C4's "one plain line" where the record stops invite the rulebook lines; the rules allow Nova suspicion, allegation and question but never opinion; and the world, T7 and the timeline's labels call every investigation "the morning".

## 3. Your words at every stop

**What counts.** Everything the director writes reaches every later stage as their words: their session notes, their corrections at the input review, their accusation, their answers at the story meeting, and every note they send at a stop, with approve, reweave or send-back. Today the notes at the stops reach the writers and their reworks, but neither judge, the fact check nor the evidence check.

**How a note is read.** A note can say two kinds of thing, and every reader takes them apart:
- **What happened, or a ruling on the record**, is record, as the session notes are. "Time-shifting the transfer timings 9 hours … works." "Cassandra in the photo is Cassandra Nova, the writer of this article." A judge never calls a line that follows such a note an error.
- **A direction for the article** is the director's direction, not evidence that something happened. "What if Alex's appointment as CEO was an intended outcome …" The article writes it as Nova's reading, and a judge holds that reading to the rules on opinion (section 9), never to the record as if it were a claim of fact, and never flags a line for following the direction.

**Every reader, one list.** The director's words are one list, read by:
- the story meeting's fact check, which reads the meeting's notes so far;
- the article judge;
- the article fact check, which reads them where it already reads the director's words (a pronoun the director gives Blake);
- the evidence check, so a piece of evidence may cite a note under "the director's notes";
- the verdict guard, which reads the director's words as record.

The writers keep reading the notes as standing notes, as today. The judges read the whole list, including the note a rework is acting on.

**What stays.** "From your notes" on the meeting's page still quotes only the director's session notes and corrections. A rollback that takes back a send-back note, as today, takes it back from every reader.

## 4. Ledger rows off the game's clock

The session report from the game engine logged the bonus and the transfers of 100326 and 100426 nine hours off the rest of the timeline. The fix belongs in the game engine, which the director makes separately. Until then the pipeline lines the rows up.

**Which rows.** A bonus or transfer row more than three hours outside the span of the session's sales and exposures is off the clock. On-clock rows sit inside the span; the off-clock rows of 100326 and 100426 sat seven and a half to nine hours out.

**The shift.** Every off-clock row of a session moves by one shift of whole hours.
- When the bonus is among them, the shift is the one that lands the bonus nearest the first sale, since the first-burial bonus is paid with the first sale.
- Otherwise it is the shift of the fewest hours that puts every off-clock row inside the span.
- A shift is used only when every row it moves lands inside the span. When none does, the rows stay as logged and are flagged.

| Session | Off-clock rows | Shift | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100326 | the bonus at 6:21 AM; sales 9:20 to 10:23 PM | back 9 hours | the bonus at 9:21 PM, a minute after the first sale (9:21 AM in the story); 8 hours would also fit the span, and the bonus decides |
| 100426 | the bonus at 12:55 AM and transfers 1:28 to 2:14 AM; sales 3:54 to 5:15 PM | back 9 hours, the only shift that fits | the bonus at 3:55 PM, a minute after the first sale; the transfers 4:28 to 5:14 PM |

**At the input review.** The ledger panel says what moved: "The session report logged the first-burial bonus and 4 transfers 9 hours off the game's clock. They're shifted back 9 hours to line up with the sales." When no shift fits: "3 ledger rows sit off the game's clock, and no single shift lines them up. They print as the session report logged them." The row keeps its logged time beside the shifted one, for the panel.

**Where it reaches.** The shifted times are what every writer, rework and judge reads on the timeline, and what the evidence check reads under the ledger. Once the rows are lined up, no writer needs to ask the director about them; the input review is where the director sees the shift.

**If a shift is wrong,** a note at any stop corrects it for the writers and the judges (section 3). A session parsed before this phase keeps its logged times; only a new parse lines the rows up.

## 5. The article's authors, known to every stage

**Nova.** Every writer and judge is told Nova's name for the session, from the first name the director gave at the start: "Nova, the NovaNews reporter who writes this article, signs it Cassandra Nova. The director's notes may call Nova by that first name." Only on site can a photo show Nova. In an on-site session, the character-IDs parse knows Nova's name too, so it never "corrects" the name to a player's, and a photo's entry marks Nova as the article's writer. Today the map writer met "Cassandra" in a photo, no first name for Nova, and the game character Cass Zhang in the canonical roster, and raised her as missing from the roster. A caption names Nova by that name, as 100426's "Ashe Motoko and Cassandra Nova compare notes" did.

**The guest reporter, when the session has one.** The meeting's writer, the map's writer and the judges are told who the guest reporter is and that they share the byline. Today only the article's byline line knows. On the timeline, a memory turned in under the guest reporter's name is marked as theirs: "named: Taylor (the guest reporter, Taylor Chase)". A session with no guest reporter prints no such line.

How the article writes about its authors is phase 15's (section 6).

## 6. The article's authors are in the story

Where Nova stood depends on the session's reporting mode, and the mode block says which.
- **On site, Nova is a character in the room, working it for the story.** Nova makes and offers deals, such as a byline for memories; questions people; is told secrets; is asked for theories; and appears in photos. On 100426 the director's notes name Nova 27 times: "Ashe made a deal with Nova", "Nova offered Taylor a chance to screw Ashe over", "Nova asked if he thought one of them did it".
  - The article tells Nova's own part in the first person where it carries the story: "Ashe Motoko had made me a deal", "I also tried to work with Taylor on this article".
  - What Nova said in the room is told in prose ("I asked Sam whether…"), never as a quote. What Nova saw and heard is Nova's own witness.
  - The rules today say Nova "is never one of the room", and the on-site block lets Nova's "we" take in the room "for being there and for nothing more". Both change.
- **Remote, Nova was outside the warehouse and took no part in the room.** The players had no dealings with Nova beyond turning memories in. The room's events reached Nova as reports, told as scenes with attribution where it matters, as today's remote block says. Nova is never in a photo. The notes of the remote sessions 092026, 100226 and 100326 never mention Nova.
- **In both modes, Nova takes no side in the verdict.** Nova never votes, joins the room's accusation or exposes a memory. "Uninterested" is about the verdict, not the story: the police deal made Nova a neutral monitor of what the room decides, and Nova works for the story.
- **A guest reporter, when the session has one,** is in the room and shares the byline for what they contributed, in either mode. Many sessions have none, remote ones included. A memory they turned in under their name is their reporting for this article, and the article credits it that way, never as a conflict to disclose or a name on a board. How they earned the byline, such as a deal with Nova on site, is part of the story where it carries it.
- **Nova's motive.** C12 allows "one honest line about Nova's own motive"; it produced 100426's "I should declare an interest here", which the director cut. It goes.

## 7. Nova holds only what exists in the world

**Character sheets.** Six of the players' character sheets (Jamie, Flip, Morgan, Riley, Vic and Alex) have been in the writers' record in every fall session since 092626 (five on 092626, all six since). They arrive as paper documents like any other, because the paper-evidence stop starts with every item ticked. In the world they are each player's private instructions.
- **What the writers may use:** a sheet's backstory, what happened before the party and who people are to each other, reported as evidence in Nova's words, in the third person. "There's evidence he met Vic 'at 8 for an emergency strategy meeting'" stands.
- **What never appears:** the sheet itself ("his file", "her brief"), its suspected motive, its goals and its instructions to the player, and no card prints a sheet.
- **Code:** the record marks each sheet as a player's character sheet, and drops its SUSPECTED MOTIVE and WHERE TO START blocks before any writer or judge sees it. The goals also sit inside CORE IDENTITY ("convince his boss Vic to work with you instead"), which code cannot separate from the backstory, so the rule covers them.

**What Nova knows.** The rules that say the record holds what Nova can know, and that Nova states what a document backs, take the sheets' exception.

## 8. The game's rules stay off the page

The players know how the game works; they played it. The mechanics stay in the rules as what Nova knows, never as something to explain to the players.
- **Where the record stops,** Nova reasons inside the world: wonders, suspects, asks, or offers an opinion. C4's allowance of "one plain line" saying so goes; it is where "The ledger doesn't say who sold" came from.
- **Account names** (the director's ruling). Nova may wonder about the character an account is named after, or read the name as someone trying to frame them, as opinion or a question, grounded where it can be in what the room saw. Nova never states as fact that the character sold, unless the director saw the sale or it happened in front of the room.
  - May print: "Vic1 may or may not be an account belonging to Vic Kingsley, but it does make one wonder."
  - May print: "By all accounts, Kai was nowhere near Blake at 9:07, so this account is likely a ruse to try and frame her."
  - May not print: "Vic sold three memories into his own account, Vic1." (stated as fact)
  - May not print: "Sellers name their own accounts, so Vic1 says nothing about Vic." (the rulebook)
  - Why: a name is never proof, so stating it as fact could be wrong, and NovaNews answers for it. But the players know the game of naming accounts, and a reporter wondering aloud is part of the pleasure of reading it.
- **Every other mechanic** follows the same line: the ledger never shows which memory was sold, the buyer is never named, an exposure is anonymous unless the evidence log or the director names who turned it in. The writer holds to each, and never prints it as an explanation.

## 9. Nova commits to a theory

- **Opinion** joins suspicion, allegation and question as a way Nova writes what the record cannot prove: "it is the opinion of this reporter that the NeurAI board of directors had to have been involved." It is labelled as Nova's, so NovaNews never states it as fact.
- **The theory may reach past the room,** toward where the director's epilogue takes the world: NeurAI, its board, the technology, the people who gain. It is never stated as fact, and it never grades the room's verdict, which stays the official story the room negotiated.
- **No forced forms.** Each rule says what a move does and when it fits, never a form to fill. The closing lands where the article's story leads. Ending with the investigation still running is common, never required. C14's "what Nova is chasing next" and C2's "what is still open often belongs in the closing" are rewritten to say so.
- **The players see their choices as their characters would.** A player's choice is told in their character's terms, such as Taylor sourcing memories as a journalist. A player's suspicion is a lead the article may follow, not "their belief alone".

## 10. The time of day

- **The world** (the director's ruling). Marcus died around 4 AM, and the investigation cannot begin before 5 AM. A session that starts at 5:00 PM or later runs in the morning in the story; an earlier one runs in the afternoon, at the time it was played.
- **Code says which.** The session clock already decides it. Code writes one line for every writer and judge: "The investigation ran this morning, from 9:20 to 10:23 AM." or "The investigation ran this afternoon, from 3:50 to 5:18 PM."
- **Every label that fixes the morning** says "the investigation" instead: the world, T7, the timeline's name and its introduction, the money summary's "paid out this morning", the Sources line, and the judges' questions. "Tonight" stays Nova's evening, after either.

## 11. The judges and the fact check

- **The judges** read the same rules, so their questions follow them: Nova's place (no longer "from outside its choices"), the account-name clause, the time-of-day wording, opinion as one of Nova's forms, and the director's notes among their words (section 3).
- **The fact check's** message on a vote or an exposure in Nova's mouth follows the new wording of Nova's place; its prefix does not change.
- **No new checks.** Code does not look for a sheet's text in print, a rulebook line or "this morning" in an afternoon session; the rules and the code-built lines come first, and the next session shows whether they are enough. The existing card checks refuse a character sheet as a card's document, which is the one place code can hold the rule outright.

## 12. The standing step after each session

After each session's readout, the director's desk edits are read for what they do in the world: who wrote the line, what Nova could hold, what the player reading it knows, where the epilogue takes the world. The reasons go to the director before any rule text is proposed, and a pattern is checked against every place, including where the director kept the words.

## Decisions (2026-10-09)

- Every note the director sends at a stop counts as their words for every later writer, the judges, the fact check and the evidence check. What a note says happened or rules is record; a direction it gives is written as Nova's reading and never flagged for being followed.

Remote, Nova took no part in the room: no dealings beyond the memories turned in, and never in a photo. In both, Nova takes no side in the verdict.

- Nova may wonder about an account's namesake, or read the name as a frame, as opinion or a question; never as fact unless the director saw the sale or it happened in the open.
