# The story meeting and the map

Phase 4 of the roadmap (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-22-roadmap.md`). The design was settled with the director on 2026-10-02, in the design session that followed session 0926262, the first session run on phase 3. The phase takes in phases 6 (the article stop's hands) and 8 (the thesis at the arc stop). It replaces the targets for the arc, outline and article stops in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-22-director-console-design.md`. The vocabulary is `CONTEXT.md`'s; this phase added story meeting, weave, main thread, connection, reweave and photo description, and gave beat and story map their present meanings.

## 1. What the phase is for

The director settles the story before anything is planned, steers the plan in minutes, and finishes the article at their own desk.

- Each decision stop shows a decision, not a document: what is being decided, readable in a few minutes. Everything else is folded away or gone.
- What the director leaves at a stop is exactly what the next writer works from. The director edits the brief itself. A note is never appended to someone else's document as "emphasis".
- The article writer writes all the prose. It is the only writer that reads the voice guidance.
- Selection happens on the map, where the director can see it, and length follows from it.
- The article stop is the director's desk. The machine's work is done before the director opens it.

## 2. Why: session 0926262

Session 0926262 ran cleanly on phase 3. Every call completed on its pinned model, the automatic reworks kept 97 to 100% of their input word for word, and the judges held the truth floor. The story was wrong, and the director replaced it at the outline stop. The director's verdict on the published article: "too long and meandering, trying to squeeze in all the random notes and pieces of data instead of crafting a compelling article with a strong structure, resulting in lots of nonsense asides and attempts to make connections that didn't exist". The call log shows why.

- **The story was in the director's notes, and no stage had a place for it.** The notes of all four fall sessions end with the director's own read of the session. On 0926262 that read named the frame against Jess.
  - The arc writer had no field for a story and filed "the alleged frame job" as a caveat.
  - The director's arc note reached the outline writer labelled "emphasis", at the end of a 175,000-character prompt. It called the Jess Kane account "a supporting arc", and the writer kept it as one.
  - The outline writer chose the house story ("who writes the official story"), which the director replaced at the outline stop. The director's story landed the third time they gave it.
- **The weave was made twice and never shown.** The interweaving call wrote links between the threads, which the arc cards hid. The outline writer then planned the weave again in a 2,200-word script. False links started there: two of the six errors the arc judge caught were the same false cause, the scoreboard costing Alex a vote.
- **The outline was a script.** At about 6,000 words it was four times the article. 38% of the draft's five-word phrases came straight from it (56% in the lede), written by a call that never reads the voice guidance. The outline gave THE STORY 580 words and scripted it in 2,200. The draft's STORY came in at 898 words, and the whole draft at 2,356 against about 1,500.
- **Everything pushed toward coverage.** Every player needed a role in each thread's write-up, every photo a beat, every debated theory a mention. Nothing rewarded selection.
- **Walls of text.** The director read about 4,300 words at the arc stop, about 6,000 at the outline stop and 2,400 at the article stop, at night after a show.
- **Judging the unsettled.** Judges took about 70 of the session's 150 minutes of model time.
  - In the outline's first round, 27 of 39 minutes went to judging and reworking an outline the director then sent back. Its 21 notes were all about single lines.
  - The article judge found nothing to fix in the first draft. The director then sent the article back only to move three photos. After that send-back the judge flagged two of the director's own edits, and the automatic rework changed them.

## 3. How the director works

The director works the pipeline in bursts between other things, and each stop waits until they come back. What costs them is each return and what it asks of them.
- The decision stops, the story meeting and the map, take minutes.
- The article stop is the director's desk, where they finish the copy with full control.
- A notification when a stop opens is not needed for now.

## 4. The story meeting (the arc stop)

### 4.1 What feeds it

The arc writer reads what it reads today: the record and the morning timeline, the director's notes and corrections, the director's accusation word for word beside its parse, the whiteboard reading, the roster with pronouns, and the rule set. When the notes end with the director's own read of the session, the story starts from it (C1). Everything else supplies the threads, the receipts and what complicates the story.

### 4.2 What it writes: the weave

One call writes the weave:
- **The story:** the thesis, in one to three sentences.
- **The question it carries** through the article.
- **A working headline.**
- **From your notes:** the director's own words the story rests on, quoted exactly, when the story starts from the director's read.
- **The threads**, one line each: its claim, its role and its strongest receipt (a named document, or the ledger). The roles are:
  - main thread;
  - grounds it;
  - complicates it;
  - mirrors it;
  - carries it forward;
  - left out, with a one-line reason.

  Every thread the writer found stays on the page.
- **The connections:** where two threads touch, named exactly: a shared person, a moment, a document or a line. A cause counts as a connection only when the record shows it.
- **Where they converge:** the point near the end where the threads meet and the story lands.
- **A stronger main thread**, optionally: one line, when the writer thinks another thread would carry a stronger story, with the reason.
- **Questions**, only those whose answer changes what prints. These are C15's three cases: a player the record says nothing about, a missing pronoun, a ledger entry that looks wrong. Each question says what its answer changes.

The arc writer no longer writes a long write-up for each thread: no role for every player, caveat lists, notes for each lens, emotional hooks, bridges or callback seeds. Since phase 2 every later writer reads the record itself, so the write-ups repeated what the next writer already had. And every rule that added a field to them added it to the director's screen; that is how the arc stop drifted from the story memo it was designed to be. The lens analysis (C16) happens in the writer's reasoning, not in its output.

### 4.3 What the director sees

A page of about 400 words, in this order:
1. the verdict, printed by code from the parse;
2. the story, the question and the working headline;
3. from your notes;
4. the threads, with their roles and receipts;
5. the connections, and where they converge;
6. the optional stronger main thread;
7. the questions, each with its answer box.

Nothing else is on the page.

A worked example, built from 0926262's material:

> **The verdict:** Alex Reeves, 5 to 4 over Jess Kane (first round: Alex 6, Jess 4, Vic 0).
>
> **The story:** Someone built a case to put Marcus's death on Jess Kane. It failed in the room and went off in the hands of Alex Reeves, and the people with the money are already writing the next version.
> **The question carried through:** will the verdict cost Alex anything?
> **Working headline:** Alex Reeves Pointed the Room at Jess Kane. It Named Him Instead.
> **From your notes:** "the alleged frame job this morning did not work as well as intended".
>
> **The threads**
> - **Main thread · The case against Jess:** a jilted lover, a baby and Marcus's money. It held her at four votes, and the room named Alex.
> - **Grounds it · The JessKane account:** $3,235,000 in the last two minutes, held up by Alex as his last evidence. *Receipt: the ledger.*
> - **Complicates it · Two cards, one baby:** Jess and Sarah, allies in the bathroom; then Sarah pointed the room at "the baby mama". *Receipt: Jess's memory.*
> - **Carries it forward · "He's out. You're in.":** the plan to replace Marcus, which the verdict may not stop. *Receipts: Vic's memory; Alex's cease-and-desist.*
> - **Mirrors it · Reality is negotiable:** in January, Taylor made Ashe's story disappear. *Receipt: Taylor's email.*
>
> **Where they touch**
> - Sarah: Jess's ally, witness to Alex's fight, the voice that named "the baby mama", now tipped as NeurAI's interim CEO.
> - The scoreboard: the money thread enters the vote, and the room still names Alex.
> - Patchwork: the firm that demanded Marcus's removal for Alex in January is the firm NeurAI is offering to pay to defend him.
> - Taylor's sign-off, "Reality is negotiable": planted early, paid off at the end.
>
> **Where they converge:** near the end, the retainer, the CTO's chair and Alex abroad meet Taylor's line: the room negotiated one story this morning, and the people with money are negotiating the next.
>
> **Question:** the room heard JessKane held "more than double the second"; the ledger has $3,235,000 against Banana's $2,505,000. Print it as an exaggeration made in the room? *Changes the money section's key line.* [answer box]

### 4.4 What the director does

- **Edit** the story, the question, the headline and the convergence in place. The director's words are final, and they are what the map writer reads.
- **Change a thread's role** from the list: bring a left-out thread in, or make another thread the main thread. The director's 0926262 arc note was two such choices: the account "grounds it", and Reality is negotiable "mirrors it".
- **Add a thread** the writer missed, in one line, with a role.
- **Strike a connection** that isn't real, or add one in the note.
- **Answer each question** in its own box. The answer travels with its question to every later writer, as the director's words, so no later writer asks it again.
- **Leave a note** for anything else. It stands for every later writer, as it does today.
- **Approve.** The weave goes to the map writer with the director's changes. The map writer fits any changed role, added thread or new main thread in, and the map shows what it changed in the weave, marked.
- **Reweave**, when the director would rather see a change first, such as a new main thread. The writer fits the change in, keeps everything the director did not touch, and reopens the meeting with the changes marked. About 10 minutes, with the fact check.
- **Send back** with a note when the director wants the writer to rethink the weave.

### 4.5 Checks

- **Code checks**, free:
  - every named document exists in the record;
  - the room's verdict is one of the threads, as today's accusation-arc check requires;
  - the page is within its length.

  A failed check sends the weave back for one automatic rework.
- **One fact-check pass.** A model judge scores phase 3's truth criteria for the arcs and nothing else. When it finds a breach, one automatic fix runs, and the meeting opens without a second pass. No notes on the writing and no score reach the page.
- **No player coverage here.** The rule that every player appears leaves this stage; it is checked on the map (section 5.4).

### 4.6 Cost

About 400 words to read. About 15 minutes of model time: the arc writer about 8, the fact-check pass about 6, and a fix when one is needed. On 0926262 the arc stage took 38 minutes.

## 5. The map (the outline stop)

### 5.1 What feeds it

The map writer is today's outline writer. It reads:
1. the settled weave first, as its task, with the director's answers and notes;
2. then what the outline writer reads today: the record and the morning timeline, the director's notes and accusation, the photos with the director's descriptions, the roster with pronouns, and the rule set.

Its job is to lay the weave across the article's sections. It may not change the story. If it finds the record cannot carry part of the story, it says so in one line at the top of the map.

### 5.2 What it contains

- The headline and the deck.
- The settled story, printed by code from the meeting. It cannot be edited on the map.
- The sections, in order: the article's six slots, as since phase 3. The map gives each slot it uses a heading, and drops a slot the story does not need. Each section has:
  - its **job**: one sentence on what the section does to the question;
  - its **beats**: scenes, receipts, lines and figures, each named by its material (the document; the speaker and the line; the ledger entry). A beat that is a card says so. A connection from the meeting that lands in the section is named where it lands;
  - its **photos**, each beside the beat it sits with.
- The dropped sections, each with a one-line reason.
- **Everyone:** where each roster player appears, in a line code builds from the beats.
- The cards, and the expected total length.
- **Left out**, folded: the material the writer considered and did not use, one line each.
- When the director approved the meeting with changes: what the map writer changed in the weave to fit them, marked.

Length comes from what is on the map. There is no word budget per section, because a budget encourages arbitrary copy edits instead of structural fixes. If a map runs long, the fix is to strike a beat. The whole map runs to about 450 words.

### 5.3 What the director does

- Edit any line: a job, a beat, the headline, the deck.
- Move a beat or a photo to another section, or strike a beat. A struck beat joins Left out.
- Bring anything back from Left out into a section the director picks. On 0926262 the director's added line "It seems Marcus had a thing for Valets" would have started here, from Jess's acting-gig memory.
- Leave a note, then approve, or send the map back for a rework.
- To change the story itself, go back to the meeting.

The article writer writes from the map as the director leaves it.

### 5.4 Checks

No model judge reads the map. A map holds no prose, and on 0926262 the outline judge's notes were about single lines of a script. Code checks, free:
- every roster player appears in a beat (C7), some of them as supporting players;
- every kept photo is placed once (T13);
- the cards exist in the record and number three to five (C9);
- every connection from the meeting lands in a section.

A failed check sends the map back for one automatic rework, with the check's line.

### 5.5 Cost

About 450 words to read. The map writer takes about 5 to 6 minutes. On 0926262 the outline's first round took 39 minutes, and the director's send-back round another 24.

## 6. The article

### 6.1 The writer

The article writer reads the settled weave first, then the map as approved, then what it reads today. It writes all the prose from the beats: the words, the order of the beats within a section, the transitions, and each scene's detail from the record. It uses every beat. It adds no beats and no connections between threads. It uses the map's headline and deck, and aims at about 1,500 words (C4).

### 6.2 The judge

The article judge fixes errors of fact in the writer's draft before the director sees it: phase 3's truth criteria, must-fix, within the automated budget of two automatic reworks per round. Its writing criteria and notes go. It never sends a line the director wrote back for a rework (section 7). The fact check's code checks stay as they are. Its advisory flags (an em-dash, a production word, a gendered pronoun for Nova, length over 1,800 words, the head count) are marked beside their paragraph instead of listed.

### 6.3 The desk

- The article as it will print, with every editor visible: no edit button hidden until hover.
- Edit any text in place, including the headline, the deck and the captions.
- Move, delete or insert any block. Paragraphs and quotes can be inserted; photos and cards can be moved or deleted. An empty block is caught before the director approves or sends back.
- A fact the judge could not fix within its budget, and a code check's flag, are marked beside their paragraph. Nothing else sits in front of the article: no score, and no notes on the writing. What the automatic passes did stays folded below the article.
- Approve publishes exactly what is on the desk.
- Send back with a note for a structural rework of the whole article. Sending back one section is phase 11.

On 0926262 the director sent the article back only to move three photos. With the desk, that send-back does not happen, and neither does the judge pass and rework it set off against the director's copy.

## 7. The director's edits are final

An edit is the final word on its text (`CONTEXT.md`). This goes in before the phase (section 9):
- An automatic pass never changes a line the director wrote. A judge that disagrees with one lists it for the director beside the line; it never makes it a must-fix.
- A send-back the director writes may change one of their edits only where the structural change the note asks for means the edit no longer fits. The stop then lists each edit that changed, and why.

The same holds for the director's edits at the meeting and on the map.

## 8. Photos

- **A leave-out box.** The photos stop gets a "leave this photo out" box on each photo. It writes the exclusion straight into the photo mapping. Today an exclusion happens only if the character-ID parse reads one out of the director's free text. The writers' photo lists honour an exclusion since phase 3.
- **Photos outside the story.** A photo whose moment is not in the story sits beside the section where its people appear, captioned with the director's description (T13). No paragraph is written to justify it.
- **The count.** How many photos appear stays the director's call. On 0926262, eleven photos in about 1,400 words would be one every 130 words.

## 9. Before the phase

Three small fixes go in before phase 4, on the director's go-ahead of 2026-10-02:
- The director's edits are final (section 7).
- Photos are resized when the article is published: 1600 px on the long edge, JPEG quality 85, sRGB, no metadata. These are the settings used by hand for 0926262, which took its photos from 87.8 MB to 2.2 MB. The publish step copies full-size originals today.
- Never two photos in a row. Assembly moves the second photo below the next paragraph.

## 10. What goes

- The interweaving call, its plan, and the arc rework's duty to keep the plan.
- The long write-up for each thread: roles for every player, caveat lists, lens notes, emotional hooks, bridges and callback seeds.
- The every-player rule at the arc stage.
- The outline's scripted prose, its paragraph counts, and the outline judge.
- The judges' writing notes and scores, at every stop.
- The writers' questions at the outline and article stops.
- The arc stage's second and third judge passes.

## 12. Rule text to change

The rule set changes where it names a stage's job. The new text comes to the director to read before the build, as phase 3's did.
- **C1** says the outline names the thesis. The story meeting settles it.
- **C16** says the arc writer and the interweaving call find the threads and where they can meet, and the outline decides how the thesis lands. Instead, the arc writer weaves the threads at the meeting, and the map lays the weave across the sections.
- **C2:** the map decides which sections exist.
- **C4:** length comes from what is on the map, with no budget per section.
- **C7:** every player appears, some as supporting players, and the map places each.
- **C8:** the arc writer weighs the director's lines; the map places what the weave uses and lists the rest as left out.
- **C15:** questions only at the story meeting, each saying what its answer changes, answered in its own box.
- **Section 8 of the rule-set spec** (who reads what) becomes section 11 here.

## 13. Cost and the test

- **Reading:** about 2,400 words per session (meeting 400, map 450, article 1,500), instead of about 13,500.
- **Model time on the story:** roughly 30 to 45 minutes before any send-back, instead of about two hours on 0926262 with its send-backs.
- **The test** is the director's next session. Its readout counts what the pipeline asked of the director:
  - how many times it called them back;
  - how much they read at each return;
  - what they changed at the article stop, moving and cutting blocks against rewording;
  - whether the story changed after the meeting.

  All of it comes from the saved versions and the call log.
- **Prose:** nothing new in this phase. After the next session, the writing guidance is refined from the director's edits (rule-set spec, R20).

## 14. Out of scope

- The phases after 4 on the roadmap: 3b, 13, 5, 9, 12 and 11. That includes editing on the rendered page (12) and sending back one section (11).
- A list of every claim checked against the record. It was dropped with phase 7, and comes back if errors of fact reach the director.
- A notification when a stop opens.

## 15. For the plan

Where the design meets the code. These are integration points for the planner to read before writing briefs, not decisions.

- **The weave's shape** replaces the arc writer's schemas (`CORE_ARC_SCHEMA`, `PLAYER_FOCUS_GUIDED_SCHEMA` in `lib/sdk-client/subagents.js`) and the arc rework's addendum. The arc stop's approval payload (`selectedArcs` and `outlineGuidance` in `buildResumePayload`) becomes the weave as the director left it.
- **The interweaving call** goes: `enrichWithInterweaving`, `INTERWEAVING_PRINCIPLES`, `_arcAnalysisCache.interweavingPlan` and `hasInterweavingPlan`, with their tests and their place in `scripts/lib/render-calls.js`.
- **`validateArcStructure`** loses the roster-coverage check and gains the length check. The arc judge keeps only its truth criteria, and the arcs' automated budget drops to one fix.
- **The map's shape** replaces the fields inside `outline.schema.json`'s six slots. `outline-edit-logic.js`, its shape validator and the server's schema gate follow. The outline evaluation leaves the graph, with its revision cap and its rollback entries.
- **The article writer and its reworker** read the weave and the map where they read the outline today. The thesis echo at the article stop (`outlineThesis`) becomes the settled story.
- **The article judge's** criteria shrink to the truth groups, and its prompt drops the craft files. The director's edited lines are exempt from its must-fix list (section 7).
- **The console:**
  - `ArcSelection.js` and `arcCardModel` become the meeting;
  - `Outline.js` becomes the map;
  - `Article.js` gains move, delete and insert, with the empty-block check, and loses the evaluation bar's score and notes;
  - `CharacterIds.js` gains the leave-out box.
- **The e2e harness's step mode, `scripts/render-prompts.js`, `CLAUDE.md` and the deep dive** follow.
- **Threads from before phase 4.** A thread paused at an arc, outline or article stop holds the old shapes. The plan decides whether such a thread finishes on the old path or must be started over.
- **The writers' questions** (`lib/writer-questions.js`) remain only at the arc stage, with each answer attached to its question.
- **The publish step** (`template-nodes.js`, which copies the photos) and assembly (`TemplateAssembler`) carry the two photo fixes in section 9.

## Decisions (2026-10-02, the design session)

- The arc stop becomes the story meeting, and the arc writer writes it in the same call.
- The meeting shows one weave in full, with every thread and its role. It shows no alternative stories, only a one-line note when the writer sees a stronger main thread.
- The director changes the weave through roles, added threads and struck connections. Approving fits the changes in at the map; Reweave shows them first.
- The writers' questions are asked only at the meeting, each in its own answer box.
- One fact-check pass runs before the meeting, with one fix and no notes on the writing.
- Every player appears by the time the article is written, some as supporting players. This is checked on the map.
- The map is as in section 5, with no budget per section and no model judge.
- The article writer writes all the prose from the beats, and adds no beats and no connections between threads.
- The article stop is the director's desk. Move, delete and insert come in this phase.
- The director's edits are final, except where a send-back's structural change means one no longer fits.
- Photos get a leave-out box. Photos outside the story are placed by their people. Photos are resized at publish, and never appear two in a row.
- The next session is measured by what the pipeline asked of the director. The director's time per stop is not measured.
- Withdrawn in the session: extra checks on the article beyond facts; a word budget per section; a dashboard at the article stop; the writer's answers to notes; notifications for now.
