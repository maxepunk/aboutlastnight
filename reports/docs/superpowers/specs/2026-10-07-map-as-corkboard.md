# The map as a corkboard

This is piece 4 of phase 4b in the roadmap (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-22-roadmap.md`). The design was settled with the director on 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07, on mock-ups of session 100326's map. It does four things:
- it changes the map's page;
- it adds a summary to each move;
- it makes the order of the moves on the map the order the article tells them;
- it counts the page twice: as it opens, and with every summary open.

It supersedes:
- the phase 4 spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-story-meeting-and-map.md`) on the map's page, sections 5.2 and 5.3, where piece 1 left them standing;
- piece 1's spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-story-level-and-evidence.md`) on the map's page, section 4.2, whose bound of 450 words now counts the page as it opens, and the map's part of section 9;
- piece 3's spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-meeting-as-angles.md`), section 12.

What piece 1 says about a move, its evidence and the story terms stands, and so does what piece 3 says about the settled story. The vocabulary is `CONTEXT.md`'s. A move is a beat; this piece gives it a summary.

## 1. What piece 4 is for

The director sees the article's shape at a glance and arranges it by hand.
- The sections stand side by side. Each move is a card, in the order the article tells them.
- A move reads by its title. What the article says there is one click away, in its summary, and what supports it is one more.
- The director can follow a thread across the sections, and places a move by moving its card.
- The page as it opens is at most 450 words, and at most 750 with every summary open.

## 2. Why: session 100326

On 100326, the first session on piece 1, the map's page came to 535 words. The director found it "difficult to follow", and put that largely down to the page's design. The page was one long column: the headline and the deck, then each section's heading, job, moves and photos, then Everyone by section, the counts and the gap note.
- The sections ran one under another, so the article's shape, and which section carried how much, never showed at once.
- The threads were not on the page. Piece 1 took the ids off the moves and nothing replaced them, so the director could not see how the angle ran through the sections.
- Every photo printed its description in full. On 100326 that was 99 words, repeating what the director had written at the character-IDs stop.

On 2026-10-06 the director accepted corkboard columns, mocked up from 100326, with a title and a summary on each move's card. On 2026-10-07 they set the length:
- the summaries are folded when the page opens, and do not count toward the 450;
- with every summary open, the page stays within 700 to 750 words.

As first drawn, the mock-up opened at 716 words. It was re-cut, showing the threads as dots and the photos' descriptions on hover, and the director accepted that version: it opens at 401 words, and comes to 708 with every summary open.

## 3. A move and its summary

A move is a beat as piece 1 defines it: a few words in story terms, the people in it, the threads it carries, and its evidence underneath. Its words are its **title** on the page. Piece 4 gives it a **summary**: one sentence, in story terms, saying what the article tells at that move.

> **Morgan erases her name; three accusers undo it.**
> With minutes left, Morgan wipes her own name off the suspect board, and Sarah, Remi and Riley put it back.

- The map writer writes a summary for every move, about 20 words.
- The article writer tells each move as its summary says, from the move's evidence.
- The director can rewrite a summary like any other line.
- A move the director adds needs only a title and its people. Its summary is the director's to write or leave empty.
- A summary is in story terms, as piece 1 sets them out: no quotations, figures, times or document ids.

**The order of the moves is the map's.** Within each section, the article tells the moves in the order of their cards. Today the article writer chooses that order (C16); piece 4 hands it to the map, where the director can see it and change it.

## 4. What the director reads

The page reads top to bottom, as the mock-up "Map: corkboard columns" on the design canvas shows it:
1. **The round's lines,** when there are any, as now: the round and its note after a send-back, a check still failing, an edit of the director's that a rework changed, or that the director's edits stand.
2. **The counts:**
   - whether everyone is placed, and who is not;
   - the cards, with the range they print in when they leave it;
   - the photos placed, of those kept;
   - the expected length.
3. **The settled story:** its story and its question, from the angle the director sent, with the way back to the meeting. Beside it, the writer's gap note and its people, when there is one.
4. **The threads:** the settled story's threads in its order, each a coloured dot and its name.
5. **The top of the article:** the top photo, the headline and the deck.
6. **The board:** one column per section, in the article's order. Each column is headed by its slot, its heading (or that none prints) and its job. Its moves follow as cards, in order, and its photos by themselves sit at the foot.
7. **Left out:** a tray under the board, each move by its title and a dot for its thread.
8. **The dropped sections,** each with its reason, and what the map changed to fit the meeting, as now.
9. **The note box,** with the standing notes folded under it, Approve and Send back, and the trace of an automatic rework, folded, as now.

**A card** shows:
- the move's title and its people;
- a dot for each thread it carries;
- "Card" when its evidence prints as a card;
- its photos, as thumbnails.

Its summary is folded behind an arrow beside the title. One button above the board opens or closes every summary. The page opens with every summary folded, each time.

**A thread** picked at the top shows its line under the threads, outlines the cards that carry it, and dims the rest. Picking it again, or Show all, clears it. A dot names its thread when the director points at it.

**A photo** shows as a picture. Its description shows when the director points at it or selects it, and in place of the picture when the picture cannot load.

Nothing on the page is an id. Thread names are written out only at the top and on a selected card. The Everyone list goes: the cards carry the people, and the counts name anyone not placed.

## 5. Selecting a move

Clicking a card selects it and opens it in place, as the mock-up "Map: a move opened" shows. An open card shows:
- its title, its summary and its people;
- its threads, each by its dot and its name;
- its photos, each with the director's description;
- **the controls:**
  - Edit the words: the title, the summary and the people;
  - Move up and Move down;
  - Move to another section;
  - Put a photo beside it;
  - Leave it out, or Take it out for a move the director added at this look;
- **what's behind it,** as the fold shows it now:
  - each piece of its evidence, by its sources' names and what it shows, with a piece that cuts against the move marked so;
  - the card's document, marked "Prints as a card";
  - where its threads meet, by the connection's line, when a connection lands in the move;
  - for a move the director added with no evidence, that the article writer finds it, as now.

Clicking the card again, pressing Escape, or selecting something else closes it. One thing is selected at a time. While an editor is open, selecting something else waits until the director saves or cancels, as at the desk, so nothing they type is dropped.

Other things open the same way when selected:
- **A move in Left out** shows Bring it back, and the section to bring it into.
- **A photo,** wherever it sits, shows its description and where it can go: beside a move, into a section by itself, or to the top of the article. A photo the director has since left out shows that it does not print, with no controls, as now.

A section's heading and job, the headline, the deck, the gap note and the expected length keep their editors, as now.

## 6. What the director does

- **Move a move.** Drag its card to another place in its column, or into another column. The photos beside it go with it. Or select it and use Move up, Move down, or Move to another section, which puts it at the foot of that section.
- **Leave a move out.** Drag it into the tray, or select it and press Leave it out. Its photos stay in their section by themselves, as now.
- **Bring one back.** Drag it from the tray into a column, or select it and bring it back into a section.
- **Add a move.** The line at the foot of each column takes a title and the people in it.
- **Rewrite a line:**
  - a move's title, summary or people;
  - a section's heading or job;
  - the headline, the deck, the gap note or the expected length.
- **Place a photo.** Select it and choose, or drag it:
  - onto a move, to put it beside that move;
  - onto a column, to stand there by itself;
  - onto the top of the article, to make it the top photo.

  Every kept photo stays placed once, as now: the top photo it replaces takes its place.
- **Follow a thread,** and open or close the summaries.
- **Write a note,** and press Approve or Send back, as now.

The counts change as the director works. Every change stays on the page until the round ends, as now. A column the director empties stays on the board until the map is sent, and then drops, as now.

Dragging is for the mouse. Every drag can also be done through the selected card's controls, for the keyboard and for a touch screen. On a narrow screen the columns stack, one section under another.

## 7. The margin and the look-through

- **The look-through** is the selected card: a move's evidence opens with it (section 5).
- **The margin.** What the round has to say about a move stays on its card, in a line under the title, whether the card is selected or not. That line can be:
  - a check still failing there;
  - a concern about one of the director's edits;
  - an edit of the director's that a rework changed.

  A line about a section sits under its head. One about the headline, the deck or the top photo sits at the top of the article, and one with no place sits among the round's lines. The rules that decide what shows, and its words, are today's.

## 8. Length

| The page | Bound | The writer's own words |
|---|---|---|
| **As it opens:** every summary folded, no thread picked, nothing selected | 450 words | Aim for 300, as now. The writer may use 300, or more while the page stays within 450. |
| **With every summary open** | 750 words | The summaries aim for about 250 between them, about 20 words for each move. The writer may use 550, or more while the page stays within 750. |

Both are counted as the page prints. Not counted:
- a thread's line;
- a photo's description;
- the evidence, and everything else folded;
- anything only a tooltip or a screen reader carries.

A failure on either count sends the map back for its one automatic rework, as now. The message says which page ran long and names its longest lines, the summaries among them for the page with every summary open. The director's own lines are never held to it.

On 100326's re-cut, the page opens at 401 words, about 250 of them the writer's. With every summary open it comes to 708, and the 16 summaries are 307 of those.

## 9. What travels

The map goes on as the director left it, as now, with each move's summary. The article writer and the article judge read it. The article writer tells each section's moves in the map's order, each as its summary says, from its evidence.

## 10. The checks

As now, and:
- each summary the writer wrote is in story terms;
- each of the writer's moves in a section carries only threads the settled story tells;
- the page's length on both counts (section 8).

A summary the director wrote, like any of their lines, is never a check's failure.

## 11. The writers

- **The map writer** writes each move's summary and orders each section's moves as the article will tell them. Its task asks for a page whose own words aim for 300 as it opens, and summaries of about 20 words each.
- **Its rework** reads and returns the summaries and the order, as it does every line.
- **The article writer** tells the moves in the map's order, each as its summary says.

## 12. Rule text to change

The director reads the new text before the build, as they read the rule text of pieces 1 and 3.
- **C2** says a section's beats are the moves of its story, each in a few plain words with its people, and that the article writer writes each beat from its evidence. Instead:
  - each beat is also one sentence saying what the article tells there;
  - the article writer tells it as that sentence says, from its evidence, in the map's order.
- **C16** gives the article writer "the order of the beats within each section". That goes: the order is the map's. The article writer keeps the words, the transitions, each scene's detail and the sidebar.
- **`CONTEXT.md`:** Beat gains its summary, and Story map says the order of its beats is the article's.

## 13. Sessions already running

A map written before piece 4 has no summaries. Its page shows each move without an arrow, and nothing else changes. A send-back's rework writes the summaries. No session is refused or rolled back.

## 14. What goes

- The map's page as one long column.
- The Everyone list by section.
- The photos' descriptions printed beside them.
- "(card)" after a move's words, replaced by the "Card" mark.
- The article writer's choice of the order within a section.
- The single count of the page with every line shown, replaced by the two counts in section 8.

## 15. Cost and the test

- **Reading:** at most 450 words as the page opens, and 750 with every summary open. On 100326's re-cut that is 401 and 708, against the 535 shown on the page that session used.
- **Model time:** the map writer writes about 250 more words, about a minute more on 100326's timings. On that session the writer took about 6 minutes and its check rework about 5.
- **The test** is the director's next session after piece 4 lands:
  - what the stops log records at the map, on both counts;
  - what the director changed there, and how long the stop stayed open;
  - whether the article tells the moves in the map's order and as their summaries say;
  - the article.

## 16. Out of scope

- Bringing back a section the writer dropped, and moving a section: a note does it.
- Remembering which summaries were open between visits.
- Editing which threads a move carries.

## 17. For the plan

This section is where the design meets the code. The footprint survey, kept locally at `.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-07-phase-4b-map-page/surveys/piece-4-footprint.md`, lists every place by file and line at `c06b354e`. These are integration points and the integrator's rulings, not decisions for the director.

- **The field.**
  - The summary is a beat's `synopsis`, Scrivener's name for a corkboard card's text. `summary` already names two other things: the sidebar's printed line, and the record's paraphrase that the evidence and card checks refuse to read as a source (`lib/evidence.js`). The article writer's prompt holds both.
  - The field goes in both copies of the beat shape in `outline.schema.json` (sections and `leftOut`, held equal by a test). It is required in the writer's schema. It is optional in the director's: `directorMapSchemaFor` keeps `required: ['id', 'move']`.
  - The map writer has no OUTPUT FORMAT block; it reads `<SCHEMA>`. So the schema's description of the field, with `mapTask`, is how the writer learns it.
  - Every place that reads a beat's fields from a fixed list takes the field (survey A):
    - `mapWritersTextBlank`, `writersLinesInPrint` and the story-terms check in `mapFindings`;
    - `BEAT_KEYS`, `validateBeat`, `initBeat` and `buildBeat`. The add line stays `{id, move, players}`;
    - `mapParts`, so a sentence the director cut from a summary that comes back is flagged;
    - `STORY_MAP_LABEL`;
    - `addBeat` in `lib/stop-pages.js`, and `beatView`;
    - the skill's `schemas.md`.
  - `mapBeatText`, a whole beat on the report, stays the title and the people. The tests that list a beat's keys move with the field (survey A, "Tests that pin").
- **The order** (the riskiest part).
  - Today `moveBeat` appends to the end of a section, and the map's diff ignores order within a section (`hand-edit-diff.js` `mapElementEdits`).
  - `moveBeat` and `bringBackBeat` take a place. Move up and Move down are moves to a place.
  - The director's order in a section is their edit. It stands while the section holds those beats in that order, and code puts it back after an automatic pass that changes it, as it puts back every edit. The report reads it in the page's words, as the order of that section.
  - The plan chooses between one order edit per section (the director's order of its beat ids) and the desk's move within a section (`between` and `inOrder`, task 4.3b). Map beats have ids, which the desk's blocks lack, so the per-section edit is the likely fit.
  - `ARTICLE_TASK`'s line "the order of the beats within a section ... are yours" changes with C16.
- **The legend.**
  - `mapCheckpointData` sends the settled story's threads in its order, each `{id, name, line}`, and its connections, each `{id, joins, line}`, built from `settledAngleOf`. A thread that a move in Left out carries outside the story is named from the weave's left-out threads.
  - `Outline.js` reads these only through `mapView` (`console-edit-gates.test.js`).
  - `mapView` gives each beat its dots, each `{color, name}`, and never an id. The colours come from a fixed palette, by the thread's place in the angle, in the console's light and dark tokens.
  - The new check, that a writer's move in a section carries only the story's threads, joins `mapFindings`. Today nothing checks it (survey D).
- **The page in code.**
  - `mapView` gains an `order`, as `meetingView` has, which `Outline.js` and `lib/stop-pages.js` both follow.
  - A photo prints no words on the page. Its line in `lib/stop-pages.js` is folded (its description, for the harness's print). The console shows the thumbnail, with the description in its `title` and on selection.
  - The summaries and the thread lines are folded lines.
  - `stopPage` and `wordsShown` take an option that opens the summaries. The map's two pages differ by a fold, not by a value in the payload as the meeting's angles do (survey B).
  - The per-card toggles and the board's toggle are controlled state in `Outline.js`, not `CollapsibleSection`, which reads `defaultOpen` once. The tray is always open and the evidence shows on the selected card, so `Outline.js` keeps two `CollapsibleSection`s, the standing notes and the trace, and `console-stop-pages.test.js`'s count of four follows (amended with the plan).
- **The length.**
  - `MAP_WORD_BOUND` (450) and `MAP_WORD_AIM` (300) stand for the page as it opens. `MAP_OPEN_WORD_BOUND` (750) and `MAP_SYNOPSIS_AIM` (250) join them. The open page's floor is their sum, 550.
  - `mapPageWords` counts both pages on the writer's share, each with its own overhead. The blanked page blanks `synopsis` too.

  - The photos' descriptions leave the overhead, which raises the writer's allowance where the photos ran long. That is wanted: the bound is on what the director reads.
- **The stops log.** The map's pause line gains `wordsOpen`, the count with every summary open. `words` stays the page as it opens. The line's keys are pinned in `stops-log.test.js`.
- **The console.**

- The map's page is a corkboard: one column per section. Each move is a card with its title, its people, its threads as dots, its card mark and its photos, and a summary folded under the title.
- As it opens, the page is at most 450 words; with every summary open, at most 750. The summaries are folded each time the page opens.
- The threads are coloured dots keyed to their names at the top. A thread's line shows when it is picked.
- A photo's description shows when the director points at the photo or selects it.
