## How the order is set

Phases are ordered by what a problem costs the director to fix by hand at the stop where it surfaces, then by dependency. A structural problem costs a whole-article rework or an hour of rewriting; a wrong sentence costs minutes but needs reading against the record; a caption costs seconds. Words the director already writes, and facts the session already holds, reaching the right prompt come before new controls, because that work is deterministic and cheap.

Since 2026-10-02 the order also counts the director's returns. The director works the pipeline in bursts between other things, and every stop waits until they come back, so a phase that spares the director a return to a stop moves up.

## Phase 2: the record and your words reach every call

**Intent.** Every call that decides, writes, reworks or judges the story sees the same record in full, and the director's own words beside their parse. A rework runs with everything its writer had. A judge sees what it judges. The director sees what the automatic passes did before reading.

**Why now.** The analysis found the fidelity running backwards: the stages that decide the story and the three judges see the least, the article writer alone sees the record whole, and the reworkers see least of all. The whole usable record of 092026 is about 68,000 characters (about 17,000 tokens), less than half the instructions the article writer already carries. It also found the director's words converted once and replaced by the conversion: the article writer saw `ACCUSATION: Marcus` for an overdose verdict, no writer saw a photo description, and corrections reached only the parse. The phase addresses root causes 1, 2, 5 and 6 of the analysis, and the reporting-mode lines of root cause 4.

**How it lands (agreed 2026-09-25).** Slice 2.0 merges to `main` first, alone, after its own gate. Slices 2.1 to 2.7 are built in two waves and merge together after one live gate. The briefs are in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-25-phase-2-the-record-and-your-words.md`. Between the two merges `main` runs the new SDK on today's prompts, so a session in that window shows Opus 5.5's effect on its own.

### 2.0 The SDK and Opus 5.5

The pipeline's `opus` calls run on Opus 4.8, because `lib/llm/client.js` pins `claude-opus-4-8`. Sonnet calls run on Sonnet 5 and Haiku calls on Haiku 4.5. On 2026-09-24 a probe sent one structured call on `claude-opus-5-5` through the wrapper. The API refused it before the model ran and used no tokens: "Claude Code 2.1.119 does not support this model; version 2.1.280 or newer is required." The installed Agent SDK is 0.2.119, and the `^0.2.119` range in `package.json` cannot reach a new enough version. Only SDK 0.3.280 to 0.3.282 bundle a new enough CLI. This slice is therefore an SDK upgrade across the 0.2 to 0.3 bump, and it changes every call, not only the Opus calls.

- **What changes.**
  - The SDK moves to 0.3.28x.
  - `@anthropic-ai/sdk` moves to 0.93 or later, which the new SDK requires as a peer. 0.81 is installed now, and no pipeline code imports it.
  - The `opus` pin moves to `claude-opus-5-5`.
  - The wrapper names a declined request (`stop_reason: "refusal"` with its category) and does not retry it. Today a decline would fail as a schema mismatch. Four nodes re-wrap SDK errors and several swallow them into state, so the rule has to survive those paths.
  - The gate's missing probes become scripts: the tool count at `init`, memory paths, readable thinking. `probe-content-bundle-channel.js` fails, not only prints, on the text fallback.
  - CLAUDE.md gets correct model pins, SDK notes and budget ceilings. The code's ceilings are $100, $50 and $10, and they only stop a runaway call, because the pipeline runs on a subscription.
- **What already holds.**
  - Opus 5.5 cannot run without thinking. Every call already sends adaptive thinking, and no call turns it off.
  - Opus 5.5 rejects forced tool use. No call forces a tool.
  - The 0.3 SDK ships the same ESM-only entry as 0.2.119, which `client.js` already loads with `require()` on Node 24.
  - The environment change in the SDK changelog (0.2.113) is already absorbed: the wrapper passes all of `process.env`.
- **Invariants.**
  - The npm change needs the director's go-ahead.
  - The director's server on port 3001 runs from the main checkout's packages. So the slice's worktree gets its own `node_modules` instead of a junction to the main checkout.
  - The main checkout's packages change only at merge, and the director restarts the server to pick them up.
- **Gate.**
  - The isolation probes on the new SDK: the tool count at `init` is 0 or 1, no memory paths load, thinking streams readable text.
  - For the article schema, structured output arrives through the SDK channel, not the text fallback.
  - A pass on a copy that makes every kind of call at least once: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus; text, structured and image. Each call's duration is recorded next to its duration on 092026.

### 2.1 One record view

- **Prompts.** One renderer produces every document the same way, with its id, kind, name, owner, disclosure layer and full text. Buried memories appear as transactions only: account, amount and time. The renderer feeds the arc writer, the interweaving call, the arc reworker, the outline writer (without the five-per-arc cap), the outline reworker, the article writer, the article reworker, and the three judges (with 2.3).
- **Plumbing.**
  - The Haiku preprocessor summarises each memory from its full text, not its name. Its summaries are used as summaries, never in place of a document.
  - The character extraction reads the documents the director selected.
  - Derived material carries a derived label wherever it reaches a writer or judge. That covers character context, contradiction notes and Haiku's photo descriptions, which are labelled today as "use for factual accuracy", "verified" and "ground truth".

### 2.2 Your words as record

- **Accusation.** The director's full text reaches the arc, outline and article writers and the judges, beside its parse. A verdict with no culprit, such as an overdose or an accident, gets its own shape instead of naming the victim.
- **Photos.** Each photo description reaches the outline and article writers, and the outline judge, word for word, with its filename and the identified names.
- **Corrections.** Input-review corrections are stored and applied to the text the writers read, not only to the parse.
- **The session report.** "Exposed By", each memory's owner and the exposure time stay in the record: in state, on disk and at the input review. They reach the writers' prompts after phase 3 rules on when an exposer may be named, because three instructions about it conflict today.
- **Whiteboard.** The parse reaches the outline and article writers as well as the arc writer.
- **Notes.** Standing notes reach the arc writer and the arc reworker. The arc stop's note survives a page reload.
- **Handoffs.** An arc rework keeps the interweaving plan.

### 2.3 Reworkers see what their writers saw

- Each stop's reworker is built from the same section builders as its writer, then the revision block (the findings, the previous version, the director's edits) and the director's guidance last. The arc reworker gains the whiteboard, the investigation focus, character context, the generation rules, the boundaries and the tensions. The outline reworker gains the arcs, the photos, the director's observations and everything else its writer reads. The article reworker gains the approved outline, the roster with pronouns, the money figures and the writer's rules.
- A send-back rework's banner names the director's round instead of "automated pass 0".

### 2.4 Judges that see what they judge

- **Prompts.** The arc, outline and article judges get the record view, the roster with pronouns, and the director's notes and accusation. The outline judge gets every photo and the real interweaving plan. The article judge also gets the fact check's result.
- **Screen.** Each stop labels the judge's score as uncalibrated. Calibration against the director's verdicts belongs to phase 7.

### 2.5 Checks and cards

- The writer is no longer asked for document text in sidebar entries, which print only a headline and a summary.
- The fact check reads only text that prints. That applies to the card check and to the roster check.
- A sentence after a closing quotation mark, and a card that swaps double quotes for single, stop failing when the words match.
- A defect shared by an inline card and its sidebar entry is reported once.
- The fix line points at the document in the prompt instead of offering "or drop the card".

### 2.6 The reporting-mode lines

- The remote block tells the writer to show its absence through attribution, and to state it at most once.
- About twenty presence and absence lines outside the block are named in the brief and changed to defer to it. They sit in the article prompt builder, the outline's investigation-observations header, four craft files, the photo-enrichment examples and the arc character categories.
- The article judge's remote rule says the same.

### 2.7 The trace

- **Plumbing.** The version before each automatic pass in the current round is kept, with the findings that triggered the pass, in one new state channel. It is cleared on a send-back and at every rollback point, like the hand-edit fields. The evaluation history does not record why a pass ran: the evaluator's reasons are overwritten by the next evaluation, and the arc check writes no history entry.
- **Screen.** A read-only panel at the outline and article stops lists each automatic pass, why it ran, and what it changed.

**Out of scope.** Every other rule conflict (phase 3). Server-filled card text (phase 5). Calibrating the judges (phase 7). The rest of the photo work (phase 9). Comments and undo on the trace (phase 10).

**Open decisions, with recommendations** (the integrator's, for the phase document).

1. **Prompt size.** Each document is rendered once per prompt, and per-arc sections refer to documents by id instead of repeating their text.
2. **How to prove it live.** One gate for 2.1 to 2.7: a copy of 092026 rolled back to input review and run to the article, with one send-back at each stop. It starts at input review rather than arc selection because 2.1 changes preprocessing and 2.2 changes the parse, and neither reruns on a rollback to arc selection. The gate reads each call's prompt for the record view, the director's words and the labels. It checks that cards survive the automatic pass and that the trace appears.
3. **Effort on Opus 5.5.** Opus 5.5 defaults to `medium`. At `xhigh` it thinks more per turn than earlier models, so its calls run longer. The wrapper passes `xhigh` to the Opus writers and evaluations, and `medium` to the director-notes enricher. Keep those levels for the first gate and record each call's duration. Lower a call's effort only if the gate shows it running much longer than on 092026 with no gain the director can see. **Decided 2026-09-26: keep `xhigh`.** At the 2.0 gate the first-pass Opus calls took 26% longer than on 092026 (about 6.6 minutes a session; the article writer 521 s against 371 s), and the enricher at `medium` took 53 s against 134 to 171 s.

**Surveys.** Done on 2026-09-24 at `b3d1a1a` and kept locally: the four phase 2 surveys in `.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-24-phase-2/surveys/`, and the upstream, judges-and-craft and objectives files in `.superpowers/analysis/2026-09-24-information-architecture/`. Line numbers there are at `b3d1a1a`.

## Phase 4: the story meeting and the map

**Intent.** The director settles the story before anything is planned, steers the plan in minutes, and finishes the article at their own desk. Each stop shows a decision, not a document, and what the director leaves at a stop is exactly what the next writer works from.

**Why now.** On 0926262 (above), the outline writer decided the story, late and without the director; the plan was a script; and the director read walls of text at the two stops where steering is cheapest.

**What changes.** The spec holds the detail.
- **The story meeting (the arc stop).** One call writes the weave:
  - the story, starting from the director's read when the notes end with one;
  - the question it carries and a working headline;
  - every thread with its role: main thread, grounds it, complicates it, mirrors it, carries it forward, or left out;
  - the connections where threads touch, and where they converge.

  It runs to about 400 words. The director edits the words, changes roles, adds a thread or strikes a connection, then approves; the outline writer fits the changes in, or a reweave shows them first. The writers' questions are asked only here, each with its own answer box.
- **The map (the outline stop).** The headline, the deck and the settled story; each section's job, beats, connections and photos; the sections dropped; who appears where; the material left out, folded. About 450 words. Length comes from what is on the map, with no budget per section. The director edits, moves, strikes or brings back any line.
- **The article.** The writer writes all the prose from the beats and adds no beats and no connections between threads. The judge fixes errors of fact in the writer's draft and never touches the director's lines. The article stop is the director's desk: edit any text; move, delete or insert any block, photos and cards included; approve to publish exactly what they finished. No score and no writing notes.
- **Photos.** A "leave this photo out" box at the photos stop. A photo outside the story sits beside its people, captioned with the director's description.

**What goes.**
- The separate interweaving call, and the long write-ups for each thread.
- The rule that every player gets a role at the arc stage. Coverage is checked on the map.
- The outline's scripted prose, and the model judge on the outline.
- The judges' writing notes at every stop.
- The writers' questions after the story meeting.

**Before phase 4** (the director's go-ahead, 2026-10-02). Push phase 3, and make three small fixes:
- The director's edits are final. An automatic pass never changes them, and a send-back changes one only where the structural change the director asked for means it no longer fits; the stop then lists which edits changed and why.
- Photos are resized at publish.
- Never two photos in a row.

**What it should cost.** About 2,400 words of reading per session instead of about 13,500, and roughly 30 to 45 minutes of model time on the story instead of about two hours.

**The test.** The director's next session, read as described under "What the live sessions showed".

**Out of scope.** Every phase after 4 in the status table. A notification when a stop opens is not needed for now.

**Result.** Built and merged to local `main` at `ab4af37d` on 2026-10-04, with the fixes before it. Session 100226 showed the story meeting and the map written at the level of citations and built as forms (above). Phase 4b redoes them.

## Phase 4b: the stops at the story level

**Intent.** The director gives input only at the level of the story. The story meeting and the map show the story and its structure in plain terms, short enough to read and change in minutes. The evidence behind every line travels underneath, for the writers and the fact check. And the pipeline answers each change the director makes.

**Why now.** On 100226 (above), the first session on phase 4, the two stops where steering is cheapest did not work. They were written at the level of citations and built as forms, so the director read more planning than article and worked through menus.

**The distinction it rests on.** A thread is a line of the story: an idea about what happened or what it means. The writers tell it through several pieces of evidence, and some threads only appear when sources are set side by side, such as the ledger's times against the time a memory surfaced. A piece of evidence is what gets cited: a memory, an email, a ledger line, something said in the room. The director works with the threads. Choosing and citing the evidence is the writers' job, held to the record by the fact check.

**What changes.** Six pieces, each with its own spec, which the director reads before its plan.
1. **The story level, with the evidence underneath.** The meeting's threads and the map's moves are written in story terms, with no quotes, figures, times or document ids. Each carries its evidence underneath through every stop: the writers attach it, the fact check reads it, and the article writer cites from it. The director can open it but never needs to. The meeting runs to at most 300 words and the map to at most 450, aiming for 300; the article stays at about 1,500.
2. **The change review.** When the director approves or reweaves with changes, one call reads all of them together against the record. It reads each change back in one line, so the director sees it landed, and translates it into evidence. It holds the stop only for:
   - a critical ambiguity;
   - a change the record can't support, where the director chooses: Nova's question, their own account of the room, the wording the record supports, or drop it;
   - a change that leaves another line without support.

   When everything is clear it goes straight on, and the readbacks show at the next stop.
3. **The meeting's page** (redesigned 2026-10-06). A memo of two or three pitched angles over one shared set of threads. The angles sit side by side; the one the director opens is its pitch (headline, story, question, why it lands, where it ends up), edited in place; its threads are flipped in or out, named for what happened; the questions sit beside what they change. When the notes end with the director's read, angle 1 is that read. The roles, the main thread and the connection strike go. At most 450 words. Any line opens in place to show what supports it and what cuts against it.
4. **The map's page.** A corkboard of the story's moves by section. Moving a card places it, and Left out is a tray to strike into and bring back from. Photos show as pictures with the director's descriptions, and the counts update as the director works. The margin and the look-through work as at the meeting.
5. **The desk, in stages.** First, the article as it will print is the editor itself: a block editor that shows each block's controls when the director is in it, with the fact check's notes in the margin, pinned to their lines. This takes in phase 12. Then AI help on sections, in the margin, which may cover phase 11.
6. **The review as the director works,** once piece 2 has run on a few sessions: the same call, run on each change while the stop is open.

**The order.** Piece 1 first, then pieces 3 and 4, then piece 2; piece 5 alongside them, piece 6 later. Piece 1 goes first because most of the wall was content: on its own it should bring today's pages down to the agreed length, and every page depends on its shape. After 100326 the director moved the two pages ahead of the change review, the meeting first (2026-10-06).

**The patterns, from comparable tools.**

## How a phase runs

- **Briefs, not code.** A phase document holds one brief per slice: intent from the director's side, the three layers with their integration points, invariants, the tests to move, verification on a copy, the "done" observable, and what is out of scope. Implementers read the code and write their own.
- **Subagents run on the session's model.** That is Opus 5.5 since 2026-09-22, so the `model` field stays unpinned. Reviewers are never weaker than the integrator. Every seat runs on it, the final whole-phase review included (the director's ruling on 2026-09-24; phase 1 gave that seat to Fable).
- **Integration.** Each task runs in its own worktree and branch. The integrator rebases and fast-forwards into the phase branch in dependency order.
- **The gate.** The full test suite. A plain diff of the prompts rendered by `scripts/render-prompts.js` against a baseline from `main`, in which every hunk must be one a brief names. A live pass on a copy of a real thread, through `CHECKPOINT_DB_PATH` on a throwaway port, reading the call log at each step. A click-through of the screens, each read against its mock-up with a real session's material. The gate proves the pipeline works; whether the article got better is shown by the director's next session.
- **Merging and running.** A phase merges to `main` only when every slice passes, so `main` always runs. The director decides when to pause and when to run a session. The production database and the director's server on port 3001 are never touched. Pushes happen when the director asks.
