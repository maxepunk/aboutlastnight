# Director console roadmap

The build order for the design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-22-director-console-design.md`, with the status of each phase. Updated 2026-09-22 after session 092026. It supersedes the phase table in the local grill workspace (`.superpowers/grill/2026-09-20/phase-plan.md`). That file's footprint notes are still worth reading before briefing a phase, but their line numbers date from `main` `4ba3bbd`.

## How the order is set

Phases are ordered by what a problem costs the director to fix by hand at the stop where it surfaces, then by dependency. A structural problem costs a whole-article rework or an hour of rewriting; a wrong sentence costs minutes but needs reading against the record; a caption costs seconds. Words the director already writes, and facts the session already holds, reaching the right prompt come before new controls, because that work is deterministic and cheap.

## Phase 2: the record reaches the reworker

**Intent.** At the arc, outline and article stops, a rework runs with everything its writer had. An automatic pass never removes a card because it could not see the source, and a send-back at the outline stop is worked by a writer that can see the arcs and the director's notes. The director sees what the automatic pass did before reading.

**Why now.** On 092026 the article reworker had no document text, so it answered a "copy the card from the source" finding by deleting four correct cards; the director restored them by hand. The outline reworker has never seen more than the selected arc ids and three evidence counts, and phase 3 makes outline reworks the main structural tool.

**Scope, by layer.**

- **Prompts.**
  - The article reworker gets the evidence packages the article writer got.
  - The outline reworker gets the outline writer's inputs: arc details, interweaving, photos, the director's notes, and the arc evaluation's advisories.
  - The arc reworker gets the full evidence list its writer got; today it sees about half of it.
  - The writer stops putting document text in sidebar entries, which print only a headline and a summary.
  - The remote reporting-mode block tells the writer to show its absence through attribution instead of announcing it.
- **Plumbing.**
  - The fact check stops reading sidebar text. Its fix line points at the documents in the prompt instead of offering "or drop the card".
  - A sentence after a closing quotation mark stops failing when its words match.
  - A defect shared by an inline card and its sidebar entry is reported once.
  - The version before each automatic pass in the current round is kept, and its diff sent with the stop.
- **Screen.** A read-only trace panel at the outline and article stops: each automatic pass, why it ran, what it changed.

**Out of scope.** The evaluation seeing the record, which belongs to the claim check in phase 6. Server-filled card text, in phase 4. Comments and undo on the trace, in phase 9.

**Open decisions, with recommendations.**

1. **All of the writer's evidence, or only the documents the draft cites and the findings name?** Recommend all of it. A rework may need a document the draft does not cite yet, and the writer's prompt already carries it all, so the cost is prompt size, not new risk. On 092026 the writer's prompt was 211,000 characters and the reworker's 88,000.
2. **Where the pre-pass versions live.** Recommend one new state channel holding the versions for the current round. It is cleared on a send-back and at every rollback point, like the hand-edit fields. The evaluation history already records why each pass ran.
3. **How to prove it live.** Recommend a copy of the 092026 thread rolled back to the article stop: the regenerated article meets the same fact check. The gate checks that the reworker's prompt holds the documents, that cards survive the automatic pass, and that the trace appears. Add one outline send-back to prove the outline reworker's inputs.

**Before briefing.** Survey the current code, because phase 1 moved the line numbers. The survey covers the three rework prompt builders, how the writers' evidence reaches their prompts, the fact check's card loop, the sidebar instructions in the article prompt, and the payloads at the outline and article stops.

## Phases 3 to 12, in brief

**3. Story map.** The outline keeps its six section keys and changes what they hold: purpose, material by name, how the thesis shows through the section, the hand-off to the next. The thesis, headline, deck and length sit on top. The outline prompt says the outline is the brief the writer follows; the article prompt writes to it; the outline evaluation scores against it. The console shows the map as text the director can edit and annotate. It needs phase 2, because outline reworks become the main structural tool.

**4. Evidence cards from the record.** The writer names a document and, for a long document, a passage. The server fills the card text and the citation line at assembly. The card fidelity check retires for printed cards. Open: how the writer names a passage in a long paper document. Options are sentence anchors, a start and end phrase, or a numbered passage list shown in the prompt.

**5. The article stop's hands.** Delete, move and insert blocks. An empty-block check before sending. Every editor visible. A rebuild scope on send-back, which reuses the rollback to the article stop and keeps the notes.

**6. Help for reading.** The claim check on Opus before the evaluation, with its list at the article stop. A rubric rewritten from the director's send-back reasons and the 091826 and 092026 edits. The evaluation gets the record. Should-consider findings become candidate notes the director can accept into a rework.

**7. Thesis at the arc stop.** The arc writer proposes the thesis and the director settles it at the arc stop. The rest of the story memo: arcs as claims with a quotable line per document, anchored notes on arcs, proposing an arc.

**8. Photos.** A structured payload from the character-IDs stop carries the director's description verbatim. The Haiku rewrite goes. The outline and article writers see the description; the caption rule and check ground captions in it; exclude filters work. The photo list joins by filename everywhere.

**9. The review.** Notes gain a target and a status. A composer at each stop sends one review. The writer replies to each note after a rework, and the director closes notes. Trace findings take comments.

**10. Splice and section scope.** A targeted rework sends the anchored blocks with context and gets back only those blocks, which the server splices by fingerprint. "Rewrite this section" becomes a scope.

**11. The article surface.** The rendered page with overlays for editing, a rail, and change highlights.

**12. Intake, input review, rollback.** A two-step intake. Direct edits of the parse, written to disk. Observations from the director's notes listed and marked used. Rollback as a named action on each stop with its cost stated.

## How a phase runs

- **Briefs, not code.** A phase document holds one brief per slice: intent from the director's side, the three layers with their integration points, invariants, the tests to move, verification on a copy, the "done" observable, and what is out of scope. Implementers read the code and write their own.
- **Subagents run on the session's model.** That is Opus 5.5 since 2026-09-22, so the `model` field stays unpinned. Reviewers are never weaker than the integrator.
- **Integration.** Each task runs in its own worktree and branch. The integrator rebases and fast-forwards into the phase branch in dependency order.
- **The gate.** The full test suite. A plain diff of the prompts rendered by `scripts/render-prompts.js` against a baseline from `main`, in which every hunk must be one a brief names. A live pass on a copy of a real thread, through `CHECKPOINT_DB_PATH` on a throwaway port, reading the call log at each step. A click-through of the screens.
- **Merging and running.** A phase merges to `main` only when every slice passes, so `main` always runs. The director decides when to pause and when to run a session. The production database and the director's server on port 3001 are never touched. Pushes happen when the director asks.
