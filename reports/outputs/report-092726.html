NovaNews Investigation | The Blackwood Party

Room Names Quinn Sterling, the Chemist Marcus Blackmailed

Nine of fifteen agreed Quinn Sterling gave Marcus Blackwood experimental drugs. The record they built shows a memory-extraction test that worked, and a morning that paid $4,155,000 to erase what it took.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-092726-26

About five minutes before the police arrived, Jess Kane asked Quinn Sterling whether he had been Marcus Blackwood's business partner or his friend. Quinn said they were on different sides: Marcus was blackmailing him and forcing him to run an experiment he did not want to do. Jess kept going. Marcus, she said, was “doing a program” that took people's memories. Blake told her Marcus took all of their memories, and that was what happened last night. Jess said she meant a business, “like taking memory.”

Minutes later, nine of the fifteen voted to name Quinn in their statement as the man who gave Marcus experimental drugs. Vic Kingsley drew four votes and Remi Whitman one; one person did not vote. Then Blake, who manages operations at NeurAI, told Quinn they should get everyone out before the police came through the door, told them to bring whatever they were holding, and led the whole group through the curtains into the business next door.

I had been investigating Marcus Blackwood before last night. This morning I monitored remotely, from outside the warehouse, as the uninterested third party Fremont PD required. What reached me was 36 memories, not one with a name on it, and a ledger showing $4,155,000 paid through deals with Blake to erase others. So: was this a murder investigation, or the cleanup after a test run? And whom does a verdict on Quinn serve?

The Easiest Target

With just under ten minutes left, Blake said the Board held a lot but nobody had brought a strong theory. Sam Thorne went first, with a memory she had not had time to expose: at 9:32 PM, she said, Marcus and Quinn talked privately, and Marcus seemed unwell. “I think it's Quinn's drug.” Alex Reeves countered with a message from Jamie that Marcus was alive after that and met a stranger in a lab coat. Sam answered that it takes time between taking a drug and dying. Nothing in the record settles that, and Sam's memory never reached the Board, so the statement could not cite it. The first memory turned in this morning, at 08:11 AM, puts Marcus later still:

Marcus, 1:50 AM: the extractions worked
The extractions worked. The instructions were clear: "Your memories are now in the tokens. Secure them. Combinations only you know. Protect them however you can." Simple. Now you just need these drugged zombies to LOCK them. "SAM—take this token. Lock the—." They wander off mid-command. SARAH's staring at a wall, tears streaming. This is chaos. You don’t think ANYONE has the right combinations anymore. Drugged people make terrible assistants.
Marcus Blackwood

Quinn answered that Marcus had been taking the experimental drugs, and that the program Marcus built was “incomplete and buggy.” Vic, Alex and Remi, he said, had talked privately at the party about a stolen program Marcus was using. Blake asked whether Quinn was saying they killed Marcus. “I'm not directly implying that, but I am saying that Marcus's usage of that program is faulty.” Blake called it interesting background, said nobody was surprised Marcus was stealing, and wanted a story for the police in five minutes. Then came Jess's question, and the blackmail. Marcus had written to Quinn on February 17:

"I know you're resistant because you didn't test levels anywhere near this high during the PT-3B safety assessments, but I'm certain we should go up on the dosage. And you can't afford to say no, can you?"

Marcus Blackwood

Ezra Sullivan's memory has Quinn, at 12:02 AM, asking what to do when a colleague will not listen about “something important, like safety.” The colleague was Marcus. Jamie's memory from 10:01 PM, which I take to be the message Alex meant, has a stranger in a lab coat warning Marcus: “Ingesting extracts—untested. Dangerous.” Its title pairs Marcus with Quinn; its text names only a stranger. And at 12:52 AM, Quinn told old friends why he had called off his research:

Quinn, 12:52 AM: the blackmail confessed
You, CASS, and ZIA. It’s the first time you’ve been together since that infamous conference call when you burned it all down. You finally confess why you called off your research then, and how MARCUS has been blackmailing you to continue that same work. CASS hugs you so hard. ZIA is guarded but they seem to understand. The truth is incredibly freeing.
Quinn Sterling

Then Blake called on Ashe Motoko. Marcus took some untested medicine before he died, she said, and she suspected someone passed it to him; she had heard Quinn gave him medicine, but was not sure it was the same one. With two and a half minutes left came three lines from voices the record does not name: “I vote Quinn. Vic and Alex are on the different side. Quinn is the murderer.” “I'm accusing Alex.” “I'm accusing primarily Remi.” With a minute and a half left, Quinn said Skyler had proposed Marcus a solution, and accused “Vic and Skyler both.” Blake took it as support for the Vic theory. The vote went to Quinn, a relative outsider, on what was said aloud: a memory Sam never exposed, a claim Ashe hedged herself, and Quinn's own word that Marcus was taking the drugs.

The charge was the drug, and in the record the drug changes hands. Sam's baggies are “Different formulations that Sam created based on the compound synthesized by Quinn,” and Psychotrophin3B is the one “used to extract party-goers’ memories.” Nat Francisco's memory from 11:04 PM has Jess calling it “SAM's memory drug.” Sam's diary says Marcus paid her “an ungodly amount of money off the books” to reproduce a formula, and that “it's probably the shit I'm making for him.” At 10:44 PM:

Sam, 10:44 PM: “your usual party blend”
God’s favorite idiot. Jesus Christ, MARCUS is so high! And you just gave him your usual party blend. He should not be so out of his mind that told you that! The BizAI algorithm wasn’t his own work. ALEX rewrote the whole thing and MARCUS somehow still took credit. Is he feeling remorse? No. This is MARCUS we’re talking about. You bite your tongue and take another hit.
Sam Thorne

That memory sat on the Board from 09:29 AM, and nothing in the account of the deliberation that reached me has anyone raising it. No one accused Sam, and I don't: the same diary says “I'm worried about M.” But Quinn's own memory, up since 09:05 AM, says “MARCUS and SAM played you to get their hands on your formulations for Psychotrophin-3A.” The case opened on Sam's word about the man who made the compound. Why did it stop there, when the Board held the hands that mixed the doses?

Nat's memory names a second ingredient: “some environmental controls.” On the day of the party, Marcus wrote to Kai Andersen: “I want to see a working demo tonight at the party. It's crucial that there are no problems with the environmental cues. I need them to run exactly as written.” At 12:38 AM, Kai and Cass Zhang found that the code Marcus insisted on was Cass's Synesthesia Engine, not yet paid for, and that Marcus meant to run it “on an unsuspecting party audience.” That this code is Nat's environmental controls is my reading. Six minutes earlier, Jamie overheard a stranger whom the memory's title names as Skyler:

Jamie, 12:32 AM: “Tonight is the test run”
You’re busy restocking when a stranger walks in. They have the wrong vibe for a MARCUS party. "You missed our deadline." MARCUS goes pale. "Tonight is the test run. You have my prototype. You will program it with your memory recordings. Remember I can make it do anything I need." MARCUS doesn’t respond. You freeze. THANK GOD. They walk past you like you're furniture.
Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods

The room came near this, and let it go. With under four minutes left, Riley Torres called it a possible financial crime that Alex, Vic and Morgan knew about, then added Remi, “because they stole a program.” Alex said she had already sued Marcus and won, so “it doesn't make sense for me to kill him.” The record holds her lawyers' January 28 cease-and-desist, with a February 28 deadline, and no ruling. Morgan's own memory of meeting Vic at 8:23 PM, up since 09:36 AM, reads: “Something has to be done, and soon. Fortunately, you’ve already talked to SKYLER about a potential solution.” Vic drew four votes. Alex drew none. “Well, way to defend yourself, Alex,” Blake said.

Rewind to forty minutes before the end. Riley said Jess might be Marcus's mistress; Quinn went further and said Jess has Marcus's child. Asked by Blake whether she remembered being pregnant, Jess said, “At least right now, I don't feel like I'm pregnant.” Sam pointed at Mel Nilsson with a text that reads “Sleeping w M again?” Mel's reply in the record: “I wouldn't make that same mistake twice.” When Alex turned the talk to drugs, “because there are a lot of drugs,” and someone raised business involving a lot of money, Blake called it worth investigating further. By the final minutes, the stolen program was background. The paternity test names Jess Kane and Marcus Blackwood: 99.85%. At 1:36 AM:

Jess, 1:36 AM: the bathroom
Bathroom. Both crying. SARAH knows. You know she knows. She says: “The baby's MARCUS’s?” You nod. Expect rage. Get: “You can have him. But that baby deserves better. We both do.” You're not enemies. You're both his victims.
Jess Kane

That memory went up at 08:31 AM, the second of the morning. After Blake gathered everyone to take the room's temperature, Sarah and Jess were seen deep in conversation on a couch; what they said is unrecorded. At 1:50 AM, Marcus's memory finds Sarah “staring at a wall, tears streaming.” And Jess, by Nat's memory, had already been through an extraction and lost the memory of it. As I read it, “You're both his victims” reaches past the affair, into the experiment.

All 36 memories went up anonymously, and two reporters were among those turning them in. Ashe Motoko kept hers anonymous to protect herself; Taylor Chase turned in a number to help the investigation. Which were whose, the record does not say. But there's history there. On January 31, Taylor wrote to Marcus that “Ashe's expose on your previous company won't ever see daylight,” then asked about “that exclusive on the NeurAI launch.” At 12:40 AM last night, Morgan gave Taylor a list that included “performing dangerous experiments on unwitting test subjects.” Ashe, bound by the paper's NDA, had written to Nat on February 12: “So this can NEVER link back to me.” The reporter who hedged on Quinn had her reasons, I suspect, to keep her name off everything. While the Board filled with unsigned memories, the ledger filled too.

Follow the Money

The ledger never says which memory it paid for. It does say when. At 08:11 AM, Marcus's “The extractions worked” went public. At 08:15 AM came the first sale of the morning, $450,000 into an account named Donaldtrump, and a minute later the $50,000 first-burial bonus landed in the same account. At 09:21 AM, in the minute Alex's memory went up, the one in which Vic learns a $30 million investment “bought them a sham,” five sales went into DonaldTrumpJR, a different account: $225,000, $450,000, $150,000, $450,000 and $250,000. That made it the morning's largest, at $1,525,000. The buyer pays more the more sensitive the memory, and that one minute held two of the morning's three $450,000 sales. Sellers choose their accounts' names, and timing ties no one.

At the very end, an account named Pikachu took $250,000 and $225,000 at 09:36 AM, as Quinn's confession and Morgan's “potential solution” went public, and Water took $225,000 at 09:37 AM, as Kai's and Remi's stolen-code memories went up. What went dark while the receipts of Quinn's coercion surfaced cannot be known.

As I read it, the money behind the test came before the party: the $30 million investment that, in Alex's memory, bought Vic's side a sham, and the $20 million commitment for Marcus's next round that enraged Remi Whitman. That was money into the company of a man using a drug, by Nat's memory, to extract memories. The formulations were paid for off the books. For Quinn, the currency was leverage. Who paid this morning, the ledger does not say. I suspect NeurAI's board.

The Players

Flip spent much of the morning unlocking memories, and was seen working with Vic Kingsley and Remi Whitman on a tough lock around Marcus's NeurAI one-pager, the pitch to “transform the landscape of Memory Capture” for a “$30 Million” strategic investment. When her name was added at the end, no one voted for her. Vic mostly stayed under the radar, unlocking memories, and drew four votes anyway. Remi worked with Kai Andersen on a box holding memories for both of them, and Kai, who spent much of the morning getting past locks, owns the memory that asks what the vote never did: “What makes this program worth stealing and executing on unsuspecting guests?”

Sarah Blackwood sat at the centre of the morning's quiet corners: an early, heated discussion with Ashe, the couch with Jess, counsel from Mel Nilsson, and an aside with Sam in the last ten minutes. None of it was recorded. Mel was also seen visiting Blake once or twice, at no logged time, and nothing ties those visits to any sale. Riley Torres, who pointed the room at Jess, had at 11:09 PM handed Mel a career-making case so Mel could protect Sarah.

Nat Francisco worked alongside Kai and closely with Alex, and was in a heated huddle with Alex, Jess and Sam; what was said is unrecorded. Remi told the room he had nothing to do with it, and drew one vote. His memory from 1:08 AM has him laying Zia's 2023 code beside NeurAI's for Vic and Alex: “the data is identical.” Morgan reaches this story only through memory. At 12:40 AM, by his own, Morgan decided to call Marcus's bluff and began spilling the worst he had on Marcus to Taylor.

What's Missing

When did Sam unlock her 9:32 PM memory, and why did the room get it only as her word? Was the envelope that changed hands between Marcus and a lab-coat stranger at 10:01 PM the higher-dose batch his February 17 email demanded, and who chose Psychotrophin3B for the guests? What “potential solution” had Morgan discussed with Skyler, and where are the prototype and the “memory recordings” the stranger told Marcus to program into it? Who mailed the paternity test to Sarah's home? Sam's diary ends, “Maybe there's another way to let her know...” And what went dark at 09:21 AM?

In the final minutes, Mel, Sarah and Alex were seen quietly saying something about Remi. What was it? And what did Blake mean, telling Quinn to get everyone out before the police came through the door?

Lay the record's pieces side by side. Quinn Sterling's compound, and a new batch demanded under “you can't afford to say no, can you?” Sam Thorne's formulations, with Psychotrophin3B used on the guests. Cass Zhang's code, running through Kai Andersen's installation “exactly as written.” A stranger, whom the memory's title names as Skyler, saying “Tonight is the test run.” Marcus at 1:50 AM: “The extractions worked.” Then a morning in which 36 memories went up with no name on any of them, while $4,155,000 was paid through deals with Blake to erase others. Jess Kane, who had already lost a memory to an extraction, tried to name it out loud: a business “like taking memory.” What I see in the record is a test run, and a morning that looked a lot like its cleanup. The official story the room could live with names the man who synthesized the compound. The experiment walked out through the curtains with everyone else.

As I write, Quinn Sterling is unavailable for comment; his assistant says he is traveling to the Cayman Islands for a vacation to recover from the events of last night. I still want his answer to the statement that named him. Meanwhile, an intercepted email between Vic Kingsley and NeurAI's board of directors suggests Alex Reeves will be tapped as NeurAI's new CEO once the murder investigation “blows over.” It proves no deal and says nothing about how Marcus died. But in January, Alex's lawyers demanded Marcus's equity and the CTO's chair, with a neutral interim CEO. I suspect NeurAI's board stood behind this morning's buying, and gains most from a story that ends with one outsider. NovaNews wants the board's answer on that email, the name of this morning's buyer, and the prototype and recordings from Jamie's memory. The drug Quinn tried to walk away from has now been tested. The market for erasing what it took has had its first morning.

The room voted on who handed Marcus Blackwood the drug. Nobody voted on what it was for.
