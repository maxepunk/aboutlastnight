The Blackwood Investigation

Marcus Blackwood's Guests Blamed Remi Whitman. The Evidence Might Tell a Different Tale

Seven of eleven chose the founder who admitted what he sold. Yet the exposed record leans toward the people around Marcus who made the drug, held the leverage and agreed on 'extreme measures.'

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-092626-26

Marcus Blackwood is dead. The last exposed memory of him alive is his own: guests drugged, their memories pulled out of them, Marcus losing patience with the help.

By the time this morning's investigation ended, $8.72 million had been paid into three shell accounts to bury memories like those. Eleven people spent the morning deciding who killed him. Seven chose Remi Whitman, whose initials were on the biggest account. Not Vic Kingsley, the investor who funded him. Not the woman who confessed. Not the chemist who made the drug. But the outsider who admitted what he'd sold.

The last reporter who tried to tell the truth about Marcus had her exposé buried and lost her job over it. This time, the story runs.

The Story

With ten minutes left in the investigation this morning, Taylor Chase asked who was hiding $4 million in the RW account. Alex Reeves said Remi Whitman, because he'd seen Remi with Blake. And what did Remi have against Marcus?

Remi to Marcus: 'What. The. Fuck.'
From: rwhitman@aibiocomp.com To: marcus@neurai.com Date: Feb 6, 2027 Subject: Fundraising Hey Marcus, I hear that Vic's committed $20 Million for your next round of funding. What. The. Fuck. Look, I get it, you're a slick salesman and have history with Vic, but seriously?? My company literally is going to be in the black soon with earned revenue and Vic won't give us the time of the day, but you with your mediocre idea and code can secure $20 million?? UGH, I hate silicon valley! - Remi
Remi Whitman

That was two weeks before the party, and no exposed memory puts Remi with Marcus last night. By every account I have, the room went at him anyway. He asked why he'd use his own initials. Then: “It's true. I fucking hated Marcus.”

I've spent months on Marcus Blackwood. Hating him was the least exclusive club in his orbit, and the record has plenty of other members.

With 45 minutes left, the leading theory had been Sarah Blackwood: a husband cheating on her and stealing from her. Her 10:10PM memory backs the stealing, a cleared-out shared account and her belief that Marcus “must have corporate-shielded everything.” Her lawyer, Mel Nilsson, had told her to say nothing.

Mel knew more than her client was saying.

11:09PM: 'MARCUS Has No Idea What's Coming'
MEL.3 - 11:09PM - You thought you had a solid case with the paternity test and evidence of MARCUS’s infidelity. But then RILEY revealed their cards: secret bank accounts, convoluted shell companies, environmental destruction, evidence of insider trading with someone pretty high up in the government... You stare, gobsmacked. This could be a career-making case for RILEY, but instead, they’re giving it all to you so that you can use it to protect SARAH. You won’t let them down. MARCUS has no idea what's coming.
Mel Nilsson

Blake called it relatively damning. At 12:50AM, Mel's own memory has her convening her old college circle, the Stanford Four, and what's coming looks like leverage: would Marcus return Sarah's money if Mel threatened him with what she knew? Her email to her Patchwork Law partners mentions a paternity test “anonymously mailed” to Sarah's home.

Then, people in the room say, Jess Kane told everyone she had found a bedroom in Marcus's secret warehouse, condoms in the drawer: “So I assume that is not where he spent time with you, Sarah.” Sarah shot back: “How would you know that, Jess?” Jess admitted the affair and the pregnancy. The paternity test mailed to Sarah's home was Jess's: Marcus, 99.85 percent.

Back to Alex. Only one moment on the record shows someone getting physical with Marcus last night. It wasn't Remi.

11:35PM: 'Until You Tried to Break My Husband's Nose. Again.'
SARAH.3 - 11:35PM - ALEX: I’m so sorry - you shouldn’t have to deal with us acting like this / SARAH: I somehow I recall breaking up a fight between you two the last time you were at one of his parties too / ALEX: You know why though. He doesn't know how to back down, so he ramps up instead. / SARAH: I do. We've both seen it / ALEX: Then why the hell are you still with him? He’s such an asshole! / SARAH: I know he is... but he’s my asshole. Now go home. It really was nice to see you, until you tried to break my husband’s nose. Again.
Sarah Blackwood

Marcus was alive more than two hours later. But the record never confirms that Alex went home. His cease and desist against NeurAI sits on Patchwork Law letterhead. Mel's firm. On Feb 17, Remi had asked Alex to get him on the guest list: “Maybe we can do some digging together and take that asshole down…” The man who accused Remi was the ally Remi had tried to recruit.

Then there are the people who'd bet on Marcus. Morgan Reed, those watching say, worked quietly and was seen several times with Mel, Sarah and Jess. Morgan's file in the record lists an 8PM emergency meeting with Vic, whose fund backed both Marcus and Remi, and separately says the two had agreed on “extreme measures.” Remi's parting shot was that Vic had framed him. An accusation that fits Vic's typical approach to a crisis: “deny, deflect, and distract.”

Marcus had his own worry about Vic. Someone wrote it down.

Sam's Diary, Feb 17: 'No Shit, Marcus.'
diary entry 2/17/27 M was off his head when I ran into him at the lab today. He has been there more and more often lately. He told me "I'm worried about what Vic knows. I wish I could remember that last convo with them, but my memory is not the best." No shit, Marcus. Try taking it a little easier on the drugs why don't you.
Sam Thorne

Nat Francisco's 9:42PM memory has Sam Thorne describing the experimental memory drug he'd been making in a warehouse lab, with Marcus taking it. Labeled baggies name the formulation, Psychotrophin-3B, that pulled the guests' memories last night. At 12:50AM, Sam's own memory has him confessing he helped Marcus blackmail Quinn Sterling, and privately knowing who mailed Sarah that paternity test. Him.

Add it up. A wife Marcus had cleaned out. A girlfriend carrying his child. A lawyer holding leverage. An old partner who took a swing at Marcus... Again. Two people who'd agreed on extreme measures. A chemist whose drug Marcus was on. The room weighed an overdose theory that might have implicated Sam, then quietly let it drop. Sam told Blake nothing happened, and that he knew he didn't do it. The record's last look at Marcus is his own memory from 1:50AM: the extractions done, and Marcus handing Sam one of the stolen memories to lock away.

1:50AM: 'Drugged People Make Terrible Assistants'
They wander off mid-command. SARAH's staring at a wall, tears streaming. This is chaos. You don’t think ANYONE has the right combinations anymore. Drugged people make terrible assistants.
Marcus Blackwood

With seven minutes left, attendees say, Blake read the case against Sarah off the board. Then Jess confessed to the murder, citing her pregnancy. The preliminary vote, as it was reported to me, went Remi 6, Sarah 4, Jess 0. After Remi made his case, some hands rose for Vic, but not enough. Final round: Remi 7, Vic 4.

Follow the Money

Burying a memory pays. This morning, $8.72 million went into three shell accounts, straight to whoever held them. Only one has a face.

RW holds 73 percent, and Remi claimed it. By the accounts I have, he told the room he sold to keep his company going, and that it had nothing to do with killing Marcus. His one-pager calls AI Bio Comp revenue positive and raising $10 million; RW comes to nearly two-thirds of that round. A revenue-positive company can still run short when its investor puts $20 million behind the rival. But pitch and plea don't quite match. Between Taylor's question and the moment the room turned on him, the figure on the board climbed from about $4 million past $6 million.

The other $2.36 million stayed anonymous. Mickey filled in about two minutes, just behind RW's biggest burst; Alex and Taylor asked the room who Mickey was, and the accounts I have record no answer. Nobody I heard from claimed Bulbasaur, a lone sale half an hour before either. Two people working carefully and quietly to turn last night's crisis into an opportunity this morning.

Kingsley-Fund's first two bets, NeurAI and AI Bio Comp, were built to compete. Vic's explanation, in print: “I have my reasons.” At one point, attendees say Remi approached Blake about NeurAI's future leadership. Since the investigation, I've intercepted a communication between NeurAI's board and Blake about merging NeurAI with AIBioComp. Both of Vic's bets, one company. The question is whether this is a move to push Vic out of the equation altogether, or to give him more control over both of his investments.

The Players

All the accounts I've received from the warehouse made this seem less a murder investigation and more like a room gambling on the post-Marcus power structure. And by the end the insiders set their differences aside and closed ranks, and the room accused an outsider.

Start with those who put things on the record. Remi, rebuffed early by Vic and Taylor, claimed he wanted things exposed. I was tipped off that Mel's “Marcus has no idea what's coming” memory was made public by Remi. Yet, he's also the one who owned up to selling memories for profit. This ultimately earned him seven votes. Meanwhile, Ashe Motoko dug like a true journalist, questioning everything...and everyone.

Then there was Marcus' inner circle. Mel, it emerged, had her own affair with Marcus. Sarah had known about the cheating before the party, and she claimed her divorce proceedings were alibi enough for her not to have any motive for murder. Meanwhile, Marcus' fixer, Morgan stayed under the radar, working her way through the investigation quietly and efficiently. And Sam, whose name is on every one of those old posters, who was known to have been supplying Marcus with drugs to feed his habit was quickly dismissed as a potential suspect as the theory of a potential overdose got dismissed in favor of Remi as the murderer.

Jess Kane is where the watcher's lens cuts deepest. Early on she was overheard telling Sarah, “You deserve to know the truth. Just saying.” Look at what she carried into the vote: Marcus's child, and a rose card from him reading “I'm going to leave her soon. She means nothing to me.” Sarah's card carried the same rose and the same “She means nothing to me.” Marcus recycled his apologies. Mel, those watching say, spent the morning mediating between the two women. And when Jess confessed, Blake had just read the case against Sarah: Marcus had been planning to leave her. The promise on Jess's card, recast as the wife's motive.

Grief, a shield for Sarah, the double gambit Blake floated: I can't tell you which. Some in the room told her it was the drugs talking; the accounts from the room don't record anyone asking whose. Blake read her zero as the room deciding she was pregnant and hysterical, and called that awfully misogynistic. Remi's guilt came in two letters on a ledger and asked nothing of anyone inside. Believing Jess meant reopening everything the circle had spent the morning keeping private. The room took the letters.

"We both know access = power."

Taylor Chase, in an email to Morgan Reed

And those who steered. Taylor, who worked alongside Vic early on, spent the final minutes asking the room who was hiding money. Taylor had done some hiding too.

Ashe spent this morning in the same room as the person who helped bury her. Alex named Remi from a sighting, then brushed off Sarah, who last night had told him, “We've both seen it.” Kai Andersen, hunting allies all morning, ended among the quiet three at the back that Blake called out. Vic, told his claim to fund both companies was a lie, repeated it, and said the board pointed at everyone else.

What's Missing

Everything sold this morning is out of reach now. I can see its shape, not its contents, and I won't guess. I'll admit I want inside it badly. So should you.

What were the “extreme measures” Vic and Morgan agreed on, and, separately, what happened at their 8PM meeting? Who is the Phil that Remi asked about, the one Morgan seemed to know though no Phil was on the known attendee list? The record names Flip, who scouted venues for Marcus's parties, as Phil Kowalski, owing a bookie $30K that night. Flip wasn't among the eleven who woke up at the warehouse this morning. What was Phil Kowalski's real involvement with Marcus? And could there have been a motive for murder there that we just don't know about?

What did Morgan keep talking about with Mel, Sarah and Jess before Jess confessed? What did the Stanford Four decide at 12:50AM? Did Quinn Sterling bring the stronger batch Marcus demanded by email on Feb 17? And why did the overdose theory fade before anyone voted on it?

The available evidence stops at 1:50AM. Something happened between that moment and when 11 of Marcus' guests woke up with no recollection of the party last night. And we may never know what. Yet, the movement of money since the those 11 woke up this morning tells a whole story of its own and that story appears to be far from over.

"The evidence stops at 1:50AM. But the money that's been moving since tells its own story."

What It Means

The room chose Remi Whitman. His assistant says he's taking a short trip to the Cayman Islands to recover from the trauma of last night's party. An interesting coincidence given the communications I intercepted about a potential merger between his company and NeurAI.

In this room full of potential motives. Remi was the man accused. He, admittedly, tried to profit off of the chaos left behind by Marcus' death. And we may never know what was within those memories he buried to fund his company's future. Meanwhile, the room looked past Jess, who confessed to Marcus' murder. They took her words as the emotional response of a pregnant woman coping with her lover's death. And then there was Marcus' inner circle. Sam, Mel, Vic, Morgan, and Sarah to name a few... who, it appeared, were able to put aside their differences and close ranks when the moment called for it.

When push came to shove, the room pointed the finger at the relative outsider: Remi. The memories Psychotrophin-3B pulled out of those guests are what NeurAI was built to capture, and the market that priced them is now talking merger. Yet with a murder accusation hanging over him, Remi may never get to reap the rewards of such a merger.

By the end of the morning, everyone knew what RW stood for. Yet we still don't know who else profited, nor how those who invested in NeurAI's tech plan to capitalize on their investment now that Marcus is out of the picture.
