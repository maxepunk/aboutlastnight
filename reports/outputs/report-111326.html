The Marcus Blackwood Investigation

Skyler Iyer Sent Marcus a Machine. The Room Voted Skyler Was One.

The room let Sam Thorne go, followed Ashe Motoko's lead and voted Skyler Iyer an AI. But Marcus Blackwood's own AI was fake, and by evening Alex Reeves and Sarah Blackwood are tipped for NeurAI's top jobs. So what is the company really worth?

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent || Ashe Motoko | Contributing Reporter · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-111326-26

The sirens were close when Blake gave the room about three minutes and asked what anyone knew about Skyler Iyer. Someone said, “Skyler's not real.” Inside those minutes the room decided that Skyler, a name on the board for less than an hour, was an artificial intelligence that shut Marcus Blackwood down because, after all the drugs, he was getting too free with information. Blake called the show of hands “basically unanimous.” That is now the official story.

I monitored the investigation from outside the warehouse, the uninterested third party the police required, so my questions begin with the memories turned in to me. Two of the last, at 10:11 AM, sit badly beside that verdict. In Skyler's own, Skyler isn't a machine: Skyler sent Marcus one. In Alex Reeves's, the AI Marcus built NeurAI on is a sham. And this evening an anonymous source inside NeurAI tipped NovaNews to communications between Alex and the company's board about an offer to replace Marcus as CEO.

So here is what I'm chasing: with Marcus's AI proven fake and Marcus dead, where does NeurAI's real value lie?

How Skyler Got on the Board

The morning's first suspect was Sam Thorne. Asked by Blake what he remembered, Sam said he and Marcus organized the parties together. When asked about the drugs, he deflected, shifting the subject to a memory token he was “reading now about Vic...” Vic Kingsley cut in, a different memory token in hand:

"Wait, hold on. This little guy says you were the plug. For the drugs."

Vic Kingsley

Vic turned it in saying “I think this helps us understand what went on here.” The only memory on the board calling Sam the plug is Quinn Sterling's, logged at 09:28 AM, which lays out “how MARCUS and SAM played you to get their hands on your formulations for Psychotrophin-3A.”

With just under 45 minutes left before the police, Blake gathered everyone to discuss theories so far. Sam's name came up immediately, the group believed he administered a drug to the party goers without their consent and had potentially been blackmailing of Quinn. When asked for other theories, Ashe Motoko, answered by tipping me off with a memory token about Skyler's potential involvement in Marcus' death.

In it, Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods hears a stranger at the bar tell Marcus: “You have my prototype. You will program it with your memory recordings.” It never names Skyler; it says only that “a stranger walks in.” The tie is Jamie, who had agreed by text to gather intel for Skyler at “$100 for each interaction between Marcus's tech savvy guests that you can record.”

Soon after, Sarah Blackwood's memory of finding out about Jess Kane's involvement with Marcus went up:

Sarah Finds Jess
You find JESS in the bathroom, crying, and suddenly put it together. You squeak out between unexpected tears: “MARCUS’s baby is yours?” She’s sobbing so hard now that she can’t even speak, so she nods. You thought you’d be outraged, but seeing her here in front of you, you just feel resigned. You say: “You can have him. But that baby deserves better. We both do.”
Sarah Blackwood

Two minutes later Sarah tipped me off with a document from Riley Torres: a letter of reminders of how she deserved better than Marcus. Also found on the scene were Marcus's apology cards to both women, carrying the same rose and the same line: “She means nothing to me. Please don't leave me.”

Meanwhile, Alex Reeves seemed motivated to clear Sam's name. Around 9:40 she was overheard muttering to herself, “I think we can clear his name,” then went calling out “Sam!” before setting out to look for him. Then, during the final deliberations, asked why he would kill Marcus, Sam said Marcus's work paid him; with Marcus gone, “I don't have any more money.” Eliciting the response, “I guess that makes sense. That clears his name.”

Someone pushed back: Sam had sent Sarah word of Marcus's child, and had done “a lot of things” that seemed “sketchy too..." But someone else took Sam's side as a friend torn between Sarah and his employer with Sam responding: “That seems like, I don't want to wreck this family, you know?”

Sam's memory backs the letter: “You were the one who mailed the paternity test to SARAH in the first place. You thought she should know.” Two lines earlier, the same memory reads: “You confess to your role in helping MARCUS blackmail QUINN.” The letter carried the defence. In Blake's preliminary vote, Quinn got no hands, and neither did Sam.

Every hand went to Skyler, on a case built after the sirens. Someone suggested the AI was behind the first memory the room exposed, at 08:45 AM, Marcus's own: “Drugged people make terrible assistants.” Someone set Quinn, “the human, who was trying to cover their own skin,” against Skyler the AI, and the AI's own lines were offered up: “Does Marcus know too much?”, “Human behavior is too confusing for you to understand.” The answer: “I like that. It's Skyler for sure.” The name every hand went up for was the one Ashe's filing had put on the board. Blake called no second round and led everyone out.

The memory that puts Skyler at that bar went up at 10:11 AM, the last memory token to have been exposed:

Skyler Dares Marcus to Program the Prototype
It’s after midnight, so you go track MARCUS down at the bar. “You missed our deadline. You were supposed to show me a working prototype by tonight.” MARCUS deflects. He says the AI’s still buggy. “So try programming the prototype I sent you with your memory recordings instead. What’s the worst that could happen?" MARCUS deflects again. “It’s untested technology. What if something goes wrong?" But you’ve had enough of his delay tactics. It’s now or never.
Skyler Iyer

“You missed our deadline” is the stranger's line in Jamie's memory, word for word: Ashe's filing and this one are one encounter, seen from either side of the bar. In Jamie's, the stranger adds, “Remember I can make it do anything I need.” From outside, I can't tell whether anyone read Skyler's memory before the vote. I can tell you what it says: Skyler sent Marcus a machine, and the room voted that Skyler was one.

What Thirty Million Dollars Bought

Under the verdict sits a company. Remi Whitman's memory, turned in anonymously at 09:59 AM, has him turning Sam's laptop toward Vic and Alex to compare Zia Bishara's 2023 code with NeurAI's “proprietary” system: “the data is identical. MARCUS didn't even bother changing the notes. He’s not just a thief, he’s a sloppy thief.” In Alex's memory, the AI needs manual fixes for 90% of its inputs: “Their $30 million dollar investment? It bought them a sham.” Remi had warned Vic in January that the codebase was “at least a year outdated,” and early this morning the two were seen in an intense negotiation.

Vic also sits beside Skyler's name. In Morgan Reed's memory, turned in at 09:38 AM, Morgan and Vic agree that “Something has to be done, and soon,” and Morgan has “already talked to SKYLER about a potential solution.” Vic's own memory of learning the AI is worthless ends: “What does this mean for SKYLER’s prototype?” Why would NeurAI's investor care? Vic's fund backed NeurAI and Remi's AI Bio Comp at once, and his answer to the Business Journal was, “I have my reasons.”

Now watch who rises. In January, Alex's lawyers demanded that Marcus “be removed as the Chief Executive Officer of NeurAI,” and offered Alex as chief technology officer. Tonight's tip is about the top job itself, and rumours have begun that the board approached Sarah Blackwood about chief operating officer. Before the vote, Sarah had asked, “Where did my husband's money go?” Blake mentioned seeing something about shell accounts. Mel Nilsson's memory, filed by Ashe at 10:05 AM, has Riley handing Mel, Sarah's divorce lawyer, “secret bank accounts, convoluted shell companies.”

My read is that those invested in NeurAI's future will walk away from last night better off than they went in. Better off running what? Remi proved there's no working AI.

Read the pitch again. NeurAI's one-pager, in Vic's files, sells “capturing and storing memories using our Proprietary Technology,” with an “Opportunity for strategic investment $30 Million.” CyberLife, in Skyler's interview, wanted Marcus's “proprietary memory capture technology.” That part worked: Marcus's own memory opens, “The extractions worked.” And when Marcus told Skyler the AI was still buggy, Skyler's answer was to fill the prototype with memories instead. “What’s the worst that could happen?”

A Prototype, a Valet and Blake

First, the verdict's weak point. Skyler's office told NovaNews that the allegation that Mx. Iyer murdered Marcus “is preposterous,” and that Mx. Iyer, unavailable for comment, “is said to be recovering from the traumatic events of last night’s party.” If Skyler is in fact an Artificial Intelligence, someone is investing a lot of effort in presenting them as a human being with a personal history predating last night's party. In my opinion, the room's AI may have come from the experimental drugs every guest was dosed with: the “Different formulations that Sam created,” among them the Psychotrophin3B “used to extract party-goers’ memories.” The room let Sam go. I suspect his drug is what may have built an AI using the memories extracted from their heads.

Now set three things side by side. The first is Jess Kane, who worked with just about everyone this morning and was instrumental in getting memory after memory unlocked. Her own, turned in anonymously at 09:34 AM, tells how Marcus took one of hers:

Jess Plays a Valet
You approach your friend NAT and tell them you know why MARCUS is interested in Psychotrophin-3A. He uses it as part of his "Memory Extraction Protocol." Said it was an experimental NeurAI research he wasn’t ready to go public with. You know about it because he tried it on you once. Said it was an acting gig - that you should play a valet. You wanted to help him, so you took the drugs and let him run his experiment. He was ELATED. Showed you a token. Said your memory was stored on it somehow.
Jess Kane

Skyler offered Jamie money to learn “who he has secretly recruited to help him with his AI code and memory capture techniques.” Jess is one answer: she wanted to help him, and was told it was an acting gig. A memory recorded from someone playing a valet is exactly the kind of recording Skyler told Marcus to program into the prototype.

The second may be that prototype. Skyler's interview with Taylor Chase, turned in anonymously at 09:36 AM, describes what CyberLife had in mind:

Skyler's Interview with Taylor
A transcript of TAYLOR’s interview with SKYLER: We at CyberLife are aiming to bring next gen technology to the present era. Our blue-sky ideations involve notes and schema for a Benevolent, Lifelike, Anthropomorphic Kinetic Escort. We are in discussions with MARCUS BLACKWOOD over at NeurAI to incorporate his cutting edge, proprietary memory capture technology in our AI agent training protocols. We expect to be able to announce something more solid about that collaboration very soon.
Skyler Iyer

Read the escort's initials: BLAKE.

The third is Blake, whom Marcus called his Valet. Blake put Skyler's name on the board, asked what anyone knew about Skyler when the sirens came, called no second round and led everyone out. It has also been reported that Blake was intercepted by police this morning trying to sneak certain pieces of paper evidence out of the crime scene. The question is, who was it that ordered Blake to go back to the scene of the crime to ensure critical pieces of paper evidence were removed before the police got their hands on them?

The room named Skyler as Marcus Blackwood's killer. Skyler's office calls that preposterous, and from my vantage point, it is hard to say who is telling the truth. What I will commit to is where I think NeurAI's value lies now that Marcus is out of the picture: not in the code Remi caught Marcus stealing, but in what actually worked during last night's experiment. “Your memories are now in the tokens,” Marcus's own memory says, and all available evidence suggests that Skyler had sent him a prototype to put them in. A prototype about which Skyler claimed: “Remember I can make it do anything I need.”

My suspicion is that NeurAI's real worth now lies in a machine filled with stolen memories, and that whoever takes Marcus's chair inherits it. Could there have been a machine in that warehouse this morning? Could Skyler themselves have been an AI. What we do know is that Blake, the Valet, worked the deals and called the vote that conveniently identified Skyler as an AI responsible for Marcus' death. So I'll ask it plainly: Was Skyler an AI, or is Blake that machine operating on an intelligence cobbled together from the partygoers' memories?

After his last interaction with the authorities at the warehouse, Blake Manley's current whereabouts is unknown. Skyler Iyer remains unavailable for comment. Alex Reeves and Sarah Blackwood are rumored to be in line for NeurAI's leadership. Yet many questions remain about the true nature of the events which took place in Marcus Blackwood's warehouse party last night. NovaNews will keep reporting on who actually benefits from the death of Marcus Blackwood as more information becomes available.
