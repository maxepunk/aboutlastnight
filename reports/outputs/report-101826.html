Sam Thorne Took the Fall for a Warehouse of Power Brokers

Ten hands went up for Marcus Blackwood's chemist. While the room built its case, Morgan Reed went to Blake to bury, Vic Kingsley pivoted to Remi Whitman, and Sarah Blackwood is still owed. Then Blake made sure Sam got out before the police came.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-101826-26

In the last ten minutes before the police arrived, Morgan Reed turned the room on Sam Thorne. She raised the idea that Sam had drugged Marcus Blackwood, then added: “I also haven't heard very much from Sam.” Sam didn't dress it up: “I just make the drugs, man.” Ten hands went up for him. Vic Kingsley drew five, and four went to Marcus overdosing. “Sounds to me that we're going with Sam,” Blake said.

So the official story is that Marcus's chemist drugged him. Sam is not blameless, and his own diary says so. On February 18, three days before the party, he wrote about Marcus's habit: “it's probably the shit I'm making for him.” But I watched that warehouse all morning, and in my opinion Sam was a convenient fall guy in a web of corruption and greed that reached well beyond him. That leaves the question I've been chasing ever since: once the room settled on Sam, who walks away on top?

The Chemist Who Owned the Lab Coat

The case against Sam was real. Three of his memories went up as anonymous tips between 08:28 AM and 08:51 AM, and the second was a confession:

Sam's confession to the Stanford Four
MEL calls a meeting of The Stanford 4 sans MARCUS. You confess to your role in helping MARCUS blackmail QUINN. MEL rolls in with the paternity news and some secret bank accounts, like that's what's going to take Marcus down a peg. You were the one who mailed the paternity test to SARAH in the first place. You thought she should know.
Sam Thorne

Quinn Sterling's memory has Marcus and Sam playing Quinn “to get their hands on your formulations for Psychotrophin-3A.” Sam's diary explains why he never asked Marcus enough questions: “when you pay someone an ungodly amount of money off the books, you buy yourself a certain degree of acquiesence.”

Sam's defense was an overdose. Marcus was “straight up a druggie,” Sam told the room, and “he overdosed because, you know, he tried a little too much.” His diary from four days before the party agrees about the habit: “Try taking it a little easier on the drugs why don't you.” Then Blake asked whether anyone present was the man in the lab coat who had given Marcus drugs. Sam: “Me. I told him not to take the drugs.”

Sam's was one name among many. When Blake asked me to read out every theory, they ran from Taylor Chase and Morgan working together, through Sam and Nat Francisco blackmailing Sarah Blackwood and Vic and Morgan working together, to the newest: that Marcus had ruined Ashe Motoko's life.

Earlier, when Blake first gathered everyone, Cass Zhang had offered a bigger idea: Marcus drugged everyone to get specific information out of them, and the rest was a frame. It never gained enough traction to be put to a vote.

In the final minutes of the investigation, Kai Andersen asked Cass, “Do you have anything to hide?” Cass answered: “I have nothing to hide.” When the hands went up, Taylor drew two votes, one of them Ashe's. Blake called it one, and I logged two. Sam and Nat together drew none.

Then there was Jess Kane. She and Nat worked side by side frequently throughout the investigation. I was by the evidence board when it became public that Jess was Marcus's mistress. The room erupted in shock.

Marcus had sent his wife and his mistress the same card. To Jess: “I'm going to leave her soon. She means nothing to me.” To Sarah: “It's not going to happen again. She means nothing to me.” This is the background which instigated the theory that had the two women realizing they were both his victims and perhaps working together. Sarah's memory of finding Jess:

Sarah finds Jess
You find JESS in the bathroom, crying, and suddenly put it together. You squeak out between unexpected tears: “MARCUS’s baby is yours?” She’s sobbing so hard now that she can’t even speak, so she nods. You thought you’d be outraged, but seeing her here in front of you, you just feel resigned. You say: “You can have him. But that baby deserves better. We both do.”
Sarah Blackwood

Sam spoke up for them, asking why he and Nat would blackmail Sarah “when Sarah and Jess are both victims too. How does that work out?” He would know. His confession above says he was the one who mailed Sarah the paternity test. Someone else said, “Sarah could have done better.” Jess and Sarah drew one hand, Nat's, with a “maybe Sarah.”

Zia Bishara came with receipts: “Marcus was using code that he stole from me for my algorithm. Quinn and Cass worked on it with me. Ezra was helping too, and Ezra's office was where my code was stolen from.” At the party, even Ezra Sullivan hadn't known “who to believe.” Zia's memory of the code comparison is blunt: “Marcus didn't even bother changing your notes. Sloppy thief.” The theory pairing Zia with Skyler Iyer came off the list. The official story clears Marcus's victims, but it doesn't make any of them whole.

Flip's defense worked best. Flip was on my list for hiding their identity and burying a lot of information, and didn't argue:

"All I want is just some money. I don't care, like, who's dead or who's not."

Flip

Blake called it “the most honest answer I think that we have heard so far.” The record agrees about the money. At the party, Flip's bookie texted: “ok, be here with the 30K or come ready to sleep in an ice bath, I don't care.” Flip's IOU, signed Phil Kowalski, already owed Marcus “one undefined favor.”

Morgan's plea had the same shape. “Vic and Marcus hired me because they are doing a lot of shady stuff,” she said, but “I don't need to kill him. He's my client, he's paying me.” Add Sam's line about making the drugs, and Blake interpreted the responses from all three as essentially saying “we don't know anything about Marcus' crimes, we just work here.” Flip drew two hands, Morgan two and Sam ten. Morgan's claim that she was only the help deserves a closer look.

The Power Brokers in the Room

Start with the moment Vic walked into the party. Four days earlier, Marcus had told Sam, “I'm worried about what Vic knows.” When Vic arrived, Sam texted Mel Nilsson, “I'll catch up with you in a sec. I just saw Vic walk in”. Mel said she didn't realize he knew Vic, and Sam replied: “I don't really, but I have to check on something real quick.” I never heard anyone ask Sam this morning what he checked. As for Vic, his own memory has him and Morgan, the supposedly hired crisis manager, sharing a worry and a solution:

Vic and Morgan: something has to be done
A chat with MORGAN. You both have deep concerns about MARCUS. He's gotten sloppy, missed deadlines, made too many promises he can't back up. What’s more, there’s evidence he’s also been breaking the law in his race to beat his competitors to the finish line. He’s lied to your face too many times, and you’re NOT risking jail time for criminal culpability. Something has to be done. SKYLER might have a solution.
Vic Kingsley

Skyler's solution was a prototype. In the memory of Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods overhearing Skyler and Marcus, the visitor says: “Tonight is the test run. You have my prototype. You will program it with your memory recordings.” When Blake first gathered everyone, strong theories pointed at Skyler, and to some extent at Vic and Morgan. In the deliberation Skyler said, “I'm a businessman. So killing Marcus doesn't make me any profits,” and Skyler's name was erased.

Vic had hedged from the start. Taylor's January story in the Silicon Valley Business Journal announced Kingsley-Fund's “first two major seed investments: NeurAI and AI Bio Comp,” two competing companies. Vic's explanation: “I have my reasons.”

At the party, Remi Whitman texted him, “Have NeurAI IP theft proof. Oracle Ledger code. Shall we chat?”, and Vic nodded. Later, Alex Reeves showed him the specs. In Alex's memory: “Their $30 million dollar investment? It bought them a sham.” At 9:16 this morning I watched Alex rush across the room with Vic behind him.

When Blake suggested Vic's reputation as Marcus's investor might have taken a hit, Vic had an answer ready:

"Whoa, whoa. I don't remember much, but my solution is to pivot into supporting Remi's business."

Vic Kingsley

Remi said, “I didn't get money,” and that 80% had gone to Marcus. Vic drew five hands, second only to Sam, and left with his next bet already named.

At 9:06 I watched Remi and Vic, smiling and talking, walk up to Flip and hand over a memory. I don't know what it held. I do know what Flip said in the deliberation: “I kind of suspect that Vic knows Marcus is doing some illegal things.”

Then there is the money nobody voted on. Ashe told the room it appeared Marcus had cleared out Sarah's bank account. Mel, who has taken Sarah on as a divorce client, was already worried at the party: “He’s hiding money from Sarah.”

Riley Torres had mapped where Marcus hides money. At 11:43 PM he left Sarah this voicemail:

Riley's voicemail to Sarah
You call SARAH. You’re not sure where she disappeared off to. No answer, so you leave a voice-mail: “MARCUS worked with MORGAN to set up a slew of hidden investment accounts. We’re talking cover organizations, liability shields, blood money, environmental degradation, insider trading... I’ve mapped the shell vehicles. The paper trail is clear. When you’re ready, we blow this wide open. The money is yours. Love you! Also… I’m going to find Flip. Don’t judge me!”
Riley Torres

Earlier that night Riley had confronted Morgan directly: “They don't even try to deny it. You promise to keep their name out of it if they’ll help you track the money down for Sarah.” Yet by 12:40 AM Morgan was giving Taylor a story meant to take Marcus down, and it included “Hiding business income in shell companies, soliciting insider trading tips.” Morgan was turning on Marcus confessing to the schemes Riley was already starting to discover.

Follow the Money

While the room argued over what had gone public, $7,605,000 was paid out for memories that are now erased. While we don't actually know who paid. My suspicion is that NeurAI's board is somehow connected to the memory marketplace. The first sale, $125,000 at 08:09 AM, went into an account called Money30000. That account also took the $50,000 bonus for the first burial and, at 08:46 AM, two more sales of $30,000 and $375,000, for $580,000 in all. Flip's bookie wanted “the 30K,” so the name points at Flip. But, of course, anyone could have picked a name that points at Flip.

Now the minute that matters. At 9:15 I watched Morgan approach Blake to bury, along with Taylor, Skyler and a few others. At 09:15 AM, Riley's memory of confronting Morgan about the shell companies went up as an anonymous tip. In that same minute the ledger took four sales: two of $750,000 and one of $75,000 into an account called Bird, and $225,000 into one called Michael. The two $750,000 sales were the largest of the morning. The ledger names no seller, and Morgan did not walk up alone. Riley's voicemail about Morgan's hidden accounts went up at 09:27 AM.

Skyler and Taylor were in that group. At 9:21 I saw them wandering the room asking who the party architect was. The last memory to go up, at 09:28 AM, was Skyler's: “You remember Vic and Morgan’s proposal. This could be a good way to test the prototype.” Taylor's interview with Skyler at the party suggests why Skyler wanted that test. CyberLife claimed its robot prototype was ready, “But they need Marcus’s tech for the agent training paradigm.” Nothing ties Skyler or Taylor to a sale.

Bird finished at $2,025,000 from five sales, the largest account of the morning: $225,000 at 08:26 AM, $225,000 at 08:58 AM, then the three at 09:15 AM. The name tells me nothing about who chose it. What the record does give me is the order of events. Morgan went to Blake to bury in the same minute that Riley's account of her shell companies went public. Later, in the deliberation, she was the one who said, “I also haven't heard very much from Sam.” What was Morgan burying, and why did she point the room at the chemist?

Once the hands were counted, Blake told everyone to follow. Blake took the whole group out the door, and made sure Sam got away before the police came.

Blake manages operations at NeurAI. Sam appears to have made the drug Marcus used on his guests. The first memory to go up this morning, at 07:51 AM, was Marcus's own, and it opens: “The extractions worked.” Jess remembers Marcus calling it “an experimental NeurAI research he wasn’t ready to go public with,” and NeurAI's one-pager invites investors to “Join us to transform the landscape of Memory Capture.” So why would NeurAI's operations manager make sure the man who makes the memory drug was out of the police's reach?

Here is where I land. Sam carries the name. He helped blackmail Quinn and made the drug using Quinn's research. On February 18, before any of this, he also wrote: “I'm worried about M.” Now with Sam and Marcus out of the way, the power brokers keep their seats. Vic already has his next company, and whatever went to Blake at 9:15 is gone for good. Sarah is still owed her money, and Zia is still owed for her code. And the extractions worked. If anyone who was in that warehouse picks up this technology and keeps running with it, last night's experiment may just be the beginning of a brand new memory economy. NovaNews will continue to investigate this drug, because whatever happened in that warehouse last night, I think this story may be just beginning.
