The NeurAI Investigation

Nine People Ruled Marcus Blackwood Killed Himself. The $7.5 Million They Buried Says Otherwise.

This morning a room of nine settled on accidental overdose, a verdict that clears every motive in the record at once.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent || Ashe Motoko | Contributing Reporter · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-092026-26

Marcus Blackwood is dead. By the time the nine people sifting through his stolen memories this morning were finished, they had decided the man killed himself. An accidental overdose, self-administered, delivered by the very memory-extraction drug that turned his guests' private nights into currency.

It is a tidy verdict. The kind that needs no murderer and clears a room full of people who each had a reason to want him gone.

I was not in that room. Everything I know reached me from the nine who were, and from what I could deduce from the movement of money on the eight shell accounts that were opened during the investigation.

The Story

Start with the wreckage Marcus left inside his own house. Two apology cards surfaced in the recovered memories, near-identical: a rose and 'I fucked up. I'm sorry.' printed on each front. One opened 'J.' The other opened 'S.' Both promised 'She means nothing to me.' Both begged 'Please don't leave me.' Marcus wrote the same grovel twice and sent it to two different women.

S was Sarah Blackwood, the wife who had already thrown him out and hired Mel Nilsson to pry open the assets he had hidden behind shell companies. J was Jess Kane, who spent the party trying to catch two minutes alone with him and got walked past without a word. Jess was pregnant. A paternity test in the record put it at 99.85 percent, naming Marcus the father. The room kept invoking that number this morning and never once produced the page.

The private wreckage mirrored a path of destruction Marcus had wrought in the public sphere: The career of Alex Reeves. In a draft email to Remi Whitman two nights before the party, Alex laid out a grudge she had carried for years: she engineered everything of value at their old company, BizAI, while Marcus fought for the CEO title as 'the better evangelist,' then sold the company, paid her a pittance, and buried the profits so well the money was gone.

By 11:32 that night, according to Alex's own recovered memory, the grudge culminated in violence. Marcus bragged about the BizAI sale one time too many. Alex threw a punch. And it was Sarah, who pulled her off of her husband. And it appears, that this was not the first time Alex and Marcus had come to blows.

This morning, I am told, Alex carried the same grievance around the room, showing Vic Kingsley a memory on the scanner. Vic's answer, overheard by those there: 'You're just an intern.' Three words to erase a decade. But another memory reframes who should have been sweating in that corner.

But then ALEX points out what you’re not seeing. The demo’s fake. MARCUS’s “breakthrough AI” that he’s been crowing about for years needs manual fixes for 90% of the inputs. Put another way… it’s worthless. What does this mean for SKYLER’s prototype?
Vic Kingsley

Vic had committed twenty million dollars to NeurAI; Remi Whitman had put that number in writing to Marcus weeks before. What Vic actually bought, Remi carried over on a laptop and Alex spelled out: a demo that looped perfectly and did nothing, ninety percent manual fixes, worthless. When Kai Andersen put Vic on the board this morning, the reason he gave was reflex, not evidence. And yet, I cannot help but wonder what grain of truth triggered that reflex.

"Because he's the victimizer."

Kai Andersen

By then the investigation had stopped being about any single suspect. Blake, Marcus's Valet and NeurAI's head of operations, was reading account balances aloud, and money was moving. Whole nights of other people's memories were being sold into silence. No one in that room knew that trade more intimately than the reporter sharing this byline. Ashe Motoko once wrote an expose on Marcus. It never ran.

Taylor Chase killed Ashe's story and collected the NeurAI launch exclusive in return. So when Blake told the room this morning that they did not need the truth, Ashe had heard that arithmetic before.

"Remember, we don't need the truth. We just need a truth that we can make a good case for with the police."

Blake

The room split four to four, Alex against Vic, per those keeping count. Then Ashe offered the way out that required no one to be guilty: Marcus killed himself, an accidental overdose of his own memory drug. Blake's market logic carried it to six votes. And here is what curdles the verdict. The compound they blamed, Psychotrophin-3B, the powder that harvested the entire party's memories, was formulated by Sam Thorne. The man who spent the morning insisting he exposed everything.

Follow the Money

Eight accounts. Nine people. By my reading, that means, there was only one person in that room who had actually avoided the temptation of cash.

The largest silence sits behind an account named Jung kook: $1,725,000, six memories gone, a borrowed pop-star alias giving away nothing. Then there's Benito with $1,530,000 in their account. Deez charged the steepest price per secret, only four memories but $1,425,000, someone who knew what information truly carried value and what did not. Down at the floor, an account called Anonymous took thirty thousand for a single memory and stopped. The numbers make one wonder if the name now being floated as the next CEO of NeurAI had anything to do with one of these accounts. A deal, perhaps, to prove to the board that they were willing to protect the company's most precious secrets.

Here is the trick the verdict pulls. An accident needs no beneficiary. The instant Marcus became his own killer, all $7,495,000 detached from any motive. Alex's ruined decade, Vic's ruined bet, the buried divorce, the paternity that never surfaced: the money that pointed at each of them stopped being evidence and became, conveniently, just money.

The Players

Nine people, and their choices this morning shaped Marcus's death more than the drug did.

Those who brought the record into the light did the real work. Alex Reeves exposed memories that damned her own temper, the punch included. Sam Thorne exposed too, loudly, and made sure everyone in earshot knew it.

"I'm exposing everything I find, whereas someone is burying everything."

Sam Thorne to Alex Reeves

The honest one. Also the chemist who cooked the compound the verdict named as the murder weapon. Near the end, a quieter voice put the other reading on the table: that Sam exposed everything precisely so he could point the finger at everyone but himself.

Then there is Ashe. A journalist who once had his own reporting on Marcus buried for money, who this morning handed the room the theory that let all of them walk. I cannot deny his contributions to this piece. Yet, I somehow cannot shake the feeling that the temptation of money perhaps finally got to him.

And those who deflected. Accused mid-investigation because she carried Marcus's child, Jess Kane pointed the finger straight at Remi Whitman. Remi, whose quiet emails had seeded half the room's suspicions, pointed it right back at Sarah. Kai Andersen convicted Vic in three words. Vic reduced Alex to an intern. Mel Nilsson said little; she did not have to, she had already achieved her objective of ensuring Sarah walked away from her marriage with Marcus with something to show for all the years she poured into it.

What's Missing

Here is what the verdict did not answer. Not one exposed memory shows Marcus taking that fatal dose by accident. The room concluded how he died without a record of him dying.

The paternity test everyone invoked, no one could produce. No one can say who paid to keep Sarah's divorce papers dark, or whether the marital assets Marcus hid behind shell companies ever surfaced. Vic and Morgan Reed spoke of jail in a recovered memory; the record never says what they decided to do about it, or whether Vic acted before Marcus died or only after.

"Eight accounts. Nine people. Was the one honest soul who exposed everything they found actually Sam Thorne? "

Closing

The room settled that Marcus' death was self-inflicted. It is the one story that required no one to pay, and it did not disprove a single motive in the record. It dismissed them.

Alex may inherit the chair Marcus stole from her; the NeurAI board is already floating her name for the job. Vic's investments in NeurAI may still pay off if what happened last night was an 'accident.' The drug that killed its maker is still a recipe in Sam's hands, still a powder in labeled baggies. And a room that had already turned other people's nights into $7,495,000 chose the ending that cost them nothing.

That is the market's final trick in this town. It can buy silence. It can buy a happy ending for the ones who are able to afford the cost. Taylor Chase once buried Ashe's reporting with three words: reality is negotiable. This morning nine people proved it, and the invoice came to $7,495,000.
