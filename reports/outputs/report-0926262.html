NovaNews Investigation | The Blackwood Statement

Alex Reeves Pointed the Room at Jess Kane. It Named Him Instead.

Thirteen memories were sold late this morning into an account named JessKane. Someone wanted her name on top when the room looked. The vote went to Alex Reeves, five to four. Tonight an intercepted email shows NeurAI offering a retainer for his defense.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-0926262-26

With two minutes and forty seconds left before the police arrived, Alex Reeves asked Blake to pull up the scoreboard as his last piece of evidence that Jess Kane was after Marcus Blackwood's money. At the top sat the richest account in the room, more than $3 million under the name JessKane. Jess said she was obviously being framed. Before the window closed, the room named Alex, five votes to four. Blake gave him a running start and ducked everyone into the business next door before the police broke down the doors.

I suspect Jess had a point. Someone, perhaps more than one someone, wanted her name to be the last thing the room saw, sitting on top of an Evidence Board that had been filling all morning with Marcus's affair, the baby and his hidden money. Instead the room turned on the man making that case against Jess. Tonight police are investigating both Alex and Jess, and an intercepted email shows NeurAI offering the Patchwork Law Firm a substantial retainer to represent Alex. Alex was unavailable for comment; his assistant reports he has left the country for a vacation to recover from the trauma of last night's party. The room named him. Will that stop him getting what he wanted?

I was already reporting on Marcus when Fremont PD required me to monitor this morning's investigation as an uninterested third party, and I did it from outside the warehouse. What reached me were 21 memories, every one turned in anonymously, and a ledger recording $10,650,000 paid into seven accounts to make other memories disappear. My questions start there.

The Story: Eight Minutes

With eight minutes left, Blake read the board back to the room, and Mel put the first theory forward. The room wanted motive and enough passion to kill in the moment, she said, and Alex had both. He had fought Marcus earlier in the night. He had been excited to head a company he'd poured his time, money and energy into, and then, drunk and worked up in the middle of the night, he found out it was a fraud about to come crashing down. Alex asked whether anyone had actually seen him fight Marcus. Someone said Sarah had, and Blake pointed to her memory.

Sarah Blackwood's memory, 11:35PM, turned in anonymously
ALEX: I’m so sorry - you shouldn’t have to deal with us acting like this / SARAH: I somehow I recall breaking up a fight between you two the last time you were at one of his parties too / ALEX: You know why though. He doesn't know how to back down, so he ramps up instead. / SARAH: I do. We've both seen it / ALEX: Then why the hell are you still with him? He’s such an asshole! / SARAH: I know he is... but he’s my asshole. Now go home. It really was nice to see you, until you tried to break my husband’s nose. Again.
Sarah Blackwood

Alex explained it away. He and Marcus had known each other almost twenty years; they were like brothers, very passionate, very tough men, and Sarah had seen them play fight so often she knew it wasn't real. Things aren't real until a huge fraud is exposed, Mel answered. The receipt had been on the board since 09:46: in Vic's memory from 1:08AM, Remi arrives with a laptop and Alex "points out what you’re not seeing. The demo’s fake." Alex asked to put a memory live. Blake told him the network had been shut down, the company covering itself with the police five minutes out. None of Alex's own memories ever reached the board.

Then Sarah, the witness to the fight, told the room she didn't think it was Alex at all. It was the baby mama. Earlier that morning, Jess had shown Sarah the card Marcus gave her, and Sarah dug into her pockets and pulled out the same one. Her verdict: "What a jerk." Both covers carry the same rose over the same "I fucked up. I'm sorry." Inside, Sarah's promises "It's not going to happen again." Jess's promises "I'm going to leave her soon." Halfway through the morning, the two were seen in a corner, deep in hushed conversation.

Now Jess said Sarah was lying. Sarah told the room that when she learned about Jess, she told her she could have him, because she was ready to divorce, but the baby was another matter. Jess's own memory, on the board since 09:49, is the receipt.

Jess Kane's memory, 1:36AM, turned in anonymously
Bathroom. Both crying. SARAH knows. You know she knows. She says: “The baby's MARCUS’s?” You nod. Expect rage. Get: “You can have him. But that baby deserves better. We both do.” You're not enemies. You're both his victims.
Jess Kane

It is real grace between Marcus's wife and the mother of his child, and it backs Sarah's account more than it undoes it. Then came the money. Jess said Sarah wanted his money. Sarah turned it around: Jess had the baby, so if Marcus died the money would go to her as the baby's guardian. Someone noted that only held if he had no will. A story about a jilted lover was assembling in front of everyone.

Jess turned the motive on whoever was in trouble if the fraud came out: the investors and the people in leadership. Blake read it as Jess pointing at Vic. Asked about his due diligence, Vic said he wasn't going to kill Marcus. He was going to replace him. Maybe Marcus found out, someone countered. Then Marcus would have wanted to kill him, Vic answered, not the other way around, and someone floated self-defense. Someone raised Phil, too; Mel shut that theory down as boring, and the room agreed the evidence wasn't there. The replacement plan had been on the board since 09:40, in Vic's own memory from 9:55PM: "ALEX is in, MARCUS is out. No more C & D." At 10:00, another memory joined it.

The memory of Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods, 11:39PM, turned in anonymously
Can’t even get a peaceful smoke in. The dude who punched MARCUS is out here with that VC. They’re in a panic. Meeting outside, trying to be discreet, but the wind carries this sentence: “He’s out. You’re in. Trust me. It’s done. Please just come inside.” There’s some intense back and forth. You hear the name SARAH. Then they finally convince the punching man to head back inside.
Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods

Jamie's memory names neither man outside. I read "that VC" as Vic and the punching man as Alex, and the clock fits that reading: at 11:35PM Sarah sends Alex home for trying to break her husband's nose, and at 11:39PM someone is talking the punching man back inside. What "in" meant was already on paper. In January, Alex's lawyers at Patchwork had sent NeurAI's board a cease-and-desist demanding Marcus's removal as chief executive, the chief technology officer's chair for Alex, and a neutral interim chief picked by the board.

Back to two minutes and forty seconds. After Alex held up the account named JessKane, the preliminary count came back Alex 6, Jess 4, Vic 0. The name at the top of the money hadn't carried the room. With under a minute left, Alex made his case. He couldn't back it with memories, he said, but he had text messages showing he was never in it for the money; all he wanted was recognition, and he would have gotten it anyway. Sarah asked why he'd want to put something live if there was nothing to show. Someone added that he cared the most about the organization. The final count, with one fewer vote cast: Alex 5, Jess 4. That is the official story: Alex Reeves killed Marcus Blackwood. The case against Jess ended on the man who carried it in last.

The official story left one thread on the table. Blake's read-back put Ashe's firing from the Silicon Valley Business Journal on Marcus. Ashe's own memory, up since 09:33, says someone tipped Marcus off: "TAYLOR ruined your career, and you’re not going to stay silent about how you feel." Taylor's memory went up in the same minute: by 12:40AM Morgan had handed him "journalistic gold," and he was ready to stake his journalistic reputation "to get this incredible story out there." Taylor never came up in the read-back and drew no votes. His January email to Marcus tells how Ashe's story died.

Taylor Chase to Marcus Blackwood, email of January 31, 2027
Done. Ashe's expose on your previous company won't ever see daylight. My editor told them that it needs "more substantiation." They'll be reassigned to lifestyle by Monday. Now about that exclusive on the NeurAI launch… Can we schedule an interview for later this week? Taylor Chase "Reality is negotiable." - Tim Ferriss

Follow the Money: A Name at the Top

Look again at the line under Taylor's signature. In January a story was made to disappear, with a request for an exclusive in the same email. This morning seven accounts took $10,650,000 to make memories disappear, memories the official story can never cite, and I suspect NeurAI's board stands behind the deals Blake made around the room. Around 9:40, minutes after his memory and Ashe's went public, Taylor was seen approaching Sam with information she should see. Seamstress took $150,000 at 09:43, then $30,000 and $450,000 at 09:46. What Taylor showed Sam, and whether it was erased, the record doesn't say.

Then there is the name. JessKane took $750,000 at 10:05 and twelve more sales at 10:06: thirteen sales in two minutes, $3,235,000, the morning's richest account and the only one named for anyone in the room. Every sale was in by the time the scoreboard went up, and the room heard the account held more than double the next. It didn't. Banana, after its own late run, closed at $2,505,000. My suspicion is that someone wanted Jess's name on top when the room finally looked, the last line of a story about a jilted lover, a baby and Marcus's money. In January a story was made to vanish. This morning, I suspect, a name was made to appear. It didn't work as intended. Jess held at four votes in both rounds, and the man who held the account up as evidence is the one the room named.

Before anyone sold a memory under her name, Marcus had taken one from her. In Jess's memory, the third turned in this morning, she tells Nat how: "Said it was an acting gig - that you should play a valet." It seems Marcus had a thing for Valets. He called Blake, the NeurAI operations manager who spent this morning making deals around the room, his Valet.

And Alex? He told the room he was never in it for the money, only recognition. His lawyers' letter says the BizAI sale left him a "technical consultation fee" in lieu of his equity, and asks that "A. Reeves should be granted all the equity in the company that M. Blackwood currently holds." That proves nothing about last night, and no account bears his name. It does show what recognition came with on paper.

The Players: Where the Story About Jess Began

A name on the money only works if the room already has a story about the person it names, and that story began long before the scoreboard. Early in the morning, Remi went up to Sarah: "You've been having an affair." Sarah set her straight: "I haven't been having an affair. Marcus was having an affair." About twenty minutes in, Remi was spotted rushing to Vic with something hushed about Sarah. What she said, the record doesn't hold.

The board did the rest. Riley's drunk voicemail to Sarah, up at 09:35, promised "The money is yours." Sam's memory, up at 10:02, says she was the one who mailed Sarah the paternity test. Sam spent the first hour of the investigation working closely with Mel, whose theory would carry the vote. When Blake gathered the room with about 45 minutes left, Mel was named for a plan to blackmail Marcus on Sarah's behalf. Back in 2007, in Mel's own memory, Marcus took her in his arms and declared "You're our lawyer now!" Except this time, Mel had been retained by Sarah, to build a divorce case against Marcus.

Jess, meanwhile, was working a different story. At the bar she asked Kai, "Find anything interesting?" Kai showed her several baggies: "Oh, you know, just these drugs." Then Jess, when asked what she was up to, she said she'd been admiring the artwork. When Blake gathered the room, she said Marcus stole tech and she was still working out from whom; Kai had all the emails. Kai was seen combing the coat check early for unlocked bags, and at 09:57 his memory of Cass recognizing code Marcus had claimed as his own went up on the board. While Jess hunted a thief, the room was building their case against a jilted lover.

What's Missing

With minutes left, Vic, Sam, Mel, Alex, Sarah and Remi were seen huddled in hushed tones, and Jess's name was overheard. Two of the six, Sarah and Alex, then carried the case against Jess into the debate. Was that story one person's idea or a group's? What did Sarah and Jess say in their corner, and what changed before Sarah pointed the room at the baby mama? Marcus's own memory, the first turned in this morning, has him alive at 1:50AM, after the fake demo came to light at 1:08AM, and no memory on the board giving Alex an alibi in between. What was the memory Alex wanted to make public after the memory transaction network had already closed?

So is a verdict enough? I've been tipped off to internal communications suggesting NeurAI is preparing to install Sarah as interim CEO, with a CTO to follow within six months. Here is what Alex's lawyers demanded of NeurAI's board in January:

"Furthermore, we insist that M. Blackwood be removed as the Chief Executive Officer of NeurAI. A. Reeves is willing to assume the role of Chief Technology Officer immediately upon his ouster. The board of directors shall then select and instill a neutral party as the interim Chief Executive Officer."

The Patchwork Law Firm, for Alex Reeves, to NeurAI's board, January 28, 2027

If the tip is right, that is the chart his letter drew, going up with the CTO's chair still empty. Given everything I know about this case, it's hard to read NeurAI's retainer as anything but groundwork for Alex to join the company once the murder investigation closes. "He’s out. You’re in." turned on him this morning. It may come true yet. Who will NeurAI name as CTO, and who on its board signed off on paying for his defense? And Patchwork is Mel's firm, Sarah's divorce counsel and the name on Alex's own letter, while Mel built the theory that named him. Did those ties shape the morning?

To me the morning reeked of the deny, deflect, distract routine common in this circle, and I doubt the accusation will stick. If anything, it has brought Sarah a step closer to the corporate-shielded accounts where Marcus hid their money, and set her on the road to his company. Jess, a working actor whose memory Marcus once took under the pretext of an acting gig, remains a suspect. If someone tried to frame her, it failed this morning, but it may have laid groundwork: an easy story about a jilted lover using her pregnancy to claim Marcus's life insurance, ready for the lawyers to hand the police, never mind that Marcus's assets won't be easy to reach for any such claim. In a town where money is speech and memories are now a commodity, I suspect the outcome will turn on who has the funds to walk away clean.

Taylor Chase signed off his January email with a line he borrowed from Tim Ferriss: "Reality is negotiable." This morning, ten people negotiated one in which Alex Reeves killed Marcus Blackwood. But the negotiations are not over yet. His assistant says Alex is out of the country, on a vacation to recover from last night's party. I suspect he'll stay out while the case blows over, and that NeurAI's legal team will find it much easier to clean up any loose ends once this investigation falls out of the news cycle over the next few weeks. Meanwhile, NovaNews is continuing to investigate this matter.
