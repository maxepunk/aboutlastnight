The NeurAI Investigation

The Room Blamed Jess Kane. The Evidence Points Somewhere Else.

Four of six guests voted to hand Jess Kane to the police for Marcus Blackwood's murder. But this story is about much more than the vengeance of a jilted mistress.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-091826-26

She said it before anyone asked her to. "Pregnant." By the accounts reaching me this morning, that was the word Jess Kane volunteered into a room still reeking of last night's party, and it was the word that helped end her day with four of six guests voting to hand her to the police.

Marcus Blackwood is dead. Sometime after the rave he threw in a warehouse that doubled as his secret workshop, the CEO of NeurAI stopped breathing, and a drug built to pull memories out of living skulls had already turned the guest list into unreliable witnesses. Before the vote ever happened, roughly $13.55 million in memory had been sold into silence. They accused the woman carrying his child. I am not sure the record agrees.

The Woman They Chose

The morning ran on wreckage. Six guests woke with holes where the night before should have been, and one by one, documents surfaced to fill them. Almost all of them pointed at Jess.

The affair came out first. Marcus had written the same promise to two women. To his wife, Sarah, he swore he would leave his mistress. To his mistress, Jess, he swore he would leave his wife. One man, one signature, two identical lies. It could have brought together the two women who both loved Marcus, and who were both victims of his lies. Instead it kicked off the sequence of events that may now land the mother of his child in prison for his murder.

Moments after the affair was surfaced Sarah floated the idea that Jess had killed her husband. It played as grief hardening into clarity, a wife piecing together the worst in real time. Except Sarah had already thrown Marcus out and gone looking for him the night of the party, with something she needed to settle. She was not learning anything this morning. She was managing a crisis.

The room had its villain: the mistress carrying his child, driven by jealousy and the pain of rejection. What almost no one said out loud is that while they built the case against Jess, the record of the entire night was quietly being sold off. That is where this stops being a story about a marriage.

Follow the Money

Four shell accounts swallowed the record before anyone voted. $13.55 million, gone into names that mean nothing on their own.

Spectrum ran hottest, nearly $4.9 million, eighteen payments peaking in the final minutes, the rhythm of a company story being tidied up after the affair had already turned the room. George moved $4.23 million in step with the succession talk. Junior held $3.76 million in one dense burst, and when Blake read that figure out loud, Jess claimed the account without being asked. Money to secure her baby's future, she said.

Spacebunny held a tell. The smallest account, and the earliest, moving before the affair reveal turned anyone against Jess. Someone was already burying before there was a suspect to protect. Meanwhile, Remi was overheard early on in the investigation talking about a discovery about Ezra. Yet, no memory tokens belonging to Ezra ever made it onto the public record.

The Enemies in the Room

Start with the enemies, because Marcus had a room full of them. He made them the way other men make small talk. Alex Reeves walked into that party with a cease and desist in hand, a legal claim that the work behind NeurAI was hers, taken the day Marcus sold their old company out from under her.

Alex Reeves's Cease and Desist
The Patchwork Law firm has been retained by Alex Reeves in a suit against Marcus Blackwood and NeurAI regarding the theft of intellectual property in the formation and code base of NeurAI. ... We insist that NeurAI immediately cease and desist from using any of A. Reeves's IP. A. Reeves should be granted all the equity in the company that M. Blackwood currently holds. Furthermore, we insist that M. Blackwood be removed as the Chief Executive Officer of NeurAI.
Alex Reeves

The grudge was old, and it was documented. Before any of this, Alex had written the whole history out for Remi Whitman, in a message that named where the money went and why Alex would never trust Marcus again. Read it for what it is: one of Marcus's enemies reaching for another.

Alex's Message to Remi: "I Engineered Everything of Value"
Remi, if you are running up against Marcus then you deserve to know what happened back in 2023. Here's the truth: I was the one who engineered everything of value at BizAI. ... After demo day, Marcus struck a deal with one of the VCs without consulting me, sold the biz, paid me a pittance for a "technical consultation" and secreted away the rest of the profits. ... And I have vowed to never trust that man again. - Alex
Alex Reeves

Remi had her own account to settle. She had watched Vic Kingsley commit twenty million dollars to Marcus while her own profitable company got iced out. That twenty million now depended on NeurAI's public image staying clean after Marcus' death. By the accounts reaching me, Alex and Vic spent the early morning in tight negotiation, before the room had settled on anyone to blame.

Sarah's morning was a sequence of moves, not observations. She named Jess. She pulled Alex aside to talk company structure. And at one point, I'm told, she turned to Blake and asked whether a rival's research might be better off gone.

"Let's bury this. It's in the company's interest."

Sarah Blackwood

In the company's interest. The company whose founder was lying dead on the other side of the wall. Sarah also told the room she had no idea Marcus was cheating, not until this morning. A text message exchange between Riley Torres and Sarah disagrees.

"I'm Going to the Rave to Confront Him"
Sarah: Riley, you'll be so proud of me. I finally kicked Marcus out! Things are OVER between us. But I have to talk to him about something important, and he won't answer my calls. So I'm going to the rave tonight to confront him.
Sarah Blackwood

That message predates the party. Sarah had already thrown Marcus out and gone looking for him with something she needed to settle, something he would not answer for. It is not the account of a woman blindsided at breakfast. Ashe Motoko heard the denial and said what the room would not.

"You knew that."

Ashe Motoko

Ashe was the only working journalist in that room, and the only one who refused to sign the verdict at all. When the talk turned to hunting down more of the drug, Ashe asked the question the room walked past.

"Is this really a good time to be doing drugs?"

Ashe Motoko

Now hold the four of them together. Alex, Remi, Vic, Sarah. Each with a documented reason to want Marcus gone. From Vic and Remi, not a single memory reached the board, not one. From Alex and Sarah, only old paper and pre-party messages. The memories that could show where any of them stood when Marcus stopped breathing never made it to the record. The night itself, the part that would answer the only question that matters, is gone.

Which brings us to the one memory the room actually used to convict her. It is Jess's, and it did not surface on its own. Someone in that room put it on the board. It ties Jess to the drug and to Marcus's secret extraction protocol, which is exactly the thread you would pull if you wanted her to look guilty.

"He Tried It On You Once"
JESS.3 - 11:04PM - You approach your friend NAT and tell them you know why MARCUS is interested in Psychotrophin-3A. He uses it as part of his "Memory Extraction Protocol." Said it was an experimental NeurAI research he wasn't ready to go public with. You know about it because he tried it on you once. Said it was an acting gig - that you should play a valet. You wanted to help him, so you took the drugs and let him run his experiment. He was ELATED. Showed you a token. Said your memory was stored on it somehow.
Jess Kane

Read it twice. The room saw it as proof that Jess must have been involved in Marcus' murder. Read closely, it says the opposite. Marcus deceived her into taking the drug. He told her it was an acting gig, that she should play a valet, and he ran his experiment on her. She is the test subject in her own memory, not the architect. Whoever surfaced it handed the room a victim's memory and let them hear a confession.

The Theory the Room Dropped

Nothing places Jess at the moment Marcus stopped breathing. Motive without means, and even the motive runs backward. She said she wanted him alive to help raise their child.

And there is a whole theory the room walked past. Underneath the affair and the money runs the machine that made all of this possible. It has a name. Psychotrophin. The memories trace it back to the chemist. Quinn Sterling designed the compound. And Marcus, leaning on Quinn with something Quinn could not refuse, demanded a fresh, higher dose for the night of the party.

Marcus Leans on His Chemist
To: quinn.k.sterling@gmail.com From: marcus.blackwood@neurai.co Date: February 17, 2027 Subject: Re: the latest trial. I need you to bring me another batch of Psychotrophin-3A. The ingestion method you established with the last trial is effective, but the side effects I am looking for are totally inconsistent. I know you're resistant because you didn't test levels anywhere near this high during the PT-3B safety assessments, but I'm certain we should go up on the dosage. And you can't afford to say no, can you? I'm having a party on the 21st.
Marcus Blackwood

That is the man they are mourning. Someone who blackmailed his own supposed friend and colleague into a dose no one had tested, then turned it on a room full of guests. His own record shows how it went.

The Extractions Worked
MARCUS.4 - 1:50AM - The extractions worked. The instructions were clear: "Your memories are now in the tokens. Secure them. Combinations only you know. Protect them however you can." Simple. ... They wander off mid-command. SARAH's staring at a wall, tears streaming. This is chaos. You don't think ANYONE has the right combinations anymore. Drugged people make terrible assistants.
Marcus Blackwood

Here is the theory sitting in plain sight by breakfast. Marcus forced an untested dose of his own memory drug on everyone in that warehouse, and by his own account it left them unable to follow a sentence. He had blackmailed Quinn for the drug, who, by all accounts regretted letting Marcus have access to the drug. Someone who previously had tried to bury the research Marcus had somehow forced out of him. Someone, who had perhaps decided it was time to stop letting Marcus bully him. But pointing the finger at Quinn didn't serve the ambitions of anyone who was still in the room. So the theory was dropped in under a minute. Someone even had to ask who Quinn was.

So the questions pile up. Who surfaced Jess's memory, and what did they stand to gain from the case it built? Did Sarah know about the affair before the party, the way Ashe was sure she did? And the largest blank of all: $13.55 million of that night left the record before the vote, and whatever it said about who actually killed Marcus is gone for good.

What It Means

The room reached its verdict. Four votes to send Jess Kane to the police, one abstention from Ashe, who said there was not enough evidence to accuse anyone.

Look at who that verdict serves. It was not built from the strongest theory. It was built from the evidence that was curated by people who each had a reason to fear the truth becoming public knowledge. The theory the evidence actually supported, centered around the drug Marcus forced on the room, pointed at someone no one in the room benefited from eliminating.

Then there's the tip I just received while writing this article. Sarah is being lined up to run the company once the investigation blows over. The verdict she helped steer happens to erase the unborn child's rival claim to Marcus's estate. Follow that line, and a man with many enemies is dead, and the outcome was decided by people who each stood to gain from where the blame landed.

Twenty people had their memories emptied into strangers' accounts last night. By the time the vote came, $13.55 million worth of memories from the night was gone into anonymous accounts. They convicted Jess with the memory of Marcus using her love for him to experiment on her. Marcus built a machine that turned people's most private thoughts into ammunition against his enemies. But the machine did not need him anymore. With Marcus gone, the people in that room have taken control of his machine. And the person they voted to hand over to the cops is carrying his child whom they did not want to become the heir to his empire.
