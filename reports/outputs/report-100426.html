NovaNews Investigates: The Marcus Blackwood Case

“He’s out. You’re in.” Was Marcus Blackwood’s fall a handover?

The room gave police Vic Kingsley and Flip, and never named Vic's heir. Marcus Blackwood's crimes are going public. NeurAI's own emails now discuss Alex Reeves as his replacement, and an account called ResurgenceAI took the biggest share of the money paid for the morning's erasures.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent || Ashe Motoko | Guest Reporter · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-100426-26

With just under 45 minutes left, Blake gathered all 17 of them and asked me whether anyone had given me a theory yet. No one had. Vic Kingsley offered a plan instead: “I know that Marcus was doing dubious stuff, and I was going to try to get him replaced.” His pick, Vic said, had got into a violent confrontation with Marcus Blackwood. Who? Vic didn't remember, and the memory didn't say. Blake called it “the tale of an unknown punch.” Someone pointed out that “there are only a couple of people in this room who has Marcus's skill set.” Nobody said a name.

The first memory anyone turned in to me was Marcus's own, at 3:50 PM: “The extractions worked.” Vic's newspaper had promised NeurAI technology that would “map out and store an impressive amount of human memories,” and last night, on Marcus's guests, it did. The room gave the police two names, Vic and Flip, tied 8 to 8. It never asked who inherits what worked.

Here is my theory, and it is only a theory. Marcus's crimes began coming out last night, one memory at a time. A company carrying them needs a new face, ideally one who can make its AI work without stealing memories, and what did work might want a quieter home than NeurAI. What if replacing Marcus was the point? What if Alex Reeves is the fix, and an account called ResurgenceAI the exit?

What the Board Showed

Start with the experiment, in Marcus's own words:

Marcus's own memory: “The extractions worked.”
The extractions worked. The instructions were clear: "Your memories are now in the tokens. Secure them. Combinations only you know. Protect them however you can." Simple. Now you just need these drugged zombies to LOCK them. "SAM—take this token. Lock the—." They wander off mid-command. SARAH's staring at a wall, tears streaming. This is chaos. You don’t think ANYONE has the right combinations anymore. Drugged people make terrible assistants.
Marcus Blackwood

The room pieced together the drug behind it. Jess Kane told everyone, “Apparently, I told Nat that Marcus wiped our memories by giving us this drug.” Quinn Sterling: “I wanted to stop the distribution of this drug because I knew the side effects, but it seemed like people wanted to keep using them.” Sam Thorne: “It worked. Last night, everyone was so happy.” Blake: “I saw that memory of Marcus where Sarah was staring at a wall crying. Sounds super happy.” And Cass Zhang's memory has Marcus slipping unpaid-for code into Kai Andersen's installation, to run “tonight on an unsuspecting party audience.”

Nat Francisco's memory explains why Marcus needed any of it: “he’s lied about the AI that underpins his company. It doesn’t work. But instead of fixing it, he’s taking drugs and inventing techniques to steal people’s memories instead?” Broken code, patched with other people's minds. Someone in that warehouse says the code was hers.

Sam's memory has Marcus admitting that at BizAI “ALEX rewrote the whole thing and MARCUS somehow still took credit.” Alex's letter to NeurAI's board says the concept behind its mission “was originally proposed to M. Blackwood by A. Reeves in spring 2024,” then goes for the chair: “Furthermore, we insist that M. Blackwood be removed as the Chief Executive Officer of NeurAI. A. Reeves is willing to assume the role of Chief Technology Officer immediately upon his ouster.” It leaves the top job to “a neutral party.” But if I were cleaning up NeurAI, that's the résumé I'd want: the wronged partner, and the engineer who might make its AI work without anyone's memories.

When Blake gathered everyone, Alex aimed at Sam: “Sam has been doing a lot of shady, violent things. I just want to put that out there, because it's true.” Sam called her “very eager to lob accusations.” At 5:02 PM, someone turned in Sarah Blackwood's memory:

Sarah breaks up a fight
ALEX: I’m so sorry - you shouldn’t have to deal with us acting like this / SARAH: I somehow I recall breaking up a fight between you two the last time you were at one of his parties too / ALEX: You know why though. He doesn't know how to back down, so he ramps up instead. / SARAH: I do. We've both seen it / ALEX: Then why the hell are you still with him? He’s such an asshole! / SARAH: I know he is... but he’s my asshole. Now go home. It really was nice to see you, until you tried to break my husband’s nose. Again.
Sarah Blackwood

The violence Alex pinned on Sam, Sarah's memory pins on Alex. Again. Four minutes later by the party's clock, Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods stepped out for a smoke:

Jamie sees the puncher with the VC
Can’t even get a peaceful smoke in. The dude who punched MARCUS is out here with that VC. They’re in a panic. Meeting outside, trying to be discreet, but the wind carries this sentence: “He’s out. You’re in. Trust me. It’s done. Please just come inside.” There’s some intense back and forth. You hear the name SARAH. Then they finally convince the punching man to head back inside.
Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods

Jamie names neither of them; tying them to Alex and Vic is my inference. Remi Whitman's memory has the two “arguing. V’s face tight, A’s desperate.” By 4:22 PM the pair were working closely together. Read that way, “He’s out. You’re in” sounds like a succession settled at the party, with Marcus still alive.

Ashe Motoko had made me a deal: ten memories for his name on this article, fifteen for possible work together later. He delivered all 15. His exposé on Marcus's last company never ran; Taylor Chase had emailed Marcus, “Done. Ashe's expose on your previous company won't ever see daylight.” Ashe asked the toughest questions in that warehouse and earned the name above this story. His future is guaranteed. I promise.

Follow the Money

Before that delivery, Ashe and Vic were overheard at 4:54 PM haggling over a box: who keeps the memories if they collaborate on getting through that lock? The record doesn't say what it held or where its memories went.

Alex was dealing too. I overheard her: “Sam, I was so wrong about you, it was all Morgan's fault.” She held a memory that would implicate Morgan Reed, and offered someone its release for $15,000. Later Jess walked up to her huddled with Morgan: “Hey, what's going on in here?” Alex: “Oh, you're too heartbroken to find out what's on here.” The ledger shows a $15,000 transfer between players during the investigation, from J Pandaman 2 to Mai Ott. A transfer never says what it bought, and Mai Ott also took $1,000 from Squirtle. Someone in that room was clearly making deals.

Underneath the deals, the selling never stopped. After Marcus's memory went up at 3:50 PM, nothing else went public until 4:26 PM, while Bulbasaur, Vic1, Ash, JPandaman2, Squirtle, ResurgenceAI and TeamScience took sales from 3:54 PM. Vic1 may or may not be an account belonging to Vic Kingsley, but it does make one wonder. Every sale erased a memory for good, paid for by a buyer I suspect answers to NeurAI's board. If the memory marketplace that was running this morning was not a clean-up job, then I'm not sure what else it could have been.

The biggest share went where sellers pointed it: ResurgenceAI, eleven sales from 4:20 PM, ending with four at 5:09 PM worth $450,000, $450,000, $225,000 and $225,000. Two other accounts fed it during the investigation: Johnny sent $300,000, and Vic1, which took three sales of its own, sent $925,000. ResurgenceAI closed at $4,010,000 of the $9,280,000 paid out. The last sale, Tree's $225,000 at 5:15 PM, came a minute before the final burst.

Someone chose the name Resurgence AI, and it reads like a business plan. If the memory capture that worked last night had to carry on without Marcus, and out of the spotlight NeurAI drew, it would need a fresh name and funding of its own. By the time the room left, Resurgence AI had both. Who chose it, and whether it is more than a name on a ledger, I can't yet say.

On the List

In the final deliberation, Ashe went for the foundations:

"Should we talk about his two illegal enterprises? His pharmaceutical one and his robotic AI company, both of which are built on a house of cards that is tumbling as we speak?"

Ashe Motoko

With under five minutes left, Ashe called the demo behind the company's $30 million investment “purely a fake demo.” Skyler Iyer: “Oh, what? I guess I didn't know that, but the investors seem like they would be unhappy.” Ashe: “Whoever is investing $30 million into Marcus's company definitely has a reason to eliminate him.” Blake: “That would be Vic. Are we saying Vic?” Vic: “Yeah, but it won't be me, because I'm the one who does things by the book.” His own memory, turned in at 5:17 PM:

Vic learns the demo is fake
REMI approaches you and ALEX with a laptop in hand, looking like a smug son of a bitch. At first, what you see looks familiar. The demo loops perfectly. But then ALEX points out what you’re not seeing. The demo’s fake. MARCUS's “breakthrough AI” that he’s been crowing about for years needs manual fixes for 90% of the inputs. Put another way… it’s worthless. What does this mean for SKYLER’s prototype?
Vic Kingsley

The room read this memory as Vic's motive. Look at who else is in it: Alex, the engineer who sees what Marcus's AI can't do, showing the man with the money.

Then Ashe put Morgan's name forward, and Morgan turned on his partner. There's evidence he met Vic “at 8 for an emergency strategy meeting all about Marcus Blackwood,” where the two agreed that “the time has come for extreme measures". In the room he told Vic, “You tried to replace him. But you couldn't.” In the first vote, only one hand went up against Morgan: Alex's. Blake, noting Riley Torres had been trying to uncover Morgan's nonsense, told him, “Well, you know who your friends are not.” With 40 seconds left, Blake cleared him.

With three minutes left, Blake still had no solid name. Vic and Sarah seemed the leading contenders, and Flip wanted to flip a coin between them. Blake called a vote, Morgan went on the list, and Blake joked:

"Anyone but Flip, right? That name's not on there, so we're good."

Blake

When Blake asked who else to add, Taylor called out, “Flip!” At 5:11 PM Jamie had told him, “I know, I know you're Phil Kowalski. We've been over this.” Flip had told me he was trying to help Marcus and wanted to know what happened to him; Riley, who'd heard Marcus would be replaced, asked whether Flip could keep his job after. The record holds a motive the room never argued: a bookie demanding “the 30K,” an IOU for “one undefined favor.” Taylor's shout moved the room even further from Alex.

In the first vote Sarah drew 5, one of them Mel Nilsson's: Blake called out “Sarah's lawyer, Mel,” then “Oof, I'm sorry, Sarah,” and noted Sam jumping in. Vic drew 7, Flip 7. With 40 seconds left, Blake cleared Morgan and Sarah: Flip 8, Vic 8. “You know what? We're gonna give both their names to the cops, we gotta go,” Blake said. That is the official story. Four names went on the list. Alex's was never one of them.

Ashe had asked me who Marcus was being replaced with. I didn't know, and promised to let him know. I also tried to work with Taylor on this article. “I'm sure you can write your own articles,” she said. So I did, wish Ashe's assistance. Furthermore, at the time of writing this article I have uncovered a trail of emails originating from the NeurAI servers, discussing Alex as a replacement for Marcus. NeurAI and Alex both declined comment. Ashe, now you know.

None of this makes Alex a killer, and I'm not saying it does. Vic and Flip were both unavailable for comment. Vic's assistant has informed me that Vic has temporarily left the country for vacation to recover from the trauma of last night's events. Flip's whereabouts is unknown.

The room's conclusions read to me more like crisis management than an investigation. Was the addition of Flip to this morning's accusation a smoke screen to protect people like Vic? Put Alex in Marcus's chair, and NeurAI gets a clean face and an engineer who might make its AI work without stolen memories. Move what worked last night out from under NeurAI's name, and a technology built, as I see it, to commoditize our very own lived experience can keep growing where nobody is watching. Perhaps whoever was pulling the strings decided Marcus was no longer an asset but a liability, easily dealt with amid the chaos of his own experiment. If so, this morning was no reckoning. It was a handover. Who stands behind ResurgenceAI and the accounts that fed it? I mean to keep pursuing this case until I have more answers.
