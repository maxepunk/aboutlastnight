NovaNews Investigation: The Death of Marcus Blackwood

Experiment Gone Wrong That Got Dubbed an Accident

By ten votes, the fourteen people in the warehouse called Marcus Blackwood's death an overdose that left every hand in the room clean. The memories they put on the Evidence Board describe a test run of NeurAI's memory technology, with the guests as its subjects.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent || Taylor Chase | Contributing Reporter · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-100226-26

With under three minutes left before the police reached the door, Alex Reeves offered the room a way out: “Could it be that Marcus overdosed us? You know, he laced our drinks. So he's the only one that died.” Blake immediately called a quick vote. The result: ten people said Marcus Blackwood died of an overdose.

Amidst all the evidence that was made public, the room seemed most comfortable with an accusation which kept everyone’s hands clean. My questions start out here, with the evidence the room itself put up, which tells a more complicated story. The official story says Marcus died of an overdose last night. Was this just a tragic accident, or a much more intentional test of Marcus' technology at the cost of his own life, and his guests' memories?

The Story

The overdose rested on a confession. “Quinn, you have a confession,” Blake said.

"I was researching the missing drug Psychotrophin-3A."

Quinn Sterling

With Sam Thorne's help, Quinn had come to believe the machine that wiped their memories was “heavily linked to my development of that drug,” a drug whose side effects were “heavily, heavily related to neuroplasticity.”

The record backs him on the drug, and on who was taking it. Marcus's February 17 email complained to Quinn that “the side effects I am looking for are totally inconsistent,” then leaned on him:

"I'm certain we should go up on the dosage. And you can't afford to say no, can you?"

Marcus Blackwood

Sam's diary, the next day: “I think he's experimenting on himself.” Jess Kane's memory, turned in at 09:44, catches Sam voicing that same worry at the party and answers it flatly: “You know for a fact that he is.”

The email closes with an invitation to the party: “Come find me there.” That night, Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods was behind the bar when Marcus came through the service door with company. Jamie's memory, on the Evidence Board from 09:46, never names the stranger in the lab coat.

Behind the service door: a warning, an envelope, pills
Voices behind the service door. Then MARCUS enters with a stranger wearing a lab coat. STRANGER: “Ingesting extracts—untested. Dangerous.” MARCUS: “I can handle it.” Envelope exchanges. Stranger leaves. Then MARCUS pops some pills and stares vacantly at the bar for a while. Looks scared. Rich people and their top shelf habits. Not your problem.
Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods

Untested, dangerous, and “I can handle it.” The room's verdict says he couldn't.

In the closing minutes, Blake summed up the case: Marcus stole Quinn's research from Ezra Sullivan, used it to experiment on all of them, and possibly overdosed on the drug himself. “So we should be suing Marcus,” said Nat Francisco, and Blake called it a good point. When Blake then asked whether anyone was responsible, Alex, who had floated the overdose, drew five votes, “because we were saying that the overdose was the cause,” as someone put it.

But the Evidence Board held more than a drug habit. In the go-around, Blake relayed Riley Torres's claim that Marcus's company “didn't have any real worth or value.” Alex's own memory, surfaced at 10:09, agrees: “MARCUS’s code is worthless.” That night Remi Whitman had turned Sam's laptop toward Alex and Vic Kingsley so both could see the code. This morning she working with each of them again, at different times, to unlock memories.

A worthless AI and a warehouse full of guests is a problem with an ugly solution. At 09:56, someone turned in Jamie's memory of a stranger cornering Marcus at the bar:

“Tonight is the test run”
You’re busy restocking when a stranger walks in. They have the wrong vibe for a MARCUS party. "You missed our deadline." MARCUS goes pale. "Tonight is the test run. You have my prototype. You will program it with your memory recordings. Remember I can make it do anything I need." MARCUS doesn’t respond. You freeze. THANK GOD. They walk past you like you're furniture.
Jamie ‘Volt’ Woods

At 10:02, Cass Zhang's memory followed: the last-minute code Marcus insisted Kai Andersen build into her installation was Cass's Synesthesia Engine, and he “wants KAI to run it tonight on an unsuspecting party audience.” On the room's own evidence, the party was the test run, and the guests were its subjects.

Follow the Money

While the room argued over what had gone public, the guests' memories were going dark. By the close of sales, the buyer had paid $5,725,000 into ten accounts to erase them. I suspect that buyer is an interested party associated with NeurAI, but there's currently no evidence to connect the transactions conclusively with any specific stakeholder..

The two largest accounts were Rich, with $2,075,000 from eight sales, and one named Kai, with eleven sales and the $50,000 first-burial bonus at 09:07, for $1,480,000. By all accounts, Kai was nowhere near Blake at 9:07, so this account is likely a ruse to try and frame her. Meanwhile, Rich, at least, was honest about what it wanted.

Now set the ledger against the board. At 09:56, “Tonight is the test run” went up for everyone to read. At 09:58, five sales landed in Rich in the same minute: $450,000, $450,000, $375,000, $75,000 and $50,000. Two of them matched the highest price paid all morning, and memories sell that high when someone badly wants them gone. Whatever those memories were, given the timing, chances are whoever controlled the 'Rich' account also wanted the room to know about the test run to draw attention away from their sales.

The Players

Quinn's confession carried the official story. Quinn told the room that Ezra, his mentor, was the only person he had ever talked to about the drug's side effects, and that the break-in at Ezra's office took no money or property, only information. Ezra's police report bears out the break-in.

◆ Ezra Sullivan's police report, October 4, 2024: incubator files and a hard drive taken, a filing cabinet lock tampered with, no cash or equipment removed

Quinn's own memory, among the last to surface, at 10:20, names a different route to his formulations:

Quinn to Ezra: how Marcus and Sam played him
You begin by confessing your drug problem to EZRA, the kindest person on the planet. You explain that SAM was your plug. At first they don’t understand. They have so much faith in everyone, including MARCUS. You have to explain the situation three times before they really listen. You NEED them to understand how MARCUS and SAM played you to get their hands on your formulations for Psychotrophin-3A. You NEED to make things right and you don’t know if it’s too late.
Quinn Sterling

This morning, I'm told, Quinn and Sam were seen working together frequently, and the theory the room voted on was one Quinn built with Sam's help. Something does not add up with their story. Why the b&e at Ezra's when Quinn's own memory shows Sam was involved with getting Marcus's hands on Quinn's formulations?

Not every choice in that warehouse was self-serving. Mel Nilsson's memory, up at 10:06, has Riley handing her a case that could have made his career, secret bank accounts and shell companies included, “so that you can use it to protect SARAH.” This morning Mel carried it on: she was seen making a case for Sarah's innocence to Blake soon before the final deliberations.

Sarah Blackwood's grace is on the record too, in Jess's memory of the night. This morning, word from inside has it, Jess and Sarah avoided each other like the plague. The memory went up at 09:52 all the same:

Sarah to Jess: “That baby deserves better”
Bathroom. Both crying. SARAH knows. You know she knows. She says: “The baby's MARCUS’s?” You nod. Expect rage. Get: “You can have him. But that baby deserves better. We both do.” You're not enemies. You're both his victims.
Jess Kane

Then there is Taylor Chase, who, I'm told, worked the room, working with anyone to get all the information possible about what happened last night. Taylor shares this byline because of his contributions to our investigation. Yet the record also holds his January 31 email to Marcus: “Done. Ashe's expose on your previous company won't ever see daylight.” Source of an old grudge with Ashe Motoko who was also in the warehouse this morning.

What's Missing

An overdose explains how Marcus died. It doesn't explain whom the night was for. The first memory turned in to me this morning, at 08:52, was Marcus's own, from after the extractions:

Marcus, after the extractions: “The instructions were clear”
The extractions worked. The instructions were clear: "Your memories are now in the tokens. Secure them. Combinations only you know. Protect them however you can." Simple. Now you just need these drugged zombies to LOCK them. "SAM—take this token. Lock the—." They wander off mid-command. SARAH's staring at a wall, tears streaming. This is chaos. You don’t think ANYONE has the right combinations anymore. Drugged people make terrible assistants.
Marcus Blackwood

The instructions were clear. Marcus's memory quotes them but never says who wrote them, and Jamie's stranger had already told him, “You will program it with your memory recordings.” Somebody needed those extractions to work.

The record does show who was talking about a test. Vic Kingsley's memory of an early-evening chat with Morgan Reed ends: “SKYLER might have a solution.” Skyler Iyer's memory, surfaced at 10:13, has Marcus cornering Skyler about a technique that isn't public yet, and Skyler remembering “VIC and MORGAN’s proposal. This could be a good way to test the prototype.” The record never spells out the proposal, nor what the prototype was. We just know that it needed the guests' memories.

This morning, I'm told, Vic seemed comfortable with his role as power broker, holding court with many of the investigators, while Morgan worked quietly and deliberately, trying to remain under the radar. Three of Vic's memories surfaced in the same minute, at 10:18. At the end, Ashe said Morgan and Vic had been trying, “at least business-wise,” to remove Marcus, and when Blake asked who was responsible, Ashe named Vic. Yet when it came time to vote, one went for Vic. Under an accidental overdose, no one has to explain what Vic and Morgan proposed, or why Skyler heard it as a way to test the prototype.

As I write this tonight, I've intercepted multiple emails from NeurAI's servers. The company is preparing to name Marcus's replacement. Quinn's and Alex's names crossed my desk as potential successors, one as the future CEO and the other as CTO. No offers will be made until after the investigation into Marcus's death concludes.

Those are the two men whose words carried the verdict: Quinn's confession gave the room its drug, and Alex's question gave it its answer. Alex's lawyers had already written to NeurAI's board on January 28: “A. Reeves is willing to assume the role of Chief Technology Officer immediately upon his ouster.” And midway through the morning, Alex was spotted by Ashe Motoko whilst in a hushed conversation with Blake, who runs operations at the company now choosing its next officers.

I'm not claiming either man steered the room. I'm noting that an accidental overdose, if the police accept it, is the fastest way to conclude the investigation those offers are waiting on. It closes the case on the one man who cannot answer, and leaves the test run's backers outside it, along with whoever paid $5,725,000 this morning to make the guests' memories disappear.

This seemed more like the pilot run for a much bigger rollout of NeurAI’s brilliant new memory technology, and if the police go in the direction that the room pointed, the machine will continue chugging along to unleash this terrifying new technology upon the world. Does the pilot run become the rollout? NeurAI is waiting for this case to close. NovaNews will remain on the case until all loose ends have been resolved, and there are many, many loose ends left behind by this room's explanation of 'accidental overdose'.
