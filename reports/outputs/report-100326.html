NovaNews Investigation | The Death of Marcus Blackwood

Silicon Valley Spin Doctor Can't Spin Her Murder Allegations

Morgan Reed erased her name; the room named her over Vic Kingsley. Conscience, or power play? A tip says NeurAI's board eyes Alex Reeves for CEO.

Cassandra Nova | NovaNews | Senior Investigative Correspondent || Taylor Chase | Contributing Reporter · February 22, 2027 · Article NNA-100326-26

With just under ten minutes left, Blake asked the room for theories, and Morgan Reed deleted her own name off the whiteboard listing potential suspects.

It didn't stay off. Blake told Remi Whitman he seemed out for blood, and Remi said he thought it was Morgan. Sarah Blackwood and Riley Torres pointed at her too, and Blake put her name back. By the final vote, nine hands to Vic Kingsley's six, it was the name in the room's statement.

I monitored this morning's investigation from outside the warehouse, as the uninterested third party Fremont PD required, and what reached me read like a clash of titans. In a room full of power brokers who shape the public's understanding of the truth for a living, two of the biggest were dragged into the open: Morgan, the lobbyist who made Marcus Blackwood the political face of tech, and Vic, the venture capitalist who funded him. Was this the room growing a conscience? Or was the room using Marcus's death to reorganize who holds power in this circle?

The Case Against Morgan Reed

Morgan was right at home in the crisis. She was seen smiling as she worked the room for every scrap of information. When Blake first gathered everyone, the room even clarified that she had meant to expose Marcus, not kill him. Then at 10:09 her own memory reached me, anonymously, showing what exposing him looked like.

Morgan's ultimatum on the hammock
You’re both on the hammock. You show him proof: bribes, fraudulent reports, shell company records, stolen IP... You say: “Go public about the insider trading and take full responsibility or I give all of this damning evidence to Senator Walsh.” His face says he’s considering it—but there’s something else there. Instead of answering, he reaches into his bag and hands you a generous bribe. MARCUS: “Take care of it, will you? Just like you always do,” then leaves without giving you a chance to respond.
Morgan Reed

"Blackmail? I submitted that from Morgan's memory," someone said in the deliberation. The threat became half of the charge.

Sarah was hearing a different story. Early on, she told Blake: "Well, I was pretty sure Marcus was cheating on me. I just received some information that he might actually be trying to protect me." The record doesn't say who gave her that. Mel Nilsson, her divorce lawyer, spent much of the morning working through the locked materials to surface what she could, and supporting her.

The other half of the charge reached me at 10:17, thanks to Taylor Chase's investigative reporting.

Morgan gives up the money trail
When RILEY approaches, your mood lifts. They’re always a friendly face in the crowd. Except for this time. They come in hot, brandishing a sheaf of papers documenting all the shell companies you set up for MARCUS. They've done their homework. Your name is all over the paper trail. You've seen what happens when this kind of news gets out. You'll have to figure out some way to get ahead of it. But for now, you're beat. You tell RILEY how to trace the money, in exchange for keeping your name out of their accusations.
Morgan Reed

Riley had evidence that Morgan helped Marcus set up shell accounts. In this memory, Morgan trades Riley the trail to keep her own name clear. Between 10:15 and 10:17, Taylor sourced 14 memories, while the remaining 27 other exposures were submitted anonymously. One of the 14 was his own email to Morgan: "We both know attention = power." In a December email he wanted kept "out of my official SVBJ inbox," it had been "access = power." Another was Ashe Motoko's memory of landing a story: "ZIA has a scoop, and they’ve decided to give it to you."

Morgan turned Taylor's history with Ashe against him. Before Taylor could offer his theory in the deliberation, she told the room he had gotten Ashe fired for trying to expose Marcus.

"This is a journalist that has fired another journalist who had damning evidence on Marcus. So his intention needs to be questioned when he accuses people."

Morgan Reed

Someone added, "He's not an upstanding journalist. Your credibility matters." Someone else said Morgan was distracting them. Asked by Blake to answer, Taylor said, "Yes, I did get Ashe fired, but that's just like a job thing." Blake: "Okay, it's just a job thing. No big deal, Ashe." Taylor's January email to Marcus reads differently: "Done. Ashe's expose on your previous company won't ever see daylight."

Then Morgan told Taylor it was on record that she had told him what Marcus had done, and that he buried the story. The telling is on record. His memory of her leak reached me anonymously at 9:47: "Hiding business income in shell companies, soliciting insider trading tips, performing dangerous experiments on unwitting test subjects, lying under oath…" The burial is Morgan's charge, and a question I'd put to Taylor. For a few minutes, the journalist was the one on trial.

Along the way, the room dropped four other theories. At that first gathering, Morgan herself had offered an overdose, and people pointed at Sam Thorne as the supplier. Asked on the record by Taylor, Sam said Marcus asked him to make a drug for him, and he did not know where Marcus got the formulations. Of the drugs Taylor held, someone said, "It's not this one"; the record names Psychotrophin3B as the drug that took the guests' memories. In the deliberation, Blake asked, "Wait, was it Jess?" and got a yes. A paternity test names Jess Kane as the mother of Marcus's child: "Probability of Paternity: 99.85%." Then someone raised Sarah's emptied bank account. "Well, if he's dead, I can't get any money back," she said. Her memory, turned in anonymously at 9:38, says it plainly: "he’d already cleared out your shared bank account." The preliminary count gave four hands to Quinn Sterling and Sam behind an overdose, seven to Morgan, seven to Vic, and one to Jess, from Riley. Sarah received no votes.

Sarah's case against Morgan was personal. She said Morgan had been chasing Vic from the beginning, trying to get information, and had been telling her Marcus was trying to repair their relationship, when in fact he was not. During the count, Taylor offered, "Criminal but not murder." The final round went nine to six.

"I'm sorry, Morgan, but you have powerful friends in Washington. I'm sure we can clear this up, but not if the police take you in for questioning right now."

Blake

Then the police were at the door.

Follow the Money

Morgan's last stand turned on a question I've been asking all day: "I think it's easy to look at what's on the board, but what has been buried?" The ledger can't tell me what was buried. It can tell me when, and for how much.

The burying started early. At 9:20, $450,000 landed in an account named Ratchet, along with the $50,000 bonus for the morning's first burial. By my reckoning that was about half an hour in, around when Remi and Kai Andersen were reading black-light ink on the backs of documents. At the gathering, Blake, reported money moving: Tina had made $600,000, Ratchet $575,000, Bear $225,000. Blake told the room that motives were the best it had to go on. The memories that might have told it more were already going dark.

At 10:15 and 10:16, seven memories reached me under Taylor's name. In the same two minutes, six sales landed in accounts called Bo and White, ranging from $30,000 to $375,000. The ledger doesn't say who sold. What it does show is that while Taylor was plying his trade as a journalist, someone was making sure other memories never reached it.

The heaviest selling came last. Between 10:21 and 10:22, ten sales poured into a single account, Jesse, ranging from $50,000 to $450,000, the top price any memory fetched this morning. Jesse closed at $1,975,000, the largest total on the ledger. The last sale, $225,000 to Ratchet, landed at 10:23. In all, the buyer paid out $6,960,000. We don't know who the buyer was, but it is the opinion of this reporter that the NeurAI board of directors had to have been involved.

Minutes after that rush, Morgan aimed her question: "We have a scientist, we have a drug manufacturer. We don't have any data on them." She named Quinn and Sam, and said there were "no memories about the scientist or the drug manufacturer." Taylor asked Quinn, then everyone, "Why did you bury so much evidence?" Yet Quinn's memories had reached me at 9:47, 10:01 and 10:20, and Sam's at 10:16 and 10:19. Morgan asked the right question and pointed it at two men whose memories were in plain sight. Yet the morning's biggest account still has no face.

The Other Titan

Vic's trouble reached me anonymously at 9:46: "A chat with MORGAN. You both have deep concerns about MARCUS." Then: "you’re NOT risking jail time for criminal culpability. Something has to be done." At the gathering, Sarah suspected Vic, since he and Morgan had met to discuss a solution to Marcus's criminal liability problem. Blake said that did not look great for either of them. One memory had made both titans suspects. Afterward, Morgan went to Vic: "You are being suspected." Vic: "I know, I know. I just need to solve this problem." In the deliberation he said he had submitted all his evidence. "Maybe Vic was the mastermind behind replacing the CEO," someone offered. He drew seven hands, then six, and as Blake hustled everyone out, someone called, "Run, Vic, run."

His memories had been a fight all morning. Others helped him through the names game to open his bag, then asked him to share what was inside, and he was overheard shouting, "Get these guys away from me." "Hey, I hear you had one of my memory chips," he told Jess. "Oh yeah, I just made it public," she said. The record doesn't say which one. Other memories he handed to Alex Reeves, and Vic thought she kept burying them. Riley asked, "Why are you giving her your memories?" Vic: "I DON’T KNOW!"

This memory of Vic's reached me anonymously at 10:18. It shows what he had in mind for Alex:

Alex in, Marcus out
You steel yourself before you head to the bar for your meeting. This is not going to be a fun party. But you are going to rip off the band-aid, spill the beans, AND kill two birds with one stone. ALEX is in, MARCUS is out. No more C & D. No more potential criminal conspiracy charges. You’ve just got to do this, quiet like and ASAP, so that NeurAI remains a viable investment.
Vic Kingsley

Nothing on the ledger ties a single sale to Alex: there is no account in her name and no sale made in the open. Vic's belief she buried many memories may just be supported by an anonymous tip I received at the time of writing this article.

Alex barely registered in the room's case. At 9:43, she and Nat Francisco were talking and laughing about something. In the deliberation, she and Sam said they knew who Marcus had gotten pregnant. Blake counted five theories, and none named Alex.

She had already put what she wanted in writing. Nearly a month before the party, her lawyers wrote to NeurAI's board:

Alex's cease and desist to NeurAI
We insist that NeurAI immediately cease and desist from using any of A. Reeves's IP. A. Reeves should be granted all the equity in the company that M. Blackwood currently holds. Furthermore, we insist that M. Blackwood be removed as the Chief Executive Officer of NeurAI. A. Reeves is willing to assume the role of Chief Technology Officer immediately upon his ouster. The board of directors shall then select and instill a neutral party as the interim Chief Executive Officer.
Alex Reeves

An anonymous tip tells me NeurAI's board of directors is considering offering Alex Reeves the role of CEO to replace Marcus. Her own letter asked for less: the technology chief's job, under a neutral interim CEO. The question is, what motivated this potential job offer?

The titans are faring worse. Other outlets have already begun picking up the news of the corruption in Morgan and Marcus's relationship, and if the room's accusation turns into a conviction, the way Washington deals with Silicon Valley is in for a shake-up. NeurAI, meanwhile, already appears to be diversifying its funding to distance itself from Vic's investments. This morning, Vic handed Alex his memories. Tonight, the company he funded appears to be backing away from his money.

So was it conscience, or a power play? The room did drag two power brokers into the open, using memories people chose to expose rather than sell. But the vote brought down one titan, wounded the other, and never named the person whose lawyers had demanded Marcus's removal. The board I suspect paid $6,960,000 this morning to make memories disappear is now weighing who runs Marcus's company. I'm following that money.
