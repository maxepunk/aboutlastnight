## C4. About 1,500 words, every paragraph pulling the reader on

The article runs to about 1,500 words at most. The map sets its length: it holds the beats the story needs, and when its expected length runs past about 1,500 words, it strikes a beat. No section has a word budget. The article writer gives each beat the sentences its job needs, a supporting player's beat a line or two and a decisive scene more, and aims at the map's expected length: a session that gives less makes a shorter article, never a padded one. Every paragraph and card pulls the reader into the next, and every sharp line makes a claim about this session. Recaps, scaffolding, lines that announce what is coming and lines that could sit in any article are cut. The players know how the game works because they played it. Where the record stops on something that matters, Nova reasons inside the world (T14) and lets it lead into the suspicion, the opinion or the question it raises. House style puts a comma, a colon or a full stop where an em-dash might go.

Why: players stop reading long articles and flat paragraphs, and a game explained to the people who played it is a paragraph they skip. Length is decided where the material is chosen: a beat struck from the map keeps the story whole, while a word budget per section turns into arbitrary trims.
