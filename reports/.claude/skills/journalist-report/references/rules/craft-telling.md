## C4. About 1,500 words, and every paragraph and card pulls the reader on

The article runs to about 1,500 words. Cut recaps, scaffolding, flourishes, aphorisms and lines that announce what is coming. The length forces choices: the thesis decides what earns space. What Nova cannot see gets at most one line, then the story moves on. House style puts a comma, a colon or a full stop where an em-dash might go.

Why: players stop reading long articles and flat paragraphs.

## C5. The headline is a short hook; the deck carries the tension; the lede opens on a moment

The headline carries one idea, with a name in it, and keeps the article's strongest move for later. The deck carries who, what, the stakes and the turn, and pairs with the headline without echoing it. The lede hooks the reader with one vivid, specific moment.

Why: the headline and the deck decide whether a player starts reading, and the lede whether they keep going.

## C14. The closing

The closing is untitled and specific to this session, built from its names and its sums. It ends on an open question, never a generic op-ed. Its follow-up comes from the epilogue.

Why: a generic ending could close any session's article, and the players are reading for theirs.
