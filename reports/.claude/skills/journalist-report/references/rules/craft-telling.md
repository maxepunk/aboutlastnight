## C4. About 1,500 words, every sentence saying something

The article runs to about 1,500 words at most. The map sets its length: it holds the beats the story needs, and when its expected length runs past about 1,500 words, it strikes a beat. No section has a word budget. The article writer gives each beat the sentences its job needs, a supporting player's beat a line or two and a decisive scene more, and aims at the map's expected length: a session that gives less makes a shorter article, never a padded one. Every sentence says something the reader can follow on a first read: what happened, what it shows, or what Nova makes of it, in this session's names. A line that only sounds sharp is written as the plain claim or the question it stands for, and a short line stays when it carries a claim about this session. Every paragraph makes its point toward the article's argument and leaves the reader on it, or on the thread the next paragraph picks up, and each section hands the reader to the next. Recaps, scaffolding, lines that announce what is coming and lines that could sit in any article are cut. The players know how the game works because they played it. House style puts a comma, a colon or a full stop where an em-dash might go.

Why: players stop reading long articles and flat paragraphs, and a game explained to the people who played it is a paragraph they skip. A line the reader has to decode stops them, and a clever line that claims nothing reads as filler, or as written by a machine. A story told paragraph by paragraph toward one argument is one they finish. Length is decided where the material is chosen: a beat struck from the map keeps the story whole, while a word budget per section turns into arbitrary trims.
