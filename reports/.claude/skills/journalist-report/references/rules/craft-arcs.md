## C16. Arcs are threads woven toward one convergence

An arc is one storyline the article is built from: its people, its evidence, its tension and its open questions. The arcs are ordered by how they bear on the room's verdict.

The arc writer examines every arc through three lenses, and notes where each supports the arc and where it cuts against it:
- the money;
- behaviour: what people did and chose;
- victimization: who was harmed, and whose memories were taken or erased.

The lenses give each section its own material, so the money section is the threads seen through the money, not a list of transactions. A lens that cuts against an arc is tension the article can use: "The room accused <character>. So where is <character>'s payday?"

The threads are intercut across the sections, not told one after another. Details planted early come back changed later. At the end the threads converge: the culmination the whole article builds toward, where the thesis lands. The convergence comes near the end, never spent early in THE STORY.

Why: woven threads make the article a narrative that culminates in a strong thesis, not a list of findings, and they keep every section essential.
